How to Install a Shower Head

Overview

Installing a new shower head is one of the easiest plumbing upgrades you can make—no plumbing experience needed, and it takes about 15 minutes. Whether you want better water pressure, a handheld combo unit, or a rain-style head, the process is nearly identical.

Tools & Materials

  • New shower head (with rubber washer)
  • Adjustable wrench or channel-lock pliers
  • Teflon tape (plumber's tape)
  • Soft cloth or rag
  • Mineral spirits or vinegar (for cleaning threads)
  • Handheld shower bracket (if applicable)

Step-by-Step

  1. Remove the old shower head. Grip the shower arm securely with one hand and unscrew the old head counterclockwise with the other hand. If it is stuck, wrap the shower arm nut with a cloth and use pliers.
  2. Clean the shower arm threads. Remove old Teflon tape and mineral deposits from the threads using a cloth dampened with mineral spirits or vinegar. Dry the threads completely.
  3. Inspect the O-ring or washer. Check that the new shower head has a rubber washer seated in the swivel ball nut. This is what creates the seal—without it, the head will leak at the connection.
  4. Apply Teflon tape. Wrap the shower arm threads clockwise with 3—4 layers of Teflon tape. Pull the tape snug so it sinks into the threads. Do not wrap so thickly that the nut cannot engage the threads.
  5. Screw on the new shower head. Hand-tighten the nut onto the shower arm by turning clockwise. Once hand-tight, use the wrench for one additional quarter-turn—no more, or you risk cracking the plastic nut.
  6. Install a handheld bracket (if applicable). For combo units, screw the wall bracket into a stud or use toggle bolts. Mount the bracket at a comfortable height (typically 48—54 inches from the tub floor). Connect the hose to the diverter and handheld head.
  7. Test for leaks. Turn the water on slowly. Check the connection at the shower arm for drips. If it leaks, tighten another eighth-turn. If it still leaks, remove, re-tape, and reattach.

Tips

Tip 1: Never use a wrench on a shower head with a plastic swivel nut—hand-tighten only to prevent cracking.
Tip 2: If the old shower head is completely stuck, wrap the shower arm nut in a rag and use two wrenches—one to hold the arm steady, one to turn the head.
Tip 3: Remove the flow restrictor (the small plastic disk behind the rubber washer) if you want maximum water pressure—check local codes first.
Tip 4: Soak a clogged old shower head in vinegar overnight to dissolve mineral buildup rather than replacing it.
Tip 5: Use white Teflon tape rated for potable water—yellow tape is for gas lines and can degrade in water applications.
