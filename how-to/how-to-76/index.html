How to Replace a Dryer Belt

Overview

If your dryer runs but the drum doesn't turn, the drive belt has likely snapped. A replacement belt costs $10–$25 and the job takes about 45 minutes. This guide covers the most common design—where the belt wraps around the drum and a tension idler pulley.

Tools & Materials

  • Replacement dryer belt (model-specific)
  • Phillips and flat-head screwdrivers
  • Nut driver set (1/4" and 5/16" are common)
  • Putty knife
  • Work gloves
  • Vacuum cleaner
  • Smartphone (take photos before disconnecting wires)

Step-by-Step

  1. Unplug the dryer. Pull the dryer away from the wall. If it's a gas dryer, also shut off the gas supply valve.
  2. Remove the lint filter and screws. Pull out the lint filter. Remove the two screws inside the filter slot that hold the top panel.
  3. Release the top panel. Slide a putty knife under the front edge of the top panel near each corner to release the spring clips. Lift the top panel and prop it open or lean it against the wall.
  4. Remove the front panel. Disconnect the door switch wiring harness (take a photo first). Remove the screws securing the front panel to the side panels and lift the front panel off.
  5. Remove the old belt. Reach in and lift the drum to release tension. Slip the old belt off the drum and then unhook it from the motor pulley and idler pulley. Vacuum out any broken belt debris and accumulated lint inside the cabinet.
  6. Route the new belt onto the drum. Slip the new belt around the drum with the grooved side facing the drum surface. Position it in the belt groove—a worn track on the drum shows exactly where it sits.
  7. Route the belt around the motor and idler. With the drum lifted slightly, thread the belt under the idler pulley wheel and then around the motor drive pulley. The idler arm applies tension. Consult your photos or a belt routing diagram for your model.
  8. Verify tension. With everything routed, rotate the drum by hand. The belt should grip and the drum should spin freely without slipping. The idler pulley should move with the belt.
  9. Reassemble the dryer. Reattach the front panel, reconnect the door switch wires, close the top panel and snap it down, and reinstall the lint filter screws.
  10. Plug in and test. Plug the dryer back in. Run a short cycle to confirm the drum spins smoothly and the belt doesn't squeak or slip.

Pro Tips

Tip 1: While the dryer is open, vacuum out all lint from inside the cabinet. Lint buildup near the motor and heating element is a major fire risk.
Tip 2: Take photos at every step—especially of wire connections, belt routing, and screw locations. You'll thank yourself during reassembly.
Tip 3: Order your replacement belt by the dryer model number (not the brand alone). Belts vary even within the same brand.
Tip 4: If the drum support rollers or glides are worn (visible flat spots or squeaking), replace those while the machine is disassembled. A $20 roller kit now saves disassembling again in 6 months.
Tip 5: Use a magnetic parts tray for all screws. Losing a screw inside the dryer cabinet causes rattling and potential damage.
