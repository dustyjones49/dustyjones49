How to Remove Hard Water Stains

Overview

Hard water stains are calcium and magnesium mineral deposits left behind when water evaporates. They appear as white, chalky film on glass shower doors, faucets, and tile. Mild acids dissolve these minerals without damaging surfaces when used correctly.

Tools & Materials

  • White vinegar (undiluted for heavy deposits)
  • Lemon juice
  • Commercial lime scale remover (CLR, Lime-A-Way)
  • Baking soda
  • Spray bottle
  • Microfiber cloths and sponges
  • Plastic scraper (for glass)
  • Old toothbrush
  • Rubber gloves
  • #0000 super-fine steel wool (for glass only, NOT coated fixtures)

Step-by-Step

  1. Identify the surface type. Chrome, stainless steel, and ceramic tile can handle mild acids. Natural stone (marble, granite, travertine) cannot—acid etches and damages them. For stone, use only pH-neutral stone cleaners.
  2. Soak with vinegar. For glass shower doors and chrome faucets, soak paper towels in undiluted white vinegar. Press them against the stained surface and leave for 30–60 minutes. The vinegar dissolves calcium bonds.
  3. For tough buildup on glass. After vinegar soaking, sprinkle baking soda on a damp sponge and scrub the glass. The mild abrasive lifts loosened mineral deposits. Use #0000 steel wool (dry) on stubborn spots—it won't scratch glass but will remove caked-on minerals.
  4. For faucets and showerheads. Fill a plastic bag with white vinegar, secure it around the faucet head or showerhead with a rubber band, and leave it overnight. Remove the bag and scrub with an old toothbrush. Run water to flush loosened deposits.
  5. For tile and grout. Spray vinegar directly on affected areas. Let sit 15 minutes, then scrub with a nylon brush. Rinse thoroughly. For corners and crevices, apply a baking soda paste with an old toothbrush, then spray vinegar and scrub.
  6. Use CLR for extreme buildup. If vinegar doesn't cut through years of buildup, apply a commercial lime scale remover. Follow the product instructions exactly. Most need 2–5 minutes of contact time. Wear gloves and ventilate—these products are stronger acids.
  7. Rinse thoroughly. Rinse all surfaces with clean water multiple times. Acid left on metal fixtures causes pitting and corrosion.
  8. Dry and protect. Dry surfaces with a microfiber cloth to prevent new water spots. Apply car wax or a glass protectant to shower doors to bead water and slow stain formation.

Pro Tips

Tip 1: Never use steel wool on chrome, nickel, or coated faucets. It will scratch the finish instantly. Reserve #0000 steel wool for bare glass only.
Tip 2: Squeegee shower doors after every use and wipe faucets dry. Prevention takes 30 seconds; cleaning takes 30 minutes.
Tip 3: Install a whole-house water softener if hard water stains are a constant battle. It's the only permanent solution and also extends the life of water heaters and appliances.
Tip 4: For toilet hard water rings, turn off the water, flush to empty the bowl, and fill a spray bottle with vinegar. Spray until the ring is submerged. Soak overnight, scrub, and flush.
