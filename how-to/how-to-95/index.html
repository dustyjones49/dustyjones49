How to Adjust Sprinkler Heads

Overview

Improperly adjusted sprinklers waste water by spraying sidewalks, driveways, and fences while leaving lawn edges dry. A well-adjusted system applies water evenly, reaches the edges of the zone, and uses the minimum water needed. You'll need the adjustment tool that came with your sprinkler system.

Tools & Materials

  • Sprinkler adjustment key (often included with sprinkler purchase; universal keys available)
  • Flat-head screwdriver (small and large)
  • Adjustable pliers (for loosening stuck adjustment screws)
  • Marking flags or stakes
  • Rain gauge or tuna cans (for measuring output)

Step-by-Step

  1. Identify the head type. There are three main types: spray heads (fixed fan pattern, pop-up), rotor heads (rotating stream, larger radius), and impact heads (older, clicking rotation). Each adjusts differently.
  2. Mark the edges of the zone. Place flags at the corners of lawn areas, the edges of hardscape (walks, driveways), and any obstacles (trees, beds). This gives you a visual reference for where spray should and shouldn't reach.
  3. Adjust spray head arc (fixed spray). For heads with adjustable arcs, insert the adjustment key into the center screw on top and turn. Turning clockwise decreases the arc; counterclockwise increases it. On some models, twist the entire nozzle collar.
  4. Adjust spray head radius (distance). Turn the center screw on top with a small flat-head screwdriver. Turn clockwise to reduce spray distance (reduces flow), counterclockwise to increase spray distance (increases flow). Don't close this screw completely—it will shut off the head.
  5. Adjust rotor head arc. Find the arc adjustment socket on top of the rotor. Insert the key and turn toward the "+" to increase arc or "-" to decrease arc. The rotor must be set to the fixed left stop position before adjusting.
  6. Set the rotor head left stop (fixed edge). Rotate the turret fully to the left stop. If the left edge is spraying the wrong direction, unscrew the body from the riser and re-orient the entire head, then tighten.
  7. Adjust rotor head radius. Insert the key in the radius adjustment socket. Turn toward the arrows marked on top. Reducing the radius decreases water output; increasing it extends the stream. Adjust gradually and observe.
  8. Adjust impact head deflector. Move the deflector shield up or down—up for a shorter, wider spray, down for a longer, narrower stream. Adjust the trip pins to set the rotation arc.
  9. Check head-to-head coverage. The spray from each head should reach the base of the next head in the zone. Run the zone and walk the coverage. Move any heads that leave dry gaps or spray onto hardscape. Raise sunken heads above soil level.
  10. Do a catch-can test. Place several identical containers (tuna cans work) evenly across the zone. Run for 15 minutes. Measure the water depth in each can. If depths vary more than 20%, adjust individual head radii or replace nozzles to even out distribution.

Pro Tips

Tip 1: Adjust sprinklers in the early morning when water pressure is highest and wind is lowest. Afternoon breezes distort spray patterns and make adjustments inaccurate.
Tip 2: Don't mix spray heads and rotor heads on the same zone. Spray heads apply water at 3–4 times the rate of rotors. Running them together grossly over-waters the spray head area.
Tip 3: If a head won't hold its adjustment, the internal mechanism is worn. Replace the head or the internal assembly rather than repeatedly adjusting it.
Tip 4: Convert high-pressure, misting spray heads to pressure-compensating nozzles (like MP Rotators). They apply water more slowly and evenly, matching soil absorption rates and reducing runoff.
