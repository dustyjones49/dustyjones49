How to Clean Dryer Lint Trap and Vent

Overview

Lint buildup is the leading cause of dryer fires. Cleaning the lint trap after every load is essential, but a deep clean of the trap housing, flexible duct, and exterior vent should be done every 6–12 months to keep your dryer safe and efficient.

Tools & Materials

  • Dryer vent cleaning brush kit (flexible rods)
  • Power drill
  • Vacuum with crevice tool and brush attachment
  • Flashlight
  • Screwdriver (to remove exterior vent cover)
  • Dish soap and warm water (for lint screen)
  • Old rag
  • Dust mask

Step-by-Step

  1. Unplug the dryer. Pull the dryer away from the wall to access the duct and vent connection.
  2. Deep clean the lint screen. Remove the lint trap screen. Scrub it with warm, soapy water and a nylon brush to dissolve invisible residue from dryer sheets and fabric softener. Rinse and dry thoroughly.
  3. Clean the lint trap cavity. Use the vacuum crevice tool to reach deep into the lint trap slot. Then attach a dryer vent brush to flexible rods and spin it slowly with a drill to pull out compacted lint from the trap housing.
  4. Disconnect the duct. Loosen the clamps at both the dryer outlet and the wall connection. Remove the flexible or rigid duct section.
  5. Clean the duct. Run the brush through the duct from end to end. Shake out loose debris. Vacuum the interior. If the duct is crushed, torn, or has multiple layers of foil tape, replace it with a new rigid metal duct.
  6. Clean the wall duct. Insert the brush rod into the wall duct opening. Spin with a drill while slowly feeding in additional rod sections. Pull the brush out and clean it off several times to remove the lint load gradually.
  7. Clean the exterior vent hood. Go outside and remove the vent cover (usually held by screws or snaps). Pull out any lint mat from the exterior end. Vacuum around the opening.
  8. Check the flapper valve. Clean the flapper so it opens and closes freely. Replace it if it's stuck open or warped—a stuck-open flapper lets cold air, pests, and moisture into the duct.
  9. Reassemble and test. Reconnect the duct with clamps, slide the dryer back, plug it in, and run on air-fluff (no heat) for 5 minutes. Go outside and confirm strong airflow from the vent.

Pro Tips

Tip 1: If your lint screen gets residue-coated quickly, you may be using too much fabric softener or dryer sheets. Try wool dryer balls instead—they reduce static without leaving residue.
Tip 2: The water test for lint screens: run water through the screen. If water beads up instead of flowing through, residue is blocking airflow. Scrub until water passes through freely.
Tip 3: Replace foil or plastic flex ducts with 4-inch rigid metal duct. Smooth metal walls resist lint accumulation and are fire-safe.
Tip 4: Never leave the dryer running when you're out of the house or asleep. If a lint fire starts, you need to be there to respond.
