How to Fix a Squeaky Floor

Overview

Floor squeaks are caused by wood rubbing against wood or against nails — usually subfloor movement against joists, loose nails, or hardwood boards rubbing against each other. Fixing from above (through the finished floor) is fastest; fixing from below (exposed joists in a basement or crawlspace) gives the most durable results. A creak means lateral movement; a squeak means vertical movement.

Tools & Materials

  • Squeak-No-More kit or breakaway screws (for carpeted floors)
  • Finish screws and trim-head screws (for hardwood floors)
  • Talcum powder or graphite powder (for hardwood board-to-board squeaks)
  • Drill with countersink bit, stud finder, hammer, nail set
  • Wood shims, construction adhesive (for below-floor access)

Step-by-Step

  1. Locate the exact squeak: Have someone walk or bounce on the squeaky spot while you mark it from above. Use painter's tape to mark the floor where the squeak is loudest. Squeaks often originate at the subfloor-joist connection and transmit along the floorboard, so locate where pressure from a single foot creates the noise.
  2. Carpet method — breakaway screws: Use a specialized squeak-repair kit (like Squeak-No-More) with scored breakaway screws. Locate the joist with a stud finder through the carpet. Drive the special screw through the carpet into the joist, then snap the screw head off below the carpet surface. The threads pull the subfloor tight to the joist. One screw every 6—8" along the joist typically eliminates all movement.
  3. Hardwood method — trim screws and plugs: Drill a pilot hole and drive a trim-head screw through the hardwood into the joist below until just below the surface. Use a wood plug cutter and matching wood plug to fill the hole. For finished hardwood, colored wood filler that matches the floor stain works for smaller screw holes.
  4. Between-board squeaks (hardwood): For squeaks where two hardwood boards rub together (not subfloor-related), sprinkle talcum powder or graphite powder along the seam and work it in with a stiff brush. The powder lubricates the joint, stopping the friction squeak. This is a temporary fix — seasonal humidity changes may require reapplication.
  5. Below-floor access method (most durable): From below (basement or crawlspace), have an helper walk above while you locate the squeaky subfloor-joist joint. Tap thin cedar shims coated with construction adhesive between the joist and subfloor until snug — do not force them so hard that you lift the subfloor. Additionally, screw through the subfloor into the joist from below with 1-1/4" screws. For bridging between joists, add blocking between joists and screw through the subfloor.
  6. Loose nail fix: If you can see the offending nail, set it deeper with a nail set and hammer. Drive a new screw within 2" of the nail into the joist. Do not simply drive the nail deeper — it will loosen again because the holding power is lost in the original hole.

Tips & Warnings

  • Used correctly, breakaway screws through carpet work remarkably well and leave no visible trace. The carpet yarns close over the screw hole after the head is snapped off.
  • Never use standard drywall screws to secure a subfloor — they have little shear strength and will snap. Use coated deck screws or structural wood screws.
  • If floors squeak over a large area, the subfloor was likely nailed instead of screwed during construction. This is common in homes built before the 1980s. Consider screwing down the entire floor from below when renovating.
  • Shimming from below should be tight but not force the subfloor upward — this can crack tile or create humps in hardwood above.
