How to Insulate a Water Heater

Overview

Wrapping an older water heater with an insulation blanket can reduce standby heat loss by 25–45%, saving $20–$45 per year on water heating costs. If your tank feels warm to the touch, it's losing heat and will benefit from a blanket. Most modern tanks (post-2015) are already well-insulated and may not need one.

Tools & Materials

  • Water heater insulation blanket kit
  • Utility knife or scissors
  • Aluminum foil tape (included in most kits)
  • Measuring tape
  • Permanent marker
  • Work gloves
  • Dust mask

Step-by-Step

  1. Check if insulation is needed. Touch the tank if it's warm. Check the tank's R-value label. If it's R-16 or lower, insulation will help. If it's R-24 or higher, it's probably already efficient enough.
  2. Measure the tank. Measure the height and circumference of the tank. Purchase a blanket kit that fits your tank size.
  3. Clean the tank exterior. Wipe down the tank with a damp cloth to remove dust. Tape won't stick to a dirty surface.
  4. Cut openings for controls. Lay the blanket out and mark cutouts for the thermostat access panel, drain valve, and pressure relief valve pipe. On gas units, also cut for the burner access, gas valve, and pilot light.
  5. Wrap the blanket around the tank. Position the blanket around the tank with the cutouts aligned over the access points. The blanket's plastic side faces out; insulation faces the tank.
  6. Tape the seams. Seal all vertical seams and the top edge with foil tape. Press firmly for a strong bond.
  7. Insulate the pipes. While you're at it, slide foam pipe insulation sleeves onto at least the first 6 feet of hot and cold water pipes leaving the tank.
  8. Do NOT cover the top. Never place insulation on top of the tank. On a gas water heater, this blocks the flue and can cause a fire or carbon monoxide buildup. On electric, it covers the wiring access.
  9. Do NOT cover the thermostat. The thermostat access panel must remain uncovered for operation and service.

Pro Tips

Tip 1: For gas water heaters, keep the blanket at least 2 inches away from the flue pipe and never cover the burner access door or the top vent opening.
Tip 2: If you're installing a new water heater, just buy an energy-efficient model with high R-value insulation—the payback is better than a blanket.
Tip 3: Set the water heater thermostat to 120°F after insulating. The reduced standby losses may now allow a slightly lower temperature setting.
Tip 4: An insulation blanket costs $20–$40 and pays for itself in about one year of reduced energy usage.
