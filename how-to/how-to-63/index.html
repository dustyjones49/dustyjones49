How to Install a Bathroom Exhaust Fan

Overview

A properly installed bath fan removes excess humidity that causes mold, peeling paint, and rot. This guide covers replacing an old fan or installing a new one where none existed, including venting to the outside through the roof or soffit.

Tools & Materials

  • Bathroom exhaust fan (size to your bathroom square footage—1 CFM per sq ft minimum)
  • Insulated flexible duct (4" or 6")
  • Roof or wall vent cap
  • Non-contact voltage tester
  • Wire strippers and wire nuts
  • Drywall saw
  • Power drill with hole saw (for exterior vent)
  • Aluminum foil tape (not duct tape)
  • Screwdriver set
  • Drywall screws
  • Caulk
  • Safety glasses and dust mask

Step-by-Step

  1. Turn off power at the breaker. Verify the circuit is dead with a non-contact voltage tester before touching any wiring.
  2. Remove the old fan. Take down the grille, disconnect wiring, and unscrew the housing from the joists. Disconnect the duct from the old housing.
  3. Cut ceiling opening (new install). Trace the fan housing template on the ceiling between two joists. Cut the drywall with a drywall saw. Avoid ceiling joists.
  4. Install the mounting brackets. Slip the telescoping mounting bars or adjustable brackets between the joists and screw them in place. The fan housing will hang from these.
  5. Mount the fan housing. Position the housing on the brackets so it sits flush with the finished ceiling. Screw the housing tabs to the brackets. Adjust until level.
  6. Run the ductwork. Connect 4" or 6" insulated flex duct to the fan housing outlet. Secure with foil tape. Run the duct to the exterior vent location using the shortest, straightest path possible—no sharp bends.
  7. Install the exterior vent cap. Cut a hole in the roof or soffit with a hole saw. Attach the vent cap from the outside, sealing the flange with roofing cement or caulk. Connect the duct to it and tape.
  8. Wire the fan. Connect the fan's black wire to the switch leg (hot), white to neutral, and green or bare to the ground. If the fan has a built-in light or nightlight, follow the wiring diagram for each circuit.
  9. Attach the blower assembly. Plug the blower motor into the receptacle inside the housing. Snap the grille into place.
  10. Seal around the housing. From above (attic), caulk or foam-seal any gaps between the housing and the drywall to stop conditioned air from leaking into the attic.

Pro Tips

Tip 1: Always vent bath fans to the outdoors—never into an attic or soffit cavity. That just dumps moisture where it causes rot and mold.
Tip 2: Use rigid metal ducting instead of flex duct when possible. It has less air resistance and lets the fan move more air.
Tip 3: Insulate the duct with at least R-6 insulation if it runs through an unconditioned attic. This prevents condensation inside the duct in winter.
Tip 4: Size the fan correctly: 1 CFM per square foot of bathroom space. A 50 sq ft bathroom needs at least a 50 CFM fan. For rooms over 100 sq ft, add 50 CFM for each toilet, shower, and tub.
