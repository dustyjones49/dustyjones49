How to Install a Dimmer Switch

Overview

A dimmer switch replaces a standard single-pole switch to give you adjustable light levels, saving energy and creating ambiance. Installation takes about 20 minutes. The critical consideration is bulb compatibility: incandescent dimmers do not work with most LED bulbs. Buy an LED-compatible dimmer and dimmable LED bulbs together—mismatched components cause flickering, buzzing, or failure to dim.

Tools & Materials

  • LED-compatible dimmer switch (match wattage rating)
  • Non-contact voltage tester
  • Flathead & Phillips screwdrivers
  • Wire strippers & needle-nose pliers
  • Electrical tape
  • Wire nuts (if not included)
  • Cover plate (screwless decor style recommended)
  • Dimmable LED bulbs (check packaging for dimmable marking)

Step-by-Step

  1. Turn off power and verify. Switch off the breaker. Use a non-contact voltage tester on the switch wires to confirm no power. Test the switch to confirm the light is dead.
  2. Remove the old switch. Unscrew the cover plate and the two mounting screws. Pull the switch out gently. Take a photo of the existing wiring. A single-pole switch has two wires plus a ground.
  3. Identify the line and load wires. Use a multimeter or non-contact tester to determine which wire is hot (line, always live) and which is the load (goes to the light). Most dimmers require the line wire on the black lead and the load wire on the red lead. Some dimmers are not polarity-sensitive on AC—check the instructions.
  4. Prep the wires. Cut off any previously back-stabbed or damaged wire ends. Strip 3/4 inch of insulation. Straighten the copper strands and form a clean hook or leave straight for the dimmer's wire leads.
  5. Connect the dimmer. Most dimmers have stranded wire leads, not screw terminals. Connect the black dimmer wire to the line (hot) wire with a wire nut. Connect the red dimmer wire (or second black wire) to the load wire going to the light. Connect the green dimmer wire to the bare copper ground in the box.
  6. Tape and fold wires. Wrap electrical tape around each wire nut, covering the bottom of the nut up onto the insulated wire. This prevents nuts from slipping off when pushing the dimmer in. Fold wires gently accordion-style into the box.
  7. Mount the dimmer and cover plate. The dimmer body is often larger than a standard switch—fold wires carefully to make room. Secure with the provided mounting screws. Snap on the cover plate. If using a screwless plate, attach the mounting bracket first, then snap the plate on.
  8. Install dimmable bulbs and test. Screw in dimmable LED bulbs. Turn the breaker on. Slide the dimmer up slowly—the lights should brighten smoothly without flickering. If there is buzzing from the bulbs or dimmer, one of them is not compatible with the other.

Tips

Tip 1: Never use a standard incandescent dimmer with LED bulbs. At minimum, the lights will flicker. At worst, the dimmer or bulbs will fail prematurely.
Tip 2: Check the dimmer's minimum load rating. Some LED dimmers require at least 10W of combined bulb draw to function—a single 6W LED may never turn on.
Tip 3: If replacing a 3-way switch, buy a 3-way dimmer. Standard single-pole dimmers will not work in 3-way applications.
Tip 4: If the dimmer gets hot to the touch, it may be overloaded. Add up the total wattage of all bulbs controlled by the dimmer and compare to its rating (derate by 25% for LEDs).
Tip 5: Smart dimmers that connect to Wi-Fi and voice assistants are installed exactly the same way but require a neutral wire in the switch box. Check the box before buying.
