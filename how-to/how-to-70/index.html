How to Flush a Water Heater

Overview

Sediment builds up in the bottom of water heater tanks over time, reducing efficiency, causing popping noises, and shortening the tank's lifespan. Flushing the tank annually removes this sediment and keeps the heater running efficiently.

Tools & Materials

  • Garden hose (preferably 6+ feet)
  • Flat-head screwdriver (for drain valve)
  • Buckets (if no floor drain nearby)
  • Work gloves

Step-by-Step

  1. Turn off the energy source. For electric heaters, flip the breaker OFF. For gas heaters, turn the gas control valve to PILOT (or OFF). Never run an electric water heater with the tank empty or partially empty—the elements will burn out.
  2. Turn off the cold water supply. Locate the cold water shut-off valve above the tank (usually on the right side) and close it.
  3. Let the water cool. Wait 1–2 hours for the water to cool down to avoid scalding yourself. Or run hot water at a nearby faucet to drain some heat.
  4. Connect the garden hose. Thread a garden hose onto the drain valve at the bottom of the tank. Run the hose to a floor drain, sump pit, or outside (to a location where hot, rusty water won't cause damage).
  5. Open a hot water faucet. Open a hot water tap on a nearby sink or tub upstairs. This prevents a vacuum that would slow draining.
  6. Open the drain valve. Turn the drain valve counterclockwise with a flat-head screwdriver. Water and sediment will flow out through the hose.
  7. Flush with cold water bursts. After the tank drains completely, briefly open the cold water supply valve for 15–30 seconds to stir up remaining sediment. Close it and let it drain out again. Repeat 2–3 times until the water runs clear.
  8. Close the drain valve. Tighten the drain valve clockwise and remove the garden hose.
  9. Refill the tank. Open the cold water supply valve fully. Leave the nearby hot water faucet open until water flows smoothly with no sputtering (this purges air from the lines). Then close the faucet.
  10. Restore power or gas. Once the tank is completely full (no more air from the faucet), flip the breaker ON for electric or return the gas valve to ON and relight the pilot if necessary. Wait one hour for the tank to heat up.

Pro Tips

Tip 1: If the drain valve is plastic and has never been opened in years, it may crack or leak when turned. Have a brass replacement valve and Teflon tape ready.
Tip 2: If sediment clogs the drain valve and prevents draining, remove the hose, poke a wire through the valve opening, and reconnect. Or remove the valve entirely and replace it.
Tip 3: Flush the tank every 6–12 months. Homes on well water with high mineral content should flush every 6 months.
Tip 4: Listen for rumbling or popping sounds from the tank—these are signs that sediment has already built up significantly and flushing is overdue.
Tip 5: Test the TPR (temperature and pressure relief) valve after refilling by lifting the lever briefly. Water should flow out and stop when released. If it drips persistently, replace the valve.
