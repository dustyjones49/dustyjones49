How to Paint a Front Door

Overview

A freshly painted front door boosts curb appeal instantly. The key to a professional-looking finish is thorough prep, removing the door from its hinges, and using the right paint for the door material. Plan on a full day for drying between coats.

Tools & Materials

  • Exterior paint (semi-gloss or high-gloss acrylic latex or oil-based)
  • Primer (bonding primer for metal/fiberglass, stain-blocking for wood)
  • Paintbrushes (2-1/2" angled sash brush for panels, 4" brush for flat areas)
  • 4-inch foam roller and tray
  • Screwdriver (to remove door)
  • Sandpaper (120- and 220-grit)
  • Tack cloth
  • Painter's tape
  • Drop cloth
  • TSP cleaner or degreaser
  • 2 sawhorses
  • Wood filler or spackle

Step-by-Step

  1. Remove the door. Tap out the hinge pins with a hammer and flat-head screwdriver. Lift the door off the hinges and lay it flat across two sawhorses. Remove all hardware—knob, deadbolt, strike plate, and knocker.
  2. Clean thoroughly. Wash both sides of the door with TSP or a degreaser to remove dirt, oils, and old wax. Rinse with clean water and let dry completely.
  3. Sand the surface. Sand the entire door with 120-grit sandpaper to scuff the old finish and give the primer something to grip. On wood doors, fill any cracks or nail holes with exterior wood filler. Sand smooth.
  4. Tape off areas you don't want painted. Tape around any glass inserts on both sides. Use a putty knife to tuck the tape edge under the weatherstrip or gasket for clean lines.
  5. Wipe down with a tack cloth. Remove all sanding dust with a tack cloth. Any dust left on the surface will cause a rough, gritty finish.
  6. Apply primer. Use a brush to cut in around edges and panels, then roll the flat areas with a foam roller. Let the primer dry per the manufacturer's instructions (typically 1–2 hours). Lightly sand with 220-grit and wipe with a tack cloth.
  7. Paint in the correct order. Always paint a panel door in this sequence: 1) panel recesses, 2) vertical stiles between panels, 3) horizontal rails, 4) outer vertical stiles. This prevents visible overlap marks.
  8. Apply thin coats. Use a brush for details and a foam roller for flat areas. Thin coats dry smoother than one thick coat. Let each coat dry completely (3–4 hours) before applying the next.
  9. Apply a second coat. Lightly sand with 220-grit between coats and wipe with tack cloth. Apply the second coat using the same sequence.
  10. Let the door cure. Allow the final coat to dry for a full 24 hours before reinstalling hardware and hanging the door. Full curing takes 7–14 days—be gentle with it during that time.

Pro Tips

Tip 1: Never paint a door while it's hanging. Gravity pulls wet paint into drips and sags. Paint it horizontally on sawhorses for a flawless finish.
Tip 2: For metal and fiberglass doors, use a bonding primer specifically for those surfaces. Standard wood primer will peel within a year.
Tip 3: Paint in a shaded area or on a mild, low-humidity day (65–80°F). Direct sunlight dries paint too fast and causes brush marks and poor adhesion.
Tip 4: Wrap your brush and roller in plastic wrap between coats instead of cleaning them. They'll stay wet for hours if you're applying multiple coats the same day.
Tip 5: After painting the hinge edge, prop the door on painter's pyramids (small plastic stands) so the wet edge doesn't touch anything.
