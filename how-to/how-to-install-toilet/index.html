How to Install a Toilet

Overview

Installing a toilet is a manageable DIY project that takes about 2—3 hours. Whether replacing an old toilet or installing one in a new bathroom, the key is a proper seal between the toilet and the closet flange. A well-installed toilet should last decades without leaks, rocking, or clogs. This guide covers both two-piece and one-piece toilets.

Tools & Materials

  • New toilet (bowl, tank, tank-to-bowl gasket, bolts, wax ring)
  • Adjustable wrench and 6" crescent wrench
  • Putty knife or scraper (for old wax removal)
  • Hacksaw (to trim closet bolts if needed)
  • Level, bucket, rags, rubber gloves, silicone caulk

Step-by-Step

  1. Shut off water and drain: Turn the shutoff valve clockwise, flush the toilet, and hold the handle down to drain as much water as possible. Sponge out remaining water from the tank and bowl.
  2. Remove old toilet: Disconnect the supply line. Remove the caps and nuts from the closet bolts at the base. Rock the toilet gently to break the wax seal, then lift straight up (two-person job). Plug the drain opening with a rag to prevent sewer gas and dropped objects.
  3. Inspect and prepare the flange: Scrape off all old wax from the flange. Check that the flange is solidly anchored to the floor and not cracked or rusted. The flange should sit on top of the finished floor, not below it. If it's too low, use a flange extender kit.
  4. Install new closet bolts: Insert new closet bolts into the flange slots, heads down. Slide them to the correct position (12" rough-in for most toilets, 10" or 14" for some). Slip a washer and nut on loosely to hold them upright.
  5. Apply new wax ring: Set the wax ring on the flange, centered over the opening. For a better seal, consider a wax-free rubber seal. If installing in cold weather, warm the wax ring to room temperature so it compresses properly.
  6. Set the toilet: Lift the toilet over the closet bolts and lower it straight down onto the flange. Do not slide or tilt — this can deform the wax ring. Press down firmly with body weight to compress the wax. The bowl should rest evenly on the floor.
  7. Secure and trim: Place washers and nuts on the closet bolts and tighten them evenly by hand, then 1/4-turn each with a wrench. Do not over-tighten — porcelain cracks easily. Cut excess bolt length with a hacksaw, leaving about 1/4" above the nut. Snap on caps.
  8. Assemble tank and connect water: Install the tank-to-bowl gasket and bolts, following the manufacturer's instructions. Connect the supply line, turn on the water, and check for leaks at all connections. Flush several times and inspect the base.

Tips & Warnings

  • Never over-tighten closet bolts or tank bolts — porcelain will crack and the toilet is ruined.
  • If the floor is uneven, shim the low spots with plastic toilet shims (not wood or cardboard that will compress).
  • Apply a thin bead of silicone caulk around the front of the toilet base only — leave the back uncaulked so leaks are visible.
  • Use a wax-free rubber gasket if you may need to remove the toilet again soon (remodeling).
  • If the flange is cracked or broken, install a metal repair ring before proceeding — a bad flange means a bad seal.
