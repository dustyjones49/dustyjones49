How to Mount a TV on the Wall

Overview

Mounting a TV on the wall frees up floor space, improves viewing angles, and creates a clean, modern look. Most mounts support TVs from 32" to 85" and can be installed in about an hour. The key is hitting at least two wall studs and getting the height right.

Tools & Materials

  • TV wall mount kit (fixed, tilting, or full-motion)
  • Stud finder
  • Level (magnetic if possible)
  • Power drill with screwdriver bits and drill bits
  • Socket wrench set
  • Measuring tape and pencil
  • Painter's tape
  • Lag bolts (use the ones included with the mount)
  • In-wall cable management kit (optional)
  • Helper (for lifting the TV)

Step-by-Step

  1. Choose the viewing height. The center of the TV should be at eye level when seated—typically 42 inches from the floor to the center of the screen. Account for furniture height and viewing distance.
  2. Find and mark studs. Use a stud finder to locate two studs at your desired mounting location. Mark the center and edges of each stud with painter's tape. Standard stud spacing is 16", which fits most mount brackets.
  3. Mark the bracket position. Hold the wall plate of the mount against the wall spanning the two studs. Place a level on top and adjust until perfectly horizontal. Mark the lag bolt hole locations on the tape.
  4. Pre-drill pilot holes. Drill 1/4" pilot holes 2-1/2" to 3" deep into the center of each stud at every marked location. For metal studs, use toggle bolts rated for your TV's weight—lag screws won't hold in thin metal.
  5. Bolt the wall plate. Position the wall plate and drive the lag bolts through the plate into the pilot holes using a socket wrench. Start all bolts loosely, check level, then tighten fully. Do not use an impact driver—you'll over-torque and strip the wood.
  6. Attach brackets to the TV. Lay the TV face-down on a soft blanket. Attach the mounting brackets (arms) to the VESA mounting holes on the back of the TV using the bolts provided. Match bolt length to TV thickness—bolts that are too long can crack the screen internally.
  7. Hang the TV. With a helper, lift the TV and hook the brackets onto the top lip of the wall plate. Most mounts have a locking bar or safety screws at the bottom that click into place. Engage these before letting go.
  8. Adjust and tighten. Set the tilt angle if using a tilting or full-motion mount. Tighten all adjustment bolts according to the manual. Route and secure cables.
  9. Install cable management. Use an in-wall cable management kit to hide power and HDMI cables behind the drywall. Never run TV power cords through the wall without an in-wall rated kit—it's a code violation and fire hazard.

Pro Tips

Tip 1: Use masking tape on the wall to map out the exact TV dimensions before drilling. This helps you visualize the final look and catch placement mistakes early.
Tip 2: For full-motion mounts that extend far from the wall, bolt the wall plate into at least two studs plus add toggle bolts in the drywall between studs for extra shear strength.
Tip 3: Don't mount a TV above a working fireplace unless you've verified the wall temperature above the mantel doesn't exceed 100°F—heat damages TV electronics.
Tip 4: If you can only hit one stud, use a mounting plate that spans to the next stud bay with heavy-duty toggle bolts in the drywall for the second attachment point. Never mount a large TV with toggle bolts alone.
