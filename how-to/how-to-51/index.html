How to Replace a Door Knob

Overview

Replacing a door knob is a 10-minute job that requires no special skills. Whether you're upgrading an old knob, switching finishes, or fixing a broken latch, this guide covers standard interior passage, privacy, and entry knobs.

Tools & Materials

  • New door knob set (includes latch and strike plate)
  • Phillips-head screwdriver
  • Flat-head screwdriver (optional)
  • Measuring tape
  • Wood filler (if old holes don't align)

Step-by-Step

  1. Remove the old knob. Locate the set screw or detent pin on the interior knob. Press the pin with a flat-head screwdriver or loosen the set screw, then pull the knob off the spindle.
  2. Remove the rosette plate. Unscrew the two screws holding the interior rosette (trim plate) to the door.
  3. Pull out the latch assembly. Remove the two screws from the edge of the door that secure the latch, then slide the latch out.
  4. Remove the exterior knob. With everything unscrewed, the exterior half of the knob should pull freely out of the door.
  5. Measure the backset. Check the hole-to-edge distance on the door (standard backsets are 2-3/8" or 2-3/4"). Ensure your new knob is set to match.
  6. Insert the new latch. Slide the new latch mechanism into the edge hole, bevel side facing the strike plate. Screw it in place.
  7. Install the new knob halves. Insert the exterior knob through the latch, then align the interior knob and press them together until they click.
  8. Secure the rosette. Thread the interior screws through the rosette into the matching posts on the exterior half. Tighten evenly.
  9. Test the knob. Turn the knob and confirm the latch retracts smoothly. Close the door and check that it latches properly.

Pro Tips

Tip 1: Wrap a piece of masking tape around the door edge marking the latch centerline to easily align the new hardware.
Tip 2: If the new strike plate doesn't align with the old mortise, trace around the new plate and chisel out the extra space.
Tip 3: For exterior knobs, make sure the keyhole is on the outside and the thumb-turn is on the inside.
Tip 4: If screw holes are stripped, insert a toothpick coated in wood glue, let it dry, then drive the screw.
