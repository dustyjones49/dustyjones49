How to Install Baseboard Trim

Overview

Installing baseboard trim gives a room a finished, professional look and covers the expansion gap between flooring and walls. A typical room (12' × 12') takes about 2—3 hours for an experienced DIYer, or 4—6 hours for a first-timer. The key skills are measuring accurately, cutting clean miters, and coping inside corners for a gap-free fit even as wood moves seasonally.

Tools & Materials

  • Baseboard molding (primed MDF or solid wood, typically 3-1/4" to 5-1/4" tall)
  • Compound miter saw (10" works for most baseboard up to 5-1/4")
  • Coping saw with fine-tooth blades (15—18 TPI)
  • 18-gauge brad nailer with 2" brads, or finish hammer and 4d finish nails
  • Stud finder, measuring tape, level, caulk gun with paintable caulk, wood filler

Step-by-Step

  1. Measure and plan: Measure each wall segment. Mark stud locations with a stud finder and small pencil marks on the wall (these will be covered by the baseboard). Plan the sequence — start on the longest wall and work around, ending on the least visible wall.
  2. Cut outside corners at 45 degrees: For outside corners, cut each piece at 45° (one left, one right). Test-fit dry. If the corner is not a perfect 90°, adjust the angle slightly (measuring with a bevel gauge and dividing by 2 is more accurate than assuming 45°).
  3. Cope inside corners: For inside corners, butt the first piece flat against the wall on one side. For the intersecting piece, cut a 45° miter as if doing an inside corner, then use a coping saw to cut along the profile line created by the miter cut. The coped piece will wrap tightly around the profile of the first piece even if the wall shifts seasonally.
  4. Scribe for gaps (if needed): If the floor is uneven, use a scribe tool or compass to mark the bottom edge, then belt-sand or plane to the line. This prevents gaps under the baseboard and eliminates the need for thick caulk beads.
  5. Nail to studs: Position the baseboard and nail into wall studs (identified earlier). Use 2" brads or finish nails, two per stud — one near the top and one near the bottom. Drive nails slightly below the surface with a nail set. For drywall between studs, angle the nails slightly in opposite directions for better holding.
  6. Splice long runs: For walls longer than your baseboard lengths (typically 12' or 16'), join two pieces with a 30° scarf joint rather than a butt joint. The angled splice is stronger and less likely to open up as wood moves. Place the splice over a stud if possible, and glue the joint with wood glue.
  7. Fill, caulk, and paint: Fill nail holes with wood filler (or spackle for MDF), let dry, and sand smooth. Caulk the top edge where baseboard meets the wall and any inside corners — do NOT caulk the bottom edge to the floor (it will crack as the floor expands). Paint or touch up as needed.

Tips & Warnings

  • Always cope inside corners, never miter them. Mitered inside corners will always open up as wood expands and contracts, creating visible gaps.
  • Paint the baseboard before installing (or at least prime all sides). It's far easier to paint on sawhorses than on your knees with a brush.
  • For long runs, pre-assemble sections on the floor (joining boards, coping ends), then lift the whole section into place. This reduces overhead work and improves alignment.
  • If using MDF baseboard, pre-drill near the ends even for brads — MDF splits easily within 2" of an end.
  • Save cutoffs with 45° angles — you can often use them on the opposite wall orientation.
