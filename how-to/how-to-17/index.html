How to Install Vinyl Plank Flooring

Overview

Luxury vinyl plank (LVP) is one of the most DIY-friendly flooring options—it is waterproof, durable, and clicks together without glue or nails. A 200-square-foot room can be installed in a day by one person. The critical step is subfloor preparation: LVP telegraphs every bump and dip beneath it. A flat subfloor is non-negotiable.

Tools & Materials

  • Luxury vinyl plank flooring (add 10% for waste)
  • Underlayment (if not attached to planks)
  • Utility knife with hooked blades
  • Speed square, pencil, and tape measure
  • Tapping block and pull bar (vinyl flooring kit)
  • Rubber mallet
  • Jigsaw or vinyl plank cutter (guillotine-style)
  • 4-foot level, self-leveling compound (if needed)
  • Spacers (1/4-inch)

Step-by-Step

  1. Acclimate the flooring. Stack boxes flat in the room for 48 hours before installation. Vinyl expands and contracts with temperature—installing cold planks from a garage leads to gaps when they warm and expand.
  2. Prep the subfloor. Remove baseboards. The subfloor must be flat within 3/16 inch over 10 feet. Fill low spots with self-leveling compound and grind high spots. Vacuum thoroughly. Roll out underlayment if your planks do not have attached padding.
  3. Plan the layout. Measure the room width. Divide by the plank width. If the last row will be less than 2 inches, trim the first row to split the difference. Planks look best running parallel to the longest wall or the main light source.
  4. Cut the first row. Cut the tongue off the long side of the first row planks with a utility knife (so they can sit flush against the wall). Place 1/4-inch spacers between the plank edge and the wall to maintain an expansion gap.
  5. Lay the first rows. Click planks together by inserting the tongue into the groove at a 15-degree angle, then folding flat. Tap the end joints together with a tapping block and rubber mallet. Never use a hammer directly—it chips the locking mechanism.
  6. Stagger the joints. Start each new row with a plank cut to a random length—at least 6 inches offset from the previous row's end joint. Avoid H-patterns (joints aligning every other row). Use the cut-off from the end of a row to start the next row when possible.
  7. Fit around doorways. Use a jigsaw to notch planks around door casings. Undercut door jambs with an oscillating multi-tool so the plank slides underneath for a professional look.
  8. Use the pull bar on the last row. When the last row cannot be angled in (the wall blocks you), use a pull bar hooked onto the edge and tap with a mallet to engage the click-lock. Keep spacers in place against the wall.
  9. Reinstall baseboards and transitions. Remove all spacers. Install or reinstall baseboards to cover the expansion gap—never caulk the perimeter. Install transition strips at doorways. The floor must be able to expand and contract freely.

Tips

Tip 1: A guillotine-style vinyl plank cutter ($50–$80) makes fast, quiet, dust-free cuts and is worth buying even for one job.
Tip 2: Direct sunlight through a window can heat dark LVP enough to buckle it—use curtains or blinds during peak sun hours.
Tip 3: Score the top wear layer with a utility knife and snap the plank for simple cross-cuts. Use a hooked blade for ripping lengthwise.
Tip 4: Do not install LVP over more than one layer of existing flooring; too much flex causes the locking mechanism to fail underfoot.
Tip 5: Keep a few extra boxes in storage. Dye lots change, and a damaged plank 5 years later will be impossible to match.
