How to Tighten Loose Chair Joints

Overview

A wobbly chair is usually caused by dried-out glue in the joints between legs and rails. Simply adding screws is a temporary fix that often splits the wood. The permanent solution is to clean, reglue, and clamp each loose joint.

Tools & Materials

  • Wood glue (PVA yellow glue, like Titebond)
  • Bar clamps or strap clamps
  • Rubber mallet
  • Syringe or glue injector
  • Utility knife or chisel (to clean old glue)
  • Sandpaper (120-grit)
  • Damp rag
  • Rubber gloves
  • Painter's tape
  • Wooden toothpicks or shims (for loose tenons)

Step-by-Step

  1. Identify every loose joint. Turn the chair upside down. Grab each leg and try to wiggle it in all directions. Mark each loose joint with a piece of painter's tape.
  2. Disassemble the loose joints. Use a rubber mallet to gently tap the leg out of its socket. If the joint won't budge, twist carefully while pulling. Don't force brittle old wood to the breaking point—if it won't come apart, skip to glue injection.
  3. Clean out old glue. Scrape all dried, crusty glue from the tenon (the peg on the end of the rung) and the mortise (the hole in the leg) with a utility knife. Glue doesn't stick to old glue. Rough up the tenon surface with 120-grit sandpaper for better adhesion.
  4. Inject new glue. For joints you couldn't fully separate, use a glue injector syringe to force wood glue deep into the gaps around the joint. Wiggle the leg to work the glue in deeper. Apply glue to both the tenon and inside the mortise for fully separated joints.
  5. Reassemble the joint. Push the tenon back into the mortise. Tap gently with a rubber mallet until fully seated.
  6. For loose-fitting tenons. If the tenon fits sloppily, glue a thin wood shim or toothpick onto the tenon to increase its diameter. Let the shim glue dry for 10 minutes, then glue the whole assembly.
  7. Clamp the joint. Use a bar clamp or strap clamp to squeeze the leg and rail together. Position clamp pads to protect the wood surface. Tighten until glue squeezes out—not so tight that you starve the joint of glue.
  8. Wipe off squeeze-out. Immediately wipe away excess glue with a damp rag. Glue residue left on the surface will be visible under the finish.
  9. Add corner blocks (for chairs that have them). Check existing corner blocks. If they're loose, remove, clean, reglue, and screw them back in. Tight corner blocks prevent future wobble.
  10. Let the glue cure. Leave the chair clamped for at least 4 hours. Don't sit on the chair for 24 hours to give the glue full strength.

Pro Tips

Tip 1: Always mark joints and take photos before disassembly. Chair legs and rails can look identical but only fit one way.
Tip 2: Use a strap clamp for chairs—it wraps around the entire chair and applies even pressure to all legs simultaneously.
Tip 3: Never use construction adhesive, epoxy, or expanding foam for chair joints. Use only wood glue—it creates a bond stronger than the wood itself when properly applied.
Tip 4: If a dowel or tenon is broken off inside the mortise, drill it out carefully with a bit slightly smaller than the hole, then clean out the remaining fragments with a chisel.
Tip 5: After the repair, install glue-in felt pads on the bottom of each leg. They reduce stress on joints by absorbing floor impacts.
