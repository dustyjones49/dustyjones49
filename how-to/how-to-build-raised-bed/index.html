How to Build a Raised Garden Bed

Overview

A raised garden bed warms faster in spring, drains better than ground soil, reduces weed pressure, and is easier on your back. A standard 4' × 8' bed built from cedar takes about 2—3 hours and costs $100—200 in materials. Cedar and redwood are naturally rot-resistant without chemical treatment. Avoid pressure-treated lumber for vegetable beds — older treatments contained arsenic; newer copper-based treatments are controversial for food contact.

Tools & Materials

  • 4 pieces of 2×6 cedar (8' long) and 4 pieces of 2×6 cedar (4' long), plus 4 cedar 4×4 posts (24" long for corners)
  • 3" exterior-grade deck screws (2 lbs)
  • Drill with driver bits and countersink bit, circular saw or miter saw
  • Level, measuring tape, carpenter's square, speed square
  • Landscape fabric (weed barrier), staples or carpet tacks, garden soil mix (1/3 compost, 1/3 peat moss, 1/3 vermiculite or coarse sand)

Step-by-Step

  1. Choose and prepare the site: Select a level area with at least 6—8 hours of direct sunlight. Remove grass with a sod cutter or shovel. For heavily weedy areas, lay down overlapping cardboard sheets under the bed to smother weeds (it decomposes over a year). Check level — a bed on a slope will drain unevenly.
  2. Cut the 4×4 corner posts: Cut four cedar 4×4 posts to 18"—24" (for two 2×6 board courses, 18" is adequate; for three courses, use 24"). The posts will be set into the ground about 6"—8" and extend above the top board. Cut the top of the posts at a 45-degree angle for a finished look (optional but looks sharp).
  3. Assemble the short sides: Lay two 4' 2×6 boards on edge. Place two corner posts at the ends, aligning the boards flush with the outside of the posts. Pre-drill and drive three 3" deck screws through each board into each post. Countersink the screws slightly below the wood surface. Repeat for the opposite short side.
  4. Attach the long sides: Stand the two assembled short sides upright. Position the two 8' boards against the corner posts on each long side. Level, clamp, pre-drill, and screw the 8' boards to the corner posts — again, three screws per board-post connection. Check that the frame is square by measuring both diagonals — they should be equal.
  5. Add second course (optional): For deeper beds (12" total depth), stack a second set of 2×6 boards on top. Stagger the screw pattern from the lower course and keep boards tight together with minimal gaps.
  6. Set the bed in place: Position the bed in the prepared area. Press down firmly so the posts seat into the soil. Check level in both directions. If needed, dig out high corners or shim low corners with pavers or gravel.
  7. Install weed barrier and fill: Line the bottom and sides (inside) with landscape fabric, stapling it to the top rim. This prevents weeds while allowing water drainage. Fill with a quality soil mix: 1/3 topsoil, 1/3 compost, 1/3 peat moss or coco coir, plus vermiculite for aeration. Fill to about 1" below the rim and water thoroughly to settle — the soil will drop 1—2 inches.

Tips & Warnings

  • Keep the bed width to 4' maximum — you need to reach the center from either side without stepping in the bed (which compacts the soil).
  • In windy areas, drive a 2' length of rebar through the corner post into the ground for additional anchoring.
  • Install drip irrigation while filling the bed — it's far easier than trying to add it to a planted bed later.
  • If gophers or moles are a problem, line the bottom with 1/4" hardware cloth in addition to (or instead of) landscape fabric.
  • Wait 2—3 days after filling and watering before planting — the soil temperature needs to stabilize and any soluble salts from fresh compost need to leach through.
