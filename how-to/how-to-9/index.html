How to Install Subway Tile Backsplash

Overview

A subway tile backsplash is one of the most rewarding and budget-friendly kitchen upgrades. The classic 3x6-inch white tile with a running bond pattern never goes out of style. A typical 30-square-foot backsplash takes a weekend for a DIYer. Careful layout planning is the secret to a pro look.

Tools & Materials

  • Subway tile (add 10% extra for cuts and waste)
  • Thin-set mortar (premixed mastic for backsplashes)
  • 1/4-inch square-notched trowel
  • Manual snap tile cutter or wet saw
  • Level (2-foot and torpedo)
  • Tile spacers (1/16-inch recommended)
  • Grout float, sponge, and unsanded grout
  • Notched trowel, margin trowel, bucket

Step-by-Step

  1. Prep the wall. Turn off power to kitchen outlets. Remove outlet covers. Clean the wall with TSP to remove grease. If there is existing wallpaper or heavy texture, sand it flat or apply 1/4-inch backer board. The wall must be smooth, clean, and dry.
  2. Plan the layout. Find the center of the wall above the counter. Dry-lay a row of tile with spacers to see where cuts fall. Adjust the starting point so end cuts are at least half a tile wide. Mark a level horizontal reference line.
  3. Install a ledger board. Screw a straight 1x4 board to the wall just below your reference line. This supports the tiles while the adhesive sets. Make sure the board is dead level—the entire job depends on it.
  4. Spread mastic. Using the notched trowel, spread mastic over a 3-square-foot area at a time. Hold the trowel at a 45-degree angle to create uniform ridges. Comb in one direction for consistent coverage.
  5. Set the tiles. Press each tile firmly into the mastic with a slight twisting motion. Place spacers between tiles for uniform 1/16-inch joints. Stagger each row by half a tile for the classic running bond. Check level every two rows.
  6. Cut edge and outlet tiles. Measure and mark tiles for end cuts, outlets, and windows. Cut with a snap cutter or wet saw. For outlet cutouts, use an angle grinder or nippers to notch out the rectangle.
  7. Remove spacers and clean. After the mastic sets (about 2 hours if using premixed), remove all spacers. Scrape any mastic that squeezed into joints with a utility knife. Vacuum joints clean.
  8. Grout the backsplash. Mix unsanded grout to peanut-butter consistency. Force it into joints with a rubber float at a 45-degree angle. Wipe excess with a damp sponge after 15 minutes. Buff haze with a dry cloth after 2 hours.
  9. Caulk edges and seal. Caulk the bottom joint between tile and countertop with silicone caulk (not grout—it cracks at this change-of-plane). Seal grout after 48 hours. Reinstall outlet covers with box extenders if needed.

Tips

Tip 1: For a pro finish, use bright white grout on white subway tile—it blends the joints and highlights the tile shape.
Tip 2: If your counter is not level, do not follow it. Instead, start with a level ledger board and cut the bottom row tiles to fit the counter slope.
Tip 3: Premixed mastic is fine for kitchen backsplashes but never use it in wet areas like showers—it re-emulsifies with moisture.
Tip 4: Leave a 1/8-inch gap at countertop and cabinet line for caulk, not grout. Grout will crack at these change-of-plane joints.
Tip 5: Buy box extenders for your outlets when tiling over them—they bring the receptacle flush with the new tile surface, which is required by code.
