How to Install Recessed Lighting

Overview

Recessed lighting (can lights) provides clean, modern illumination without visible fixtures. Modern LED retrofit units are wafer-thin and install with spring clips directly into drywall—no heavy can housing needed. Plan the layout carefully: lights should be spaced evenly, typically 4–6 feet apart in an 8-foot ceiling, with the first light about 3 feet from the wall. Budget 2–3 hours for 4–6 lights in a single room with attic access.

Tools & Materials

  • Ultra-thin LED recessed lights (wafer/disc style) with junction boxes
  • Hole saw (typically 4-inch or 6-inch, check fixture specs)
  • Drill (corded recommended for hole saw)
  • Fish tape or glow rods (for wiring)
  • Wire strippers, wire nuts, electrical tape
  • Non-contact voltage tester
  • NM-B Romex cable (12/2 or 14/2 per circuit)
  • Chalk line & measuring tape

Step-by-Step

  1. Turn off power. Switch off the breaker for the circuit you will tap. Verify with a voltage tester. Plan to tap an existing ceiling light, switch loop, or run a new circuit from the panel.
  2. Plan and mark the layout. For an 8-foot ceiling, divide the room into equal sections. Lights should be 2–3 feet from walls and spaced roughly equal to half the ceiling height. Snap chalk lines to keep lights in straight rows. Use a stud finder to verify each location is between joists—a light centered on a joist cannot be installed without moving it.
  3. Cut the ceiling holes. Attach the specified hole saw to your drill. Align the pilot bit on the center mark and drill at full speed. Let the saw do the work—do not force it. The drywall plug will usually stay in the saw; pop it out with a screwdriver. Run the fish tape between holes.
  4. Fish cable between lights. With access from above (attic), drill 3/4-inch holes through the center of each joist between lights and pull 12/2 or 14/2 Romex. With no attic access, use a flexible installer bit and fish tape from below, drilling through joists through each 4-inch hole. Connect the first light to the switch leg or power source.
  5. Daisy-chain the wiring. At each light location, the junction box needs power in, power out (to the next light), and a short whip to the LED driver. Run cable from hole to hole, leaving 12–18 inches of slack at each opening. The last light in the chain only needs power in.
  6. Wire the junction boxes. At each light, bring the cable into the junction box through a knockout and clamp. Splice black to black, white to white, and all grounds together, plus a pigtail to the driver pigtail. Tighten wire nuts and wrap with tape. Close the junction box.
  7. Connect and install the lights. Plug the driver into the junction box. Fold the driver into the ceiling through the hole. Press the spring clips inward and push the light up into the hole. The clips snap outward, holding the light flush against the ceiling.
  8. Wire the switch. Connect the home run (cable running back to the switch) to a dimmer or standard switch. Turn the power on and test each light. Adjust any that are not flush by gently pulling down and repositioning the spring clips.

Tips

Tip 1: LED wafer lights need no can housing. They are 1/2 inch thick, mount directly on drywall, and install in 10 minutes per light. Do not buy old-style can housings.
Tip 2: A 4-inch hole saw makes a tighter hole for typical 4-inch wafer lights. A 4-1/8-inch or 4-1/4-inch hole is too loose and lets the light sag.
Tip 3: Use a Cree or similar high-CRI (90+) LED for rooms where color accuracy matters, like kitchens and bathrooms. Cheap LEDs make skin tones look sickly gray.
Tip 4: Insulation contact (IC) rated lights can touch insulation safely. Non-IC lights need 3 inches of clearance—this matters for attic installations.
Tip 5: Run a separate switch leg for groups of lights—4 lights on one dimmer, 2 on another—for flexibility. The wiring is the same; you just run two home runs to the switch box.
