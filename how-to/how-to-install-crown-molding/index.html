How to Install Crown Molding

Overview

Crown molding is the most challenging trim to install because it sits at an angle between the wall and ceiling, requiring compound miter cuts. The molding's spring angle (typically 38°, 45°, or 52°) determines the cut settings. Plan 4—6 hours per room for your first installation. The secret: cut crown "in position" (upside-down on the saw) and use corner blocks if compound miters prove too frustrating.

Tools & Materials

  • Crown molding (pre-primed or stain-grade, typically 2-5/8" to 5-1/4" width)
  • Compound miter saw (10" or 12" can handle most crown nested)
  • Coping saw with fine blades (15—18 TPI) and half-round rasp
  • 18-gauge brad nailer with 2" brads, framing nailer for backing blocks
  • Stud finder, measuring tape, level, construction adhesive, paintable caulk

Step-by-Step

  1. Install backing blocks: Since crown typically misses studs (it spans the wall-ceiling corner at an angle), install triangular or rectangular nailer blocks every 16—24" in the wall-ceiling corner. Glue and screw these to wall top plates or ceiling joists. Test that crown rests fully on the blocks.
  2. Measure and cut first piece: Start on the longest wall. For the ends against walls, cut square (90°/0°). Measure carefully — crown lengths should be cut to the exact dimension, not gapped. If you mismeasure, cut a new piece — filling crown gaps is much harder than filling baseboard gaps.
  3. Cut outside corners: Cut crown "upside-down and backwards" on the saw — the bottom of the molding rests on the fence, the top rests on the table. For a 90° corner with 38° spring angle, the miter setting is 31.6° and the bevel is 33.9°. Better method: use a crown cutting jig or set the crown "nested" in position against the saw fence at its spring angle, then cut a standard 45° miter.
  4. Cope inside corners: Cut the first piece square and butt it into the corner. For the second piece, cut a 45° inside miter (with the crown oriented correctly), then use a coping saw to cut along the relief line. The cope should follow the exact profile. Use a rasp to fine-tune the fit.
  5. Splice long walls: For walls longer than 16' (max crown board length), use a 30° scarf joint. Pre-assemble the scarf with wood glue and clamp until dry, or set each half independently and glue the joint in place.
  6. Nail and glue: Apply construction adhesive to the backer blocks. Press the crown into place and nail through the crown into the blocks and into any wall studs the crown overlaps. Nail every 16" along the top and bottom edges, alternating studs and blocks.
  7. Fill, caulk, and paint: Fill nail holes with spackle or wood filler. Caulk the top edge (crown-to-ceiling) and bottom edge (crown-to-wall), as well as all corner joints. Use a damp finger or caulk tool for smooth beads. Paint with a brush — spray is faster but requires masking the entire room.

Tips & Warnings

  • The first time you install crown, buy 2 extra feet for every wall to practice miter cuts. Crown is expensive, but wasted practice cuts are cheaper than wasted long runs.
  • Pre-paint crown before installation — it's significantly easier to paint on sawhorses than overhead on a ladder.
  • If compound miters seem impossible, use pre-made corner blocks (inside corner blocks and outside corner blocks) available at any trim supply. They eliminate all miter cutting.
  • Large crown (5-1/4"+) is heavy. Use a helper for installation and ensure backing blocks are securely screwed.
  • Dry-fit every corner before nailing. Crown that doesn't fit perfectly at a corner will look worse after caulking when it cracks.
