How to Fix a Sticking Door

Overview

A sticking door is usually caused by humidity, loose hinges, paint buildup, or a shifted frame. Most fixes take under an hour with basic tools.

Tools & Materials

  • Sandpaper (60- and 120-grit)
  • Hand plane or belt sander (for severe sticking)
  • Screwdriver (Phillips and flat-head)
  • 3-inch screws (for hinge reinforcement)
  • Utility knife
  • Pencil or chalk
  • Wood block
  • Hammer

Step-by-Step

  1. Identify where it sticks. Open and close the door slowly. Look for rub marks or drag sounds. Mark the sticking area with a pencil.
  2. Tighten all hinge screws. Loose hinges are the most common cause. Tighten every screw on both the door and jamb side. If a screw spins, replace it with a longer 3-inch screw.
  3. Check the hinge mortises. If a hinge leaf is recessed too deep or sits proud of the surface, remove it and adjust with cardboard shims behind the leaf.
  4. Sand the sticking edge. If the door rubs at the top or latch side, sand the high spot with 60-grit sandpaper, then smooth with 120-grit. Sand only where it actually sticks.
  5. Plane the door bottom. If the door sticks on the floor or threshold, scribe a line keeping a 1/8" gap, remove the door, and plane or belt-sand to the line.
  6. Bevel the latch edge. If the door binds near the latch, slightly bevel the strike edge by sanding at a 3-degree angle so it clears the jamb.
  7. Check the strike plate. If the latch doesn't catch, the strike plate may be misaligned. Loosen it, reposition, and tighten. File the opening slightly if the bolt rubs the edge.
  8. Touch up the finish. After sanding or planing, apply matching paint or clear finish to the bare wood to seal it against moisture.

Pro Tips

Tip 1: In humid months, doors swell. If sticking is seasonal, run a dehumidifier before sanding—the door may shrink back in dry weather.
Tip 2: Use chalk or lipstick on the door edge to transfer rub marks to the jamb for precise identification of high spots.
Tip 3: Never plane more than 1/8" off a door. Hollow-core doors have thin edge stiles; removing too much exposes the hollow core.
Tip 4: If the top hinge is pulling away from the jamb, the framing may be loose. Drive a 3-inch screw through the hinge into the stud behind.
