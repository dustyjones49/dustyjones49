How to Replace a Circuit Breaker

Overview

Replacing a bad circuit breaker involves working inside the main electrical panel where the service wires remain live even with the main breaker off. This guide assumes you have some electrical experience and a healthy respect for 240V power. The panel cover is the last line of defense—once removed, the metal bus bars and main lugs are exposed and lethal. If you are uncomfortable, call an electrician. A breaker replacement takes 15 minutes for an experienced DIYer.

Tools & Materials

  • Replacement circuit breaker (exact brand and type match required)
  • Non-contact voltage tester & multimeter
  • Insulated flathead screwdriver
  • Rubber-soled shoes & safety glasses
  • Flashlight or headlamp
  • Rubber-insulated gloves (optional but recommended)
  • Dry towel or rubber mat (to stand on)
  • Torque screwdriver (to tighten connections to spec)

Step-by-Step

  1. Confirm the breaker is bad. Before replacing, confirm the issue is the breaker, not an overload or wiring problem. A breaker that trips immediately with no load connected, feels loose/spongy, or shows visible burn marks or melting is likely bad. Use a multimeter to check for voltage at the breaker's output terminal.
  2. Turn off the main breaker. Flip the main breaker to OFF. This de-energizes the bus bars, making the panel safer to work in. The service wires entering the main breaker from the meter are still live—do not touch them and do not drop tools near them. Tape a warning note on the panel door.
  3. Remove the panel cover. Unscrew the dead front cover screws. Carefully lift the cover straight off—do not let it touch the exposed bus bars or main lugs. Set the cover aside where it cannot fall onto the panel.
  4. Verify power is off. Use a non-contact voltage tester on the breaker's output terminal and adjacent breakers. With the main off, only the main lugs at the top of the panel should read live. If you are unsure, do not proceed.
  5. Remove the old breaker. Unscrew the terminal screw and remove the black (hot) wire from the breaker. Grasp the breaker by the outside edge near the panel center and rock it toward the outside of the panel to release it from the bus bar tab. Pull it out. If it is a tandem or double-pole breaker, remove both wires first.
  6. Inspect the bus bar. Look at the metal tab the breaker clips onto. If it is burned or pitted, the panel (not just the breaker) must be replaced by an electrician—a new breaker will not make good contact and will arc.
  7. Install the new breaker. With the breaker in the OFF position, hook the outside lip onto the panel rail. Push the inside edge toward the bus bar until it snaps into place. Tug firmly to confirm it is seated. Insert the wire into the terminal and torque the screw to the spec stamped on the breaker (typically 20–25 in-lbs).
  8. Reinstall the panel cover. Align the cover carefully, ensuring breaker toggles go through their slots without pinching. Screw the cover on. Turn off all branch circuit breakers. Turn the main breaker ON. Then, switch on branch breakers one at a time with a few seconds between each.
  9. Test the circuit. Verify the new breaker does not trip immediately. Test outlets or lights on the circuit. If the breaker trips, a short along the circuit wiring exists—the original breaker may have been fine and failed from repeated overload or short circuit operations.

Tips

Tip 1: The replacement breaker must match the panel brand exactly—Square D Homeline, Siemens, Eaton CH, etc. Breakers are not universally interchangeable despite fitting physically.
Tip 2: Stand on a dry rubber mat or towel while working in the panel. This insulates you from the concrete floor, which can be a ground path.
Tip 3: Never touch the panel with both hands. Use one hand in the panel and keep the other in your pocket—this prevents current from crossing your heart if you accidentally contact a live part.
Tip 4: If the main breaker will not turn back on after replacement, a branch circuit breaker is still in the ON position. Turn them all OFF first, then turn the main ON.
Tip 5: A burning smell from a breaker is always serious. If you smell ozone or burnt plastic from the panel, shut off the main and call an electrician immediately.
