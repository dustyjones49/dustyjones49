How to Install a Garbage Disposal

Overview

Installing a garbage disposal is a manageable DIY project that takes about 2—3 hours. This guide covers replacing an old unit or installing one where a sink already has the proper plumbing rough-in. Always turn off power at the breaker before starting any electrical work.

Tools & Materials

  • Garbage disposal unit (with mounting assembly)
  • Plumber's putty
  • Adjustable wrench & pipe wrench
  • Flathead & Phillips screwdrivers
  • Wire strippers & wire nuts
  • Bucket & rags
  • Plumber's tape (Teflon tape)
  • Hacksaw (if pipes need trimming)

Step-by-Step

  1. Turn off power. Switch off the breaker to the disposal circuit. Test the old disposal switch to confirm no power.
  2. Remove the old disposal. Place a bucket under the trap, disconnect the P-trap and drain pipes. Insert the wrench into the mounting lug on the bottom of the old unit and twist counterclockwise to release it from the sink flange.
  3. Remove or reuse the old sink flange. If reusing, clean off old putty. If replacing, loosen the three mounting screws on the sink flange assembly, then push the flange up and out from above.
  4. Install the new sink flange. Roll a rope of plumber's putty and place it under the lip of the new flange. Press it firmly into the drain opening. From below, slide on the rubber gasket, fiber gasket, and backup ring, then tighten the mounting ring until snug. Remove excess putty.
  5. Attach the mounting assembly. Snap the snap ring onto the sink flange. Mount the rubber cushion mount and the lower mounting ring. Tighten the three screws evenly against the backup ring.
  6. Wire the disposal. Remove the electrical access cover on the disposal. Strip about 3/4 inch of insulation from each wire. Connect black to black (hot), white to white (neutral), and green or bare copper to the green ground screw. Secure with wire nuts and replace the cover.
  7. Hang the disposal. Align the three mounting tabs on the disposal with the three ramps on the mounting ring. Lift and twist clockwise until locked in place. Use the wrench on the lug to tighten if needed.
  8. Connect plumbing. Attach the discharge elbow to the disposal outlet. Connect the P-trap and drain tailpiece. Tighten all slip-nut connections by hand, then a quarter-turn with pliers. Do not overtighten.
  9. Test the unit. Insert the knockout plug if connecting a dishwasher. Turn the power back on. Run cold water and flip the switch to test. Check for leaks and vibration.

Tips

Tip 1: Most disposals come with a pre-installed power cord; if yours does not, purchase a grounded appliance cord rated for 15 amps.
Tip 2: Wrap plumber's tape around all threaded drain connections to prevent leaks.
Tip 3: Only run cold water when using the disposal—hot water can melt fats that later solidify and clog pipes.
Tip 4: If the disposal hums but doesn't spin, use the hex wrench (included) in the bottom center hole to free a jammed flywheel.
Tip 5: Always use the rubber gasket provided—skipping it leads to water dripping through the cabinet and ruining the floor.
