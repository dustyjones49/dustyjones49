How to Power Wash a Deck

Overview

Power washing or pressure washing restores a weathered deck to near-new appearance by blasting away dirt, mildew, and gray oxidized wood fibers. Done correctly, it preps the deck for staining. Done incorrectly, it gouges the wood beyond repair. The key is low pressure and proper technique. Budget 1–2 hours for a 200-square-foot deck.

Tools & Materials

  • Pressure washer (1500–2500 PSI for wood decks)
  • Fan-tip nozzle (40-degree, white tip)
  • Deck cleaner/detergent (biodegradable)
  • Stiff-bristle push broom
  • Safety goggles and rubber boots
  • Garden hose with spray nozzle
  • Drop cloths for nearby plants
  • Wood brightener (oxalic acid, if staining after)

Step-by-Step

  1. Prep the deck and area. Remove all furniture. Sweep thoroughly. Cover plants, light fixtures, and siding edges with drop cloths. Close nearby windows. Put on safety goggles—debris flies at high speed.
  2. Set up the pressure washer. Connect to a garden hose with good flow. Use a 40-degree fan-tip nozzle only—anything narrower (25-degree, 15-degree, 0-degree) will cut grooves into soft wood. Set the pressure to 500–800 PSI for softwoods like cedar or pine.
  3. Test on an inconspicuous area. Start on a scrap board or a corner of the deck hidden by furniture. Hold the wand 18 inches away and spray. If the wood fibers fuzz up immediately, you are too close or the pressure is too high.
  4. Apply deck cleaner. With a low-pressure soap nozzle, spray biodegradable deck cleaner over the deck. Let it dwell for 5–10 minutes but do not let it dry. Scrub heavily soiled areas with a stiff push broom.
  5. Power wash with the fan tip. Switch to the 40-degree nozzle. Hold the wand at a consistent 12–18 inches from the surface at a slight angle. Move with the grain of the wood in long, steady, overlapping passes. Never stop moving—pausing gouges the wood instantly.
  6. Work systematically. Start at one corner and work your way across, overlapping each pass by about 1 inch to avoid stripes. Do not go back and forth—one pass per section is usually enough. Watch for fuzzing and adjust distance accordingly.
  7. Rinse thoroughly. After the entire deck is washed, switch to a lower pressure and rinse all cleaner residue off using the same systematic pattern. Cleaner left on the wood can interfere with stain adhesion.
  8. Let the deck dry. Allow 48–72 hours of dry weather before staining or sealing. Walk on the deck as little as possible while it dries to avoid embedding dirt into the open wood grain.

Tips

Tip 1: If you see fuzz or raised grain after washing, do not panic—this is normal. A light sanding with 60–80 grit after the deck dries smooths it right out.
Tip 2: A 40-degree fan tip at 600 PSI is the sweet spot for cedar and pressure-treated pine. Hardwoods like ipe can handle up to 1200 PSI.
Tip 3: Never use a turbo nozzle or rotary tip on a wood deck—the spinning concentrated jet will destroy the wood surface almost instantly.
Tip 4: Power washing raises the grain, which helps stain penetrate better. If you are staining after, skip the sanding step and apply stain to the slightly textured surface.
Tip 5: Wear closed-toe shoes and long pants. The spray kicks up small bits of wood, nails, and debris at high velocity.
