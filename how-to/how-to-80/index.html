How to Hang Shelves

Overview

Whether you're installing bracketed utility shelves, decorative floating shelves, or a full built-in, the principles are the same: locate studs, mount brackets level, and use hardware appropriate for the wall type and expected load.

Tools & Materials

  • Shelf and mounting hardware (brackets, floating shelf rod, cleat)
  • Stud finder
  • Level (2-foot or longer)
  • Power drill with drill bits and screwdriver bits
  • Measuring tape and pencil
  • Wall anchors (toggle bolts or molly bolts for drywall-only mounting)
  • Lag screws (for stud mounting)
  • Painter's tape

Step-by-Step

  1. Plan shelf placement. Decide on height and horizontal position. Standard shelf heights: 10–12" above a desk, 40–42" from floor for side tables, 55–60" for display shelves. Mark a level reference line on the wall.
  2. Locate and mark studs. Use the stud finder to find studs along your reference line. Mark both edges of each stud. Ideally, at least one bracket per shelf should hit a stud.
  3. Mark bracket positions. Hold one bracket against the wall at the desired height and mark the screw holes. Do the same for the second bracket, ensuring they're spaced correctly for the shelf length. For shelves over 36", use a third bracket in the center.
  4. Check with a level. Place a level across the marks for both brackets to confirm they align. Adjust the second set of marks up or down as needed.
  5. Drill pilot holes. For studs: drill 1/8" pilot holes 2" deep. For drywall alone: drill holes sized for your toggle bolts. 3/8" is common for 1/4" toggles.
  6. Install anchors if no studs. Push toggle bolts (with bracket attached) through the drywall holes until the wings spring open. Pull back slightly and tighten. Threaded drywall anchors are another option for lighter loads (under 50 lbs per pair).
  7. Screw brackets to studs. Drive #10 or #12 wood screws (minimum 2" long) through the bracket into stud pilot holes. Tighten firmly but don't strip.
  8. Check bracket alignment. Set the shelf on the brackets and check level. Small adjustments can be made by loosening screws, tweaking bracket position, and retightening.
  9. Secure the shelf. Most brackets have small screw holes in the top arm. Drive short screws up through the bracket into the shelf bottom to lock it in place. For floating shelves, the shelf slides over the concealed rod or mounts to an internal cleat.
  10. Test with weight. Press down firmly on the shelf or place a moderate load on it to confirm it holds securely before loading it with heavy items.

Pro Tips

Tip 1: For heavy-duty shelving (books, kitchenware), every bracket must hit a stud. Drywall anchors alone, even toggle bolts, are not sufficient for loads over 50 pounds per bracket.
Tip 2: Use a French cleat system for floating shelves over 36" long. The cleat spans multiple studs and can support 200+ pounds.
Tip 3: If shelf brackets don't line up with studs, screw a 1x4 board horizontally into the studs, then mount the brackets to the board. Paint the board to match the wall.
Tip 4: For shelves in a row, space them at least 11 inches apart vertically for standard books, or 13+ inches for larger art and decor items.
