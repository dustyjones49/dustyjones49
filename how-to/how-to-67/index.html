How to Program a Thermostat

Overview

Programming a thermostat can cut heating and cooling costs by 10–30% without sacrificing comfort. Most programmable thermostats let you set up to four temperature periods per day for weekdays and weekends.

Tools & Materials

  • Programmable thermostat (already installed) or smart thermostat
  • Thermostat owner's manual (or download from manufacturer)
  • Pen and paper (to plan your schedule)

Step-by-Step

  1. Plan your weekly schedule. Write down what times you wake up, leave home, return, and go to sleep for both weekdays and weekends. Identify four key periods: Wake, Leave (away), Return, Sleep.
  2. Set energy-saving setpoints. In winter, set 68°F when home/awake and lower by 7–10 degrees when asleep or away. In summer, set 78°F when home and raise by 7–10 degrees when away.
  3. Access programming mode. Press the "Schedule," "Program," or "Set" button on your thermostat. Refer to the manual for the exact button sequence for your model.
  4. Set weekday Wake time. Enter the time you wake up and your preferred temperature. For example: 6:30 AM at 68°F heat.
  5. Set weekday Leave time. Enter the time everyone typically leaves the house. Set the temperature 7–10 degrees lower (winter) or higher (summer). Example: 8:00 AM at 60°F.
  6. Set weekday Return time. Enter the time you return. Set it back to your comfort temperature about 30 minutes before your actual arrival time so the house is comfortable when you walk in. Example: 4:30 PM at 68°F.
  7. Set weekday Sleep time. Enter your bedtime. Set a cooler (winter) or warmer (summer) temperature for sleeping. Example: 10:30 PM at 62°F.
  8. Program Saturday and Sunday. Repeat for weekend days, which may have different Wake and Leave times.
  9. Set fan control. Choose Auto (fan runs only during heating/cooling cycles) for efficiency, or On (constant circulation) for better air filtration.
  10. Enable the schedule. Press "Hold" or "Run Schedule" to activate the programmed schedule. The display should show "Following Schedule" or the current period name.

Pro Tips

Tip 1: Use the temporary hold or override feature to manually adjust temperature for one-off situations without erasing your whole schedule.
Tip 2: Replace thermostat batteries annually, even if the display still looks bright. Dead batteries cause the unit to lose its programming.
Tip 3: Smart thermostats learn your patterns and use geofencing. If you have an irregular schedule, a smart thermostat saves more than manual programming.
Tip 4: Don't use aggressive setbacks with heat pumps in heating mode. Setbacks over 3–5 degrees may cause the auxiliary heat strips to energize, which is more expensive to run.
