How to Grout Tile

Overview

Grouting is the final step in any tile installation. It fills the joints between tiles to create a waterproof, durable, and attractive surface. Budget 2—3 hours for a typical bathroom floor plus overnight drying. The key to great grout is timing your cleaning strokes and keeping your sponge water fresh.

Tools & Materials

  • Unsanded or sanded grout (sanded for joints over 1/8 inch)
  • Grout float (rubber-bottomed)
  • Grout sponge & two 5-gallon buckets
  • Margin trowel or mixing paddle
  • Electric drill with mixing paddle
  • Grout sealer (penetrating, not film-forming)
  • Rubber gloves & knee pads
  • Clean microfiber cloths or cheesecloth

Step-by-Step

  1. Check tile and joints are ready. Tiles must be fully set (24—48 hours minimum). Use a utility knife to clean any thinset squeezed up into the joints. Vacuum all dust and debris from the joints.
  2. Mix the grout. Pour the recommended amount of cool water into a clean bucket. Gradually add grout powder while mixing with a drill and paddle at low speed. Aim for peanut-butter consistency. Let it slake for 10 minutes, then remix briefly. Do not add more water after slaking.
  3. Apply grout with a float. Scoop grout onto the float and spread it diagonally across the joints at a 45-degree angle. Press hard to force grout fully into the joints—voids will crack later. Work in sections of about 10 square feet.
  4. Remove excess grout. Once a section is filled, hold the float at a near-90-degree angle and scrape diagonally across the tile to remove excess from the tile faces. Do not dig grout out of the joints.
  5. First sponge cleaning. Wait 15—20 minutes until grout firms in the joints. Dampen a grout sponge and wring it out until no water drips. Wipe the tile surface in a circular motion, rinsing the sponge frequently in clean water.
  6. Second sponge cleaning. Wait another 30 minutes. Wipe again with a clean, barely-damp sponge, using diagonal strokes to shape and smooth the joints. Do not let water pool on the tile.
  7. Buff off haze. After 2—3 hours when a powdery haze appears, buff the tile with a dry microfiber cloth. Do not use water at this stage—it will smear the haze into a sticky film.
  8. Let cure and seal. Allow grout to cure for 48—72 hours. Apply penetrating grout sealer with a small brush or applicator bottle, wiping excess off the tile faces. Apply a second coat per manufacturer instructions.

Tips

Tip 1: Mix only as much grout as you can apply in 30 minutes—once it starts to stiffen, do not add water to extend working time.
Tip 2: For vertical surfaces like shower walls, mix grout slightly stiffer so it does not sag out of the joints.
Tip 3: Unsanded grout is for joints 1/16 to 1/8 inch; sanded is for joints wider than 1/8 inch. Using sanded on narrow joints scratches the tile face.
Tip 4: Epoxy grout is stain-proof and never needs sealing, but it is much harder to work with—reserve it for kitchen counters, not first-time DIY projects.
Tip 5: Change your sponge water frequently. Dirty water smears grout haze across the tile and turns final cleanup into hours of scrubbing.
