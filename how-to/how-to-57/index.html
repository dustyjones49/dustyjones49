How to Weatherstrip a Door

Overview

Weatherstripping an exterior door seals gaps that let in drafts, dust, and insects. A complete weatherstrip job includes the side jambs, head jamb, and threshold sweep. Expect it to pay for itself in energy savings within one season.

Tools & Materials

  • Weatherstrip kit (foam tape, V-strip, or vinyl bulb seal)
  • Door sweep or bottom seal
  • Measuring tape
  • Utility knife or scissors
  • Cleaning supplies (rag, rubbing alcohol)
  • Caulk and caulk gun
  • Hammer and finish nails (if using nailed weatherstrip)

Step-by-Step

  1. Inspect the gaps. Close the door on a sunny day and look for daylight around the edges. On a windy day, feel for drafts with a wet hand. Measure the largest gap to choose the right weatherstrip thickness.
  2. Clean all contact surfaces. Wipe the door stop, jamb face, and door edges with rubbing alcohol to remove dirt and grease. Weatherstrip adhesive won't stick to dirty or dusty surfaces.
  3. Install the side jamb weatherstrip. Cut strips of adhesive foam or vinyl bulb seal to the height of the side jambs. Apply to the door stops (not the jamb face) so the weatherstrip compresses when the door closes. Start at the top and press firmly downward.
  4. Install the head jamb weatherstrip. Cut a strip for the top jamb. Install on the door stop surface, ensuring the ends meet the side strips without gaps.
  5. Install the door sweep. Remove the old sweep if present. Slide the new sweep onto the bottom of the door, or screw it to the inside face of the door flush against the threshold. Trim to width with a hacksaw.
  6. Adjust the threshold. Many thresholds have adjustable height screws. Raise the threshold until it lightly touches the new sweep, then back off slightly so the door closes without excessive force.
  7. Test the seal. Close the door and check for any remaining gaps. Use a piece of paper—close the door on it and pull. If it slides out easily, tighten compression or add additional weatherstrip at that spot.
  8. Caulk exterior gaps. Seal any gaps between the door frame and the exterior siding with exterior-grade caulk.

Pro Tips

Tip 1: Use V-strip (tension seal) for double-hung windows and doors with uneven gaps. It flexes to fill variable spaces better than foam.
Tip 2: Replace weatherstrip in fall before heating season starts. The adhesive bonds better in moderate temperatures (50–80°F).
Tip 3: For doors with a large bottom gap, use an automatic door bottom—it drops a seal when the door closes and lifts when it opens.
Tip 4: Don't compress foam weatherstrip completely. It works best at about 50% compression.
