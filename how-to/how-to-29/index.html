How to Wire a Three-Way Switch

Overview

A 3-way switch circuit lets you control a light from two locations, like at the top and bottom of a staircase. Unlike a single-pole switch, 3-way switches have three screw terminals: one common (darker screw) and two travelers (brass screws). The key to success is identifying the common wire—this is where power comes in at one switch and goes out to the light at the other. Budget 1 hour for a straightforward replacement.

Tools & Materials

  • Two 3-way switches (15A, match existing rating)
  • Non-contact voltage tester & multimeter
  • 14/3 or 12/3 NM-B cable (the /3 carries an extra red traveler wire)
  • Wire strippers & needle-nose pliers
  • Flathead & Phillips screwdrivers
  • Electrical tape
  • Wire nuts (red and yellow)
  • Cover plates (2)

Step-by-Step

  1. Turn off power and verify. Switch off the breaker. Test both switch locations with a non-contact voltage tester. A 3-way circuit can have power at either switch box, so test every wire in both boxes. Mark the dead circuit and confirm the light is off.
  2. Remove the old switches (one at a time). Label every wire before disconnecting. Identify the common terminal—it is usually a darker screw (black or copper-colored). The wire on the common terminal is either the always-hot feed or the switched-hot going to the light. The two traveler wires are on the brass screws.
  3. Mark the common wire. This is the most important step. Wrap a piece of electrical tape around the wire that was on the common terminal and label it COMMON. This wire must land on the common terminal of the new switch—mixing it with a traveler causes the circuit to only work in certain switch combinations.
  4. Identify the travelers. The travelers run between the two switches—they carry no power in the classic circuit. In the box, they will be a black and red wire (or two black wires in older wiring). The traveler pair goes to the two brass screws on both switches.
  5. Wire the first switch. Connect the common wire (always-hot or fixture wire) to the black/common terminal screw. Connect the two traveler wires to the two brass traveler screws—it does not matter which traveler goes to which brass screw. Connect the ground wire to the green ground screw.
  6. Wire the second switch. Wire identically: common wire to the dark screw, two travelers to the two brass screws, ground to green. The second switch's common wire goes to the light fixture.
  7. Tape and mount. Wrap electrical tape around each switch body covering the terminal screws. Carefully fold wires into the box, mount the switch, and install cover plates.
  8. Restore power and test. Turn the breaker on. The light should turn on and off from both switches. Test all four possible switch position combinations. If the light only works in certain combinations, power is on a traveler—check your common wire identification.

Tips

Tip 1: The common screw on 3-way switches is always a different color (black or darker). If your new switch has no color difference, look for the word COMMON stamped below one screw.
Tip 2: In some installations, the power enters the light box first, then the switch loop runs to both switches with a 14/3 cable. In this configuration, the common terminals connect to the hot and the fixture, and the travelers connect both switches.
Tip 3: If you have only a black, white, and ground wire but the switch has 3 terminals, you are looking at a single-pole switch that was used as a dead-end 3-way—the white wire is being used as a traveler. This is a code violation in newer installations but common in older homes.
Tip 4: Buy a 3-way switch, not a 4-way switch—the 4-way has four terminals (plus ground) and is used in the middle of a 3-way circuit when controlling from 3+ locations.
Tip 5: Use a multimeter in continuity mode to verify traveler wires if you are unsure. Remove the wires from the switch and check continuity between the two wires at each box while a helper twists them together at the other end.
