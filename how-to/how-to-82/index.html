How to Install Blinds

Overview

Installing window blinds is a straightforward DIY project that improves privacy, light control, and energy efficiency. Most blinds offer two mounting options: inside mount (brackets inside the window frame) for a clean look, or outside mount (brackets on the wall or trim above the window) when the window frame is too shallow.

Tools & Materials

  • Blinds (cut to size if needed)
  • Power drill with 1/16" drill bit and Phillips driver bit
  • Measuring tape and pencil
  • Level
  • Screwdriver (Phillips and flat-head)
  • Step ladder
  • Safety glasses

Step-by-Step

  1. Decide on inside or outside mount. For inside mount, check if the window frame is at least 1-1/2" deep (2" for 2" slats). If it's shallower, use outside mount.
  2. Measure the window. For inside mount, measure width at the top, middle, and bottom—use the narrowest measurement. For outside mount, measure the exact width you want the blinds to cover and add 2–3 inches for overlap on each side.
  3. Unpack and inspect the blinds. Ensure all parts are present: brackets, valance clips, wand or cord, and mounting screws. Read the manufacturer's instructions.
  4. Mark bracket positions. For inside mount, hold each bracket inside the upper corners of the window frame. The front edge should be flush with the front of the opening. Mark the screw holes. For outside mount, hold the bracket against the wall or trim at the desired height and mark.
  5. Drill pilot holes. Drill 1/16" pilot holes at each mark. For drywall (outside mount), pre-drill for anchors if a stud isn't present.
  6. Install the brackets. Screw the brackets into place. The open end of each bracket should face you. Check with a level across both brackets—they must be level for the blinds to operate smoothly.
  7. Snap in the headrail. Slide the front lip of the headrail into the bracket, then push up and back until the rear of the headrail snaps into the bracket. You should hear a click.
  8. Close the bracket covers. Snap or slide the bracket covers shut to lock the headrail in place.
  9. Attach the valance. Clip the valance onto the front of the headrail. Align the clips with the slots and press until they click.
  10. Test operation. Pull the cord or wand to raise, lower, and tilt the slats. Check that the blinds operate smoothly across the full range.

Pro Tips

Tip 1: If blinds are slightly too wide for inside mount, many home centers will trim them for free. You can also trim them yourself with a fine-tooth hacksaw.
Tip 2: Use a drill with adjustable clutch set to low torque. Overtightening screws in drywall anchors strips them instantly.
Tip 3: For outside mount above window trim, install the brackets into the wall studs or use heavy-duty anchors. Blinds are surprisingly heavy, especially wide ones.
Tip 4: Keep cords out of reach of children and pets. Use the cord cleat (included) mounted high on the wall to wrap excess cord length.
