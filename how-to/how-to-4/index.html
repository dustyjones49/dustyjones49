How to Replace a Bathroom Vanity

Overview

Replacing a bathroom vanity transforms the look of your bathroom in a single afternoon. This project involves removing the old vanity, addressing plumbing rough-ins, and installing the new cabinet and countertop. Plan for 4—6 hours, especially if the old plumbing needs modification.

Tools & Materials

  • New vanity cabinet and countertop
  • Basin wrench & adjustable wrench
  • Reciprocating saw or oscillating multi-tool
  • 4-foot level & shims
  • Drill/driver with 2Â½-inch cabinet screws
  • Stud finder
  • Plumber's putty or silicone caulk
  • New P-trap and supply lines

Step-by-Step

  1. Shut off water and disconnect plumbing. Close the shut-off valves, open the faucet to drain, and disconnect supply lines. Remove the P-trap. Cap the drain stub-out with a rag to block sewer gas.
  2. Remove the old vanity top. Cut through the caulk bead around the backsplash and sides with a utility knife. If it is glued down, gently pry with a putty knife. Lift off the top with a helper—cultured marble tops are heavy.
  3. Remove the old cabinet. Unscrew the cabinet from the wall—look for screws through the back rail into studs. If it is nailed, use a reciprocating saw to cut through nails or simply pry the cabinet away from the wall.
  4. Inspect and repair the wall. Check for water damage, mold, or rot. Replace any damaged drywall. Patch and sand the area behind the old vanity so the new one sits flush.
  5. Locate studs and mark height. Use a stud finder to locate and mark all studs. Draw a level line at the top of the new vanity height (typically 34Â½ inches from the floor).
  6. Set the new vanity in place. Slide the cabinet into position. Shim under the base until the cabinet is level front-to-back and side-to-side. Check the cabinet face is plumb.
  7. Screw the vanity to the wall. Drive 2Â½-inch cabinet screws through the back rail into each stud—top and bottom of the rail. Drill pilot holes first to avoid splitting the rail.
  8. Install the countertop and sink. Many vanities come with a pre-attached top. If separate, apply a bead of silicone adhesive to the cabinet top edges and set the countertop. Let it cure per manufacturer instructions.
  9. Install the faucet and reconnect plumbing. Mount the faucet through the sink holes, tighten from below with a basin wrench. Connect supply lines to shut-off valves. Install the drain pop-up assembly and a new P-trap. Turn water on and check for leaks.
  10. Caulk and finish. Run a bead of silicone caulk along the backsplash-to-wall joint and the sink-to-countertop seam. Smooth with a damp fingertip. Do not caulk the vanity-to-floor joint—it traps moisture.

Tips

Tip 1: Measure the rough-in width multiple times before buying the new vanity; standard sizes are 24, 30, 36, 48, and 60 inches.
Tip 2: Always use silicone caulk (not acrylic latex) in bathrooms—it resists mold and stays flexible with humidity changes.
Tip 3: If your floor is out of level by more than 1/4 inch, cut the toe-kick rather than shimming excessively, which leaves an ugly gap.
Tip 4: Replace shut-off valves while the old vanity is out—you have more working room and newer quarter-turn valves are far more reliable.
Tip 5: Have a helper for lifting the countertop. A single-piece cultured marble top for a 48-inch vanity can weigh over 100 pounds.
