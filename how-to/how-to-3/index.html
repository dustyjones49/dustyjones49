How to Install a Kitchen Sink

Overview

Installing a kitchen sink is a straightforward project that dramatically updates your kitchen's function and look. This guide covers a top-mount (drop-in) stainless steel sink, which is the most common DIY replacement. Budget 2—3 hours for the full install including faucet assembly.

Tools & Materials

  • Kitchen sink (top-mount/drop-in)
  • Kitchen faucet (with supply lines)
  • Plumber's putty or silicone caulk
  • Adjustable wrench & basin wrench
  • Phillips & flathead screwdrivers
  • Plumber's tape (Teflon)
  • Hacksaw or PVC cutter
  • Bucket, rags, and penetrating oil

Step-by-Step

  1. Shut off water. Close both hot and cold shut-off valves under the sink. Open the faucet to relieve pressure. Place a bucket under the supply lines.
  2. Remove the old sink. Disconnect supply lines from the faucet using a basin wrench. Disconnect the P-trap and drain tailpiece. Cut caulk around the sink rim with a utility knife. Lift the old sink out from above.
  3. Clean the counter opening. Scrape away all old caulk and putty. Clean the surface thoroughly with mineral spirits so the new sealant bonds well. Check that the opening fits the new sink.
  4. Install the faucet on the sink. It is much easier to mount the faucet on the sink before dropping the sink into the counter. Thread the faucet shanks through the pre-drilled holes and tighten the mounting nuts with a basin wrench. Attach supply lines if not pre-installed.
  5. Mount the strainer basket. Roll a rope of plumber's putty under the strainer lip. Press it into the sink drain hole. From below, slide on the rubber gasket and cardboard friction ring, then tighten the large locknut by hand and snug with pliers.
  6. Apply sealant and set the sink. Run a bead of silicone caulk around the underside of the sink lip. Lower the sink into the counter opening and press down firmly. Remove excess caulk with a damp finger.
  7. Secure with clips. Most drop-in sinks include metal clips that grab the counter from below. Slide each clip into the track on the underside of the sink and tighten the screw until snug. Do not overtighten—you can crack a stone counter.
  8. Connect the drain. Attach the tailpiece to the strainer basket. Reassemble the P-trap and connect to the wall drainpipe. Hand-tighten all slip nuts, then snug with pliers. Check alignment.
  9. Connect water and test. Connect the faucet supply lines to the shut-off valves. Open the valves and run water for 60 seconds. Check all connections for leaks. Tighten as needed.

Tips

Tip 1: Use plumber's putty on the strainer basket for stainless sinks; use silicone caulk for granite or natural stone to avoid staining.
Tip 2: If the old shut-off valves are seized, this is the perfect time to replace them with quarter-turn ball valves.
Tip 3: A basin wrench with a pivoting jaw is essential for tightening faucet nuts in tight spaces behind the sink bowl.
Tip 4: Run the water for at least 60 seconds on the first test to fill the trap with water and confirm all connections hold under continuous flow.
Tip 5: Check sink level with a torpedo level before final tightening—an unlevel sink pools water in the corners.
