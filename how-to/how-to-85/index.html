How to Repair Wood Furniture

Overview

Scratches, dents, water rings, and loose joints are common on wood furniture but rarely require professional repair. Most damage can be fixed with simple techniques and products you already have or can buy for under $10.

Tools & Materials

  • Touch-up markers or wax fill sticks (match wood color)
  • Walnut or pecan (for scratch camouflage)
  • White toothpaste or baking soda paste (for water rings)
  • Iron and cotton cloth (for steam dent removal)
  • Wood glue
  • Wood filler
  • Sandpaper (220-grit and 400-grit)
  • Soft cloths
  • Furniture polish or paste wax

Step-by-Step

  1. Diagnose the damage type. Is it a surface scratch, deep gouge, water ring, dent, or structural crack? Each type needs a different approach. Hold the piece at different angles under bright light to see all damage.
  2. Fix surface scratches. Rub a walnut or pecan meat into the scratch—the natural oils darken bare wood and hide light scratches. For deeper scratches, use a wood stain touch-up marker in a matching color, then blend with a soft cloth.
  3. Remove white water rings. Mix equal parts white toothpaste and baking soda into a paste. Rub gently over the ring with a soft cloth in circular motions. Wipe clean and polish. For stubborn rings, place a cotton cloth over the ring and run a warm iron over it for 5–10 seconds—the heat releases trapped moisture.
  4. Steam out dents. Place a few drops of water in the dent. Cover with a damp cotton cloth. Press a hot iron onto the cloth for 5–10 seconds. The steam swells compressed wood fibers back up. Repeat as needed. Let dry, then lightly sand flush.
  5. Fill deep gouges. Apply wood filler that's slightly lighter than the surrounding wood. Overfill slightly. Let it harden, then sand flush with 220-grit sandpaper. Touch up with stain and topcoat to match.
  6. Repair cracks and splits. Work wood glue deep into the crack using a toothpick or syringe. Clamp the crack closed with a bar clamp or painter's tape. Wipe off squeeze-out with a damp cloth before it dries. Leave clamped for 2–4 hours.
  7. Stabilize veneer bubbles. Slit the bubble with a razor blade along the grain. Inject wood glue under the veneer with a syringe. Cover with wax paper, place a flat block on top, and clamp or weigh down until dry.
  8. Apply finish protection. Once all repairs are dry, apply furniture paste wax or polish to the entire piece to blend the repairs and restore a uniform sheen.

Pro Tips

Tip 1: Test any repair method on an inconspicuous area first—underneath the piece, inside a drawer, or on the back.
Tip 2: For ring marks from alcohol or perfume (which have dissolved the finish, not just trapped moisture), the finish is physically damaged. Rub with a mild abrasive like automotive polishing compound, then re-wax.
Tip 3: Hide scratches on dark wood instantly with an iodine-dipped cotton swab or a brown permanent marker. Blend immediately with a cloth before it dries.
Tip 4: After any repair involving moisture or glue, wait 24 hours before applying polish or wax. The wood and finish need to fully dry and stabilize.
