How to Strip Paint from Wood

Overview

Stripping old paint from wood reveals the natural beauty underneath and prepares the surface for refinishing. There are three main methods: chemical strippers, heat guns, and sanding. Choose based on the piece size, number of paint layers, and whether it has lead paint. Always test for lead if the house is pre-1978.

Tools & Materials

  • Chemical paint stripper (citrus-based for safety or methylene chloride for speed)
  • Heat gun with multiple nozzles
  • Paint scraper (multiple sizes, carbide blade preferred)
  • Contour scraper for curved profiles
  • Wire brush and brass detail brush
  • Chemical-resistant gloves and safety glasses
  • Drop cloths and disposable chip brushes
  • Mineral spirits, steel wool (#0000), and rags

Step-by-Step

  1. Test for lead. If your home was built before 1978, use a lead test kit (available at hardware stores). If lead is present, do not sand or heat-gun—use a chemical stripper and follow EPA lead-safe practices.
  2. Set up in a well-ventilated area. Work outdoors or open all windows with a box fan blowing out. Cover the floor with a drop cloth. Chemical strippers produce strong fumes—filters or respirators may not protect against organic vapors.
  3. Apply chemical stripper. Brush on a thick, even coat of stripper with a disposable chip brush. Do not brush back and forth—lay it on in one direction. Coverage should be 1/8 to 1/4 inch thick. Let it dwell for the time specified on the label (15 minutes to several hours).
  4. Scrape off loosened paint. When the paint bubbles and lifts, scrape it off with a plastic or metal scraper. Work with the grain of the wood. Collect scraped paint in a disposable container. If paint resists, reapply stripper and wait longer.
  5. Clean details with wire brushes. Use a brass or steel wire brush to scrub paint out of carvings, grooves, and corners. Brass is gentler on the wood. A contour scraper handles curved profiles on moldings.
  6. Neutralize and rinse. After scraping, scrub the wood with mineral spirits and #0000 steel wool to remove stripper residue and remaining bits of paint. Wipe clean with rags. Let the wood dry for 24 hours before sanding or refinishing.
  7. Alternative: heat gun method. Hold the heat gun 2–3 inches from the surface, moving slowly until the paint bubbles. Immediately scrape with a pull scraper. Keep the gun moving—pausing will scorch the wood. Do not use on lead paint; the heat vaporizes lead.
  8. Sand smooth. After stripping, sand the bare wood with 120-grit, then 180-grit to remove any remaining residue and scratches. Always sand with the grain.
  9. Alternative: sanding only. For flat surfaces with thin paint, an orbital sander with 60-grit followed by 120-grit can remove paint without chemicals. This creates significant dust—wear a respirator.

Tips

Tip 1: Citrus-based strippers are safer and smell better but take 2–3 times longer to work than methylene chloride strippers.
Tip 2: Cover applied stripper with plastic wrap to keep it from drying out—this extends dwell time and improves effectiveness on thick paint.
Tip 3: Never use a heat gun near glass—the focused heat will crack window panes. Shield glass with a metal guard.
Tip 4: Steel wool should always be used with mineral spirits—dry steel wool creates fine flammable dust that can ignite from a spark.
Tip 5: A sharp carbide scraper is worth every penny. Dull scrapers gouge the wood and leave you with extra sanding work.
