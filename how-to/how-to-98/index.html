How to Edge a Lawn

Overview

Edging creates a clean vertical cut along driveways, sidewalks, and garden beds that defines the boundary between lawn and hardscape. It's the difference between a lawn that looks maintained and one that looks manicured. Edge 2–3 times per growing season.

Tools & Materials

  • Power edger (gas or electric) with a metal blade
  • Manual edging tool or half-moon edger (for smaller jobs)
  • String trimmer (weed whacker) turned vertical (emergency substitute)
  • Eye protection
  • Hearing protection (for gas edgers)
  • Work gloves
  • Flat shovel (for clearing soil)
  • Broom or leaf blower (for cleanup)

Step-by-Step

  1. Determine the edge line. For straight edges along a walk or drive, the grass overgrowth typically shows where the edge should be. For new or curved bed edges, lay out a garden hose to visualize and adjust the line before cutting.
  2. Mow the lawn first. Always edge after mowing, not before. The mower defines the general boundary and the edger creates the crisp vertical cut. You'll see exactly where to edge.
  3. Position the edger blade. Set the edger's blade depth to cut about 1/2" to 1" into the soil along the edge. The blade should be perpendicular to the ground, creating a 90-degree vertical cut. A shallow angle just shaves grass without creating the trench.
  4. Edge along driveways and sidewalks. Walk the edger along the hardscape with the guide wheel riding on the concrete. Move at a steady walking pace. Let the blade do the work—don't force it forward.
  5. Edge along garden beds. Use a half-moon manual edger for curved bed edges. Step on the top of the blade to push it straight down into the sod, rocking side to side slightly. Continue along the hose line, cutting a continuous, smooth curve.
  6. Remove the soil strip. After cutting, a strip of sod and soil will be loosened on the bed side. Use a flat shovel to undercut and remove this strip, piling it into a wheelbarrow. This creates a 2–3" deep trench that prevents grass from spreading into the bed.
  7. Clean up the clippings. Use a broom or leaf blower to sweep grass clippings and soil off the driveway or walkway back onto the lawn. Wet clippings on concrete stain it brown when they decompose.
  8. Maintain the edge. For the first edging of the season, use an edger to cut the trench. For subsequent maintenance, a string trimmer turned vertically can trim blade tips without re-cutting the full trench.

Pro Tips

Tip 1: Edge when the soil is moderately moist—not soggy and not bone-dry. Dry soil is concrete-hard; wet soil becomes a muddy mess.
Tip 2: Install permanent plastic or steel landscape edging along garden beds after cutting the trench. It blocks grass roots from creeping in and eliminates the need to re-edge that border each season.
Tip 3: Never edge within 24 hours of a heavy rain. Saturated soil doesn't cut cleanly, and the edger blade throws heavy, wet chunks that stick to everything.
Tip 4: After edging along a driveway, sweep the debris into the lawn, not into the street. Grass clippings in storm drains contribute to waterway pollution.
