How to Insulate an Attic

Overview

Adding attic insulation is the single most effective way to cut heating and cooling costs—often saving 15–25% on energy bills. Most homes need R-38 to R-60 in the attic floor. You can use fiberglass batts or blown-in cellulose/fiberglass.

Tools & Materials

  • Insulation (batts or blown-in loose fill)
  • Insulation blowing machine (rental, includes hoses)
  • Caulk and expanding foam for air sealing
  • Utility knife
  • Staple gun and staples
  • Measuring tape
  • Kneeling boards (plywood planks)
  • Dust mask or respirator
  • Safety goggles and gloves
  • Headlamp or work light
  • Ruler or marking gauge

Step-by-Step

  1. Air seal first. Before adding insulation, seal all air leaks. Caulk around plumbing vents, electrical boxes, and chimney chases. Spray foam around any gaps where wires or pipes pass through the attic floor. Air leaks render insulation far less effective.
  2. Install baffles at eaves. Staple foam or plastic rafter baffles in each rafter bay where the roof meets the attic floor. This keeps soffit vents open and prevents insulation from blocking airflow.
  3. Mark the target depth. Measure up from the attic floor and mark the target insulation depth on rafters or trusses. For blown-in cellulose, R-38 equals about 10–12 inches; R-60 is about 16–18 inches.
  4. Lay kneeling boards. Place plywood planks across joists to create a safe work surface. Never step between joists—you'll put your foot through the ceiling below.
  5. For batts: lay the first layer. Unroll fiberglass batts between joists, kraft paper facing down toward the living space. Cut batts to fit around obstructions with a utility knife. Butt edges tightly together.
  6. For batts: add a second cross-layer. Lay a second layer of unfaced batts perpendicular to the joists. This covers the joist tops and eliminates thermal bridging. Don't compress the batts.
  7. For blown-in: start blowing. Load the machine hopper and have a helper manage it while you direct the hose. Work from the far end of the attic back toward the hatch. Keep the hose parallel to the floor for even coverage.
  8. Check depth as you go. Use a measuring stick to verify uniform depth. Fill to your marked lines on rafters.
  9. Insulate the attic hatch. Attach a piece of rigid foam board to the back of the attic access door and apply weatherstrip around the hatch opening for a tight seal.
  10. Keep vents clear. Double-check that soffit, ridge, and gable vents are not blocked. Insulation should never contact the underside of the roof sheathing.

Pro Tips

Tip 1: Rent the insulation blower from a home center—many offer the machine for free with a minimum insulation purchase of 20–25 bags.
Tip 2: Do attic work early in the morning or on cool days. Attic temperatures can exceed 120°F in summer, creating dangerous working conditions.
Tip 3: Build a dam around recessed light fixtures using sheet metal or a pre-made cover to keep insulation at least 3 inches away from non-IC-rated fixtures.
Tip 4: Use cellulose over fiberglass for better air-sealing performance. Cellulose fills nooks better and resists convective air movement.
Tip 5: Wear long sleeves, gloves, and a dust mask. Fiberglass particles cause severe skin and respiratory irritation.
