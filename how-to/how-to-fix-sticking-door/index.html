How to Fix a Sticking or Rubbing Door

Overview

A door that sticks, rubs, or won't latch is almost always fixable. The majority of sticking door problems fall into four categories: loose hinge screws, a door that has shifted seasonally, a strike plate that's slightly misaligned, or paint buildup. Start with the easiest fixes before reaching for a plane or saw — most sticking doors can be fixed in under 30 minutes without removing the door.

Tools & Materials

  • Screwdriver (match hinge screws — usually #2 Phillips or flathead)
  • Wood glue and toothpicks, golf tees, or wooden matches (for stripped screw holes)
  • Hand plane (block plane is ideal) or belt sander
  • Pencil, carpenter's square, hammer, center punch
  • Graphite powder or silicone lubricant (not WD-40 on hinges)

Step-by-Step

  1. Tighten all hinge screws: Check every hinge screw on both the door side and jamb side. A single loose top hinge screw can drop the entire latch side of the door enough to misalign with the strike plate. Tighten manually — power drivers can strip the old wood.
  2. Fix stripped screw holes: If a screw spins freely without tightening, the hole is stripped. Remove the screw, squeeze wood glue into the hole, stuff the hole tightly with wooden toothpicks or a golf tee coated in glue, and let dry 30 minutes. Trim flush with the jamb or door edge. Re-drive the screw — it will bite like new wood. For a quick temporary fix, use a 3" screw through the jamb into the rough framing stud.
  3. Mark the rub spots: Open and close the door slowly. Where it sticks, you'll see a shiny rub mark on the door edge or jamb. Mark these with a pencil. Check the top edge of the door (common rubbing spot), the latch side edge, and the hinge side edge. If the door scrapes the floor, check the bottom edge.
  4. Adjust the door bottom (if scraping floor): If the door scrapes only near the latch, tighten the top hinge deeper or shim the bottom hinge outward. If it scrapes across the whole bottom, remove the door and plane or sand the bottom edge. Tip: only plane the bottom 1/8" at a time and re-test — you cannot add material back. Seal the cut edge with paint to prevent moisture absorption.
  5. Plane rubbing edges: For edge rubbing that can't be fixed by hinge adjustments, remove the door and plane the marked area. Always plane the door edge (not the jamb), and plane from the outside of the door inward to prevent splintering the corners. Plane in shallow passes, test-fitting frequently. Touch up the planed edge with paint or stain.
  6. Adjust the strike plate: If the door closes and latches but you have to push/pull hard to engage the latch, the strike plate is slightly misaligned. Observe where the latch bolt contacts the strike. Sometimes bending the small tab inside the strike plate (with pliers) creates additional tension. For larger misalignment, remove the strike plate, fill the old screw holes, reposition the plate, and re-drill. If only 1/16" off, use a file to elongate the strike hole slightly.

Tips & Warnings

  • Seasonal sticking (worse in summer, fine in winter) is caused by swelling from humidity. A dehumidifier in the room or improved ventilation may fix it without any carpentry.
  • If the gap between the door and jamb is uneven (large gap on the hinge side at the top, rubbing on the latch side at the top), the door itself is out of square and hinge adjustment won't fully fix it — you'll need to plane.
  • Use powdered graphite to lubricate the latch bolt, not oil-based lubricants. Graphite doesn't attract dirt and won't gum up over time.
  • If every door in the house suddenly starts sticking, the problem may be foundation settlement or excessive humidity. Address the root cause rather than planing every door.
