How to Install Laminate Flooring

Overview

Laminate flooring is one of the most DIY-friendly flooring options, using a click-lock floating floor system that requires no glue or nails. For a typical room (150—250 sq ft), installation takes 1—2 days. The floor floats over an underlayment pad with an expansion gap around the perimeter. Proper acclimation is critical — laminate must sit in the room for 48 hours before installation.

Tools & Materials

  • Laminate flooring planks (add 10% for waste), matching underlayment with moisture barrier
  • Tapping block, pull bar, spacers (1/4" or 3/8")
  • Circular saw or laminate cutter, jigsaw for curves, miter saw (optional)
  • Measuring tape, pencil, carpenter's square
  • Hammer, pry bar (for removing baseboards), utility knife

Step-by-Step

  1. Acclimate and prep the room: Bring laminate boxes into the room and let them acclimate for 48 hours minimum. Remove all baseboards (label them for reinstallation), clean the subfloor thoroughly, and check flatness — no more than 3/16" variation over 10'. Fill low spots with leveling compound.
  2. Install underlayment: Roll out the underlayment perpendicular to the plank direction. Butt edges together without overlapping. Tape seams with vapor barrier tape. Run the underlayment up the walls about 1—2" — you'll trim it later. For concrete subfloors, use underlayment with an integrated moisture barrier.
  3. Plan the layout: Measure the room width and divide by plank width to determine the last row's width. If the last row will be less than 2" wide, rip the first row narrower so both edges are balanced. For best appearance, run planks parallel to the longest wall or the main light source.
  4. Undercut door jambs and casings: Place a piece of laminate (plus underlayment) against the door jamb and cut with an oscillating multi-tool or flush-cut saw. The flooring should slide under the jamb for a seamless look. This is one of the most important steps for a professional result.
  5. Install the first rows: Start in a corner, tongue side facing the wall. Place 1/4" spacers between the plank edge and the wall. Click planks together end-to-end for the first row. Cut the last plank to length, leaving a 1/4" gap. Use the cutoff to start the second row (stagger end joints by at least 12").
  6. Continue laying rows: Click new planks into the previous row at a low angle, then press down to lock. Use a tapping block and hammer to close any gaps along the long edge, and a pull bar on the last row near the wall. Never hammer directly on the plank edge — the locking mechanism will break.
  7. Cut around obstacles: Use a jigsaw for curves around pipes and vents. Leave 1/4" gap around all obstacles and cover with escutcheon rings or pipe collars. For heating vents, cut the opening with a jigsaw from a marked template.
  8. Install final row and transitions: Rip the last row to width (minus 1/4" for expansion gap). Install transitions at doorways — T-molding between laminate rooms, reducer strips where laminate meets lower flooring, and end caps at sliding doors or fireplaces. Reinstall or replace baseboards.

Tips & Warnings

  • Do not skip the acclimation period — laminate expands and contracts with humidity, and unacclimated planks will buckle after installation.
  • Stagger end joints by at least 12" between adjacent rows. Never align joints — H-patterns look unnatural and create weak points.
  • Laminate and water do not mix. Wipe up spills immediately. Never wet-mop laminate — use a damp (barely moist) microfiber mop only.
  • Heavy furniture should have felt pads on the feet to prevent scratching. Rolling chairs need chair mats.
  • Keep a box of extra planks from the same dye lot for future repairs. Manufacturer styles are discontinued regularly.
