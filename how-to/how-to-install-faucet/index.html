How to Install a Faucet

Overview

Replacing a bathroom or kitchen faucet is one of the most common DIY plumbing projects and typically takes 45—90 minutes. Modern faucets come with flexible supply lines and simplified mounting hardware that makes installation straightforward. The process is similar for single-hole, widespread, and centerset faucets, with minor differences in deck plate requirements.

Tools & Materials

  • New faucet with hardware (check it includes supply lines, gasket, and mounting nuts)
  • Basin wrench or adjustable wrench
  • Plumber's putty or silicone (check manufacturer's instructions)
  • Bucket and rags
  • Flashlight or headlamp (under-sink visibility)

Step-by-Step

  1. Shut off water: Turn off both hot and cold shutoff valves under the sink. Open the faucet to release pressure and drain remaining water. Place a bucket under the connections to catch drips.
  2. Remove old faucet: Disconnect supply lines from shutoff valves. Use a basin wrench to loosen the mounting nuts holding the faucet to the sink. These are often corroded — penetrating oil helps if they're stuck. Lift the old faucet out and clean the sink surface thoroughly.
  3. Apply gasket or sealant: Many new faucets have a rubber or foam gasket that seals the base to the sink. If not, apply a thin bead of plumber's putty or silicone under the faucet base. For widespread faucets, seal each handle base individually.
  4. Position the faucet: Insert the faucet through the mounting holes. For single-hole faucets with a deck plate covering three holes, ensure the plate is centered and seated evenly.
  5. Secure mounting hardware: From underneath, thread the mounting nuts onto the faucet shanks. Hand-tighten, then use a basin wrench for 1/4 to 1/2 additional turn. Do not over-tighten — you can damage the sink. The faucet should not shift when you press on it.
  6. Connect supply lines: Attach the supply lines to the corresponding hot and cold shutoff valves. Hot is typically on the left, cold on the right. Use two wrenches (one to hold the valve body, one to tighten the nut) to avoid twisting copper lines in the wall.
  7. Install drain assembly (if replacing): Apply plumber's putty under the drain flange, insert into sink, and thread the drain body from below. Tighten the compression nut, connect the pop-up rod, and adjust the stopper height.
  8. Test for leaks: Turn water on slowly. Check all connections — supply lines at valves, supply lines at faucet, and drain assembly. Run water for 2—3 minutes and inspect underneath with a flashlight. Tighten any drips.

Tips & Warnings

  • Always use two wrenches when tightening supply lines — one to hold the stop valve body, one to turn the nut. This prevents twisting copper pipes inside the wall.
  • If your shutoff valves are old gate valves, they may not fully close. If water continues to drip, shut off the main house valve and replace the angle stops.
  • Remove the aerator from the new faucet and run water for 30 seconds to flush out any manufacturing debris, then reinstall.
  • For widespread faucets, flexible supply hoses between handles and spout make installation much easier than rigid tubing.
