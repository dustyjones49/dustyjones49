How to Install an Outdoor Outlet

Overview

Installing an outdoor GFCI outlet provides safe power for landscaping, holiday lights, and power tools. The National Electrical Code requires all outdoor outlets to be GFCI-protected and have a weatherproof while-in-use cover that protects the outlet even when cords are plugged in. This guide assumes you are tapping off an interior outlet on the same circuit. Budget 2–3 hours and check local codes for permit requirements.

Tools & Materials

  • Weather-resistant GFCI outlet (WR/TR marked for exterior use)
  • Weatherproof while-in-use electrical box (exterior-rated)
  • Outdoor-rated NM-B or UF cable (if running through walls)
  • Non-contact voltage tester & multimeter
  • Hammer drill with 1/2-inch masonry bit (for brick or stucco)
  • Wire strippers, pliers, screwdrivers
  • Silicone caulk (exterior-grade)
  • 1/2-inch conduit & fittings (if surface-mounting on wall exterior)

Step-by-Step

  1. Turn off power and plan the location. Switch off the breaker. The outlet must be at least 12 inches above grade (ground level). Choose a location on the exterior wall directly opposite an interior outlet box in the same stud bay if possible.
  2. Tap off an interior outlet. Remove the interior outlet's cover plate and pull out the receptacle. Verify power is off. Remove a knockout from the back or side of the interior box. Feed a length of ROMEX/UF cable from the exterior opening through the same stud cavity to the interior box.
  3. Cut the exterior opening. From outside, mark the box outline at the chosen location. Drill a pilot hole from the inside to confirm the exact spot. Cut the exterior opening with a hole saw or reciprocating saw. For stucco, drill corner holes and cut with a masonry blade. For brick, use a hammer drill around the perimeter.
  4. Mount the exterior box. Feed the cable through the KO in the back of the weatherproof box. Apply a bead of silicone caulk around the back edges of the box where it contacts the wall. Screw the box to the wall—into sheathing, use exterior-grade screws; into masonry, use Tapcon anchors.
  5. Wire the GFCI outlet. GFCI outlets have LINE and LOAD terminals. Connect the power feed (from interior box) to the LINE terminals: black to the brass LINE HOT, white to the silver LINE NEUTRAL. Connect the ground wire to the green screw. Leave the LOAD terminals empty unless you are daisy-chaining to another outdoor outlet.
  6. Pigtail at the interior box. Inside, connect the new cable to the existing wiring: splice blacks together with a wire nut plus a pigtail to the outlet, whites together plus pigtail, and all grounds together plus pigtails to both the outlet and the box if metal. Fold wires in carefully and reinstall the interior outlet.
  7. Test and seal. Turn power on. Press the RESET button on the GFCI. Plug in a tester—verify correct wiring (lights should show correct polarity and ground). Press the TEST button—the outlet should trip and cut power. Press RESET again. Apply silicone caulk around the outdoor box perimeter for a watertight seal.
  8. Install the in-use cover. Snap or screw the weatherproof while-in-use cover onto the outdoor box. These covers have deep lids with cord pass-throughs that protect the outlet even with a plug inserted.

Tips

Tip 1: A GFCI outlet protects itself and everything downstream on the LOAD terminals. One outdoor outlet can protect an entire outdoor circuit.
Tip 2: If the outdoor GFCI trips frequently during rain and nothing is plugged in, water is getting into the box—re-caulk and ensure the in-use cover seals tightly.
Tip 3: The LINE vs LOAD terminals on a GFCI matter. If you reverse them, the outlet will work but will not provide GFCI protection—the TEST button will not trip it.
Tip 4: Concealed outdoor Romex must be buried 24 inches deep. If running a surface raceway on the exterior wall, use rigid conduit with proper weathertight fittings.
Tip 5: NEC requires at least one outdoor receptacle at the front and back of a residence. Install a dedicated 20A circuit if you plan to run heavy loads like electric grills or power tools.
