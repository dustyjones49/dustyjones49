How to Clean Grout Lines

Overview

Grout is porous and absorbs dirt, grease, and mildew over time, turning from its original light color to dark gray or brown. Regular cleaning with the right methods restores the original color without damaging the tile or grout.

Tools & Materials

  • Baking soda
  • White vinegar
  • Hydrogen peroxide (3%)
  • Oxygen bleach powder (sodium percarbonate)
  • Grout brush or stiff-bristle toothbrush
  • Steam cleaner (optional but very effective)
  • Spray bottle
  • Microfiber cloths
  • Small bowl
  • Rubber gloves

Step-by-Step

  1. Start with the gentlest method. Mix equal parts warm water and white vinegar in a spray bottle. Spray grout lines, let sit for 5 minutes, then scrub with a grout brush. Wipe clean with a damp cloth. This works for light surface dirt.
  2. Use baking soda paste for moderate dirt. Mix baking soda with enough water to form a thick paste. Apply the paste along the grout lines. Spray vinegar over the top—it will fizz. When the fizzing stops, scrub with the brush. Rinse with water.
  3. Use oxygen bleach for deep stains. Mix oxygen bleach powder with warm water per package instructions. Apply to grout lines and let it sit for 15–30 minutes. Scrub, then rinse thoroughly. Oxygen bleach is color-safe and won't damage colored grout.
  4. Try hydrogen peroxide and baking soda. For stubborn dark stains, make a paste of hydrogen peroxide and baking soda. Apply liberally, let it sit for 15 minutes, scrub hard, and rinse. Peroxide whitens and disinfects.
  5. Use a steam cleaner for best results. Steam at 200+ degrees loosens embedded dirt without chemicals. Run the steam nozzle slowly along each grout line, then immediately wipe with a microfiber cloth. Steam also kills mold and mildew spores.
  6. For bleach (last resort). If grout is still stained, apply a 50/50 mix of chlorine bleach and water with a small brush. Ventilate the room. Let sit 5 minutes, scrub, and rinse thoroughly. Never mix bleach with vinegar or ammonia—toxic gases result.
  7. Rinse thoroughly. Wipe the entire floor or wall with clean water at least twice to remove all cleaning residue. Residue left in grout attracts dirt faster.
  8. Dry completely. Buff grout lines with a dry microfiber cloth. Run a fan or open windows to accelerate drying.

Pro Tips

Tip 1: Seal grout after deep cleaning. Apply a penetrating grout sealer with a brush applicator bottle. Sealed grout repels water and stains, making future cleaning much easier. Reapply sealer every 1–2 years.
Tip 2: Avoid acidic cleaners (straight vinegar, lemon juice, muriatic acid) on unsealed natural stone tiles like marble, travertine, or limestone—acid etches the stone permanently.
Tip 3: For bathroom grout with pink or black mildew, use the peroxide or oxygen bleach method. Chlorine bleach whitens but doesn't penetrate porous grout to kill mold roots the way oxygen bleach does.
Tip 4: After shower use, squeegee the walls and leave the bathroom door open. Reducing standing moisture on grout prevents mildew from forming in the first place.
