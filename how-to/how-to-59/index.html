How to Replace Window Screen

Overview

Replacing a torn window screen is a quick $10 fix that takes about 20 minutes per screen. You'll pull new screen mesh tight and secure it with a spline (rubber cord) that presses into a groove in the frame.

Tools & Materials

  • Replacement screen mesh (fiberglass or aluminum)
  • Spline roller (convex and concave wheels)
  • Replacement spline (match diameter to groove)
  • Utility knife
  • Flat-head screwdriver (to remove old spline)
  • Scissors
  • Awl or small pick
  • Clamps or a helper

Step-by-Step

  1. Remove the screen frame. Lift the screen out of the window track. For full-frame screens, unscrew or release the clips.
  2. Remove the old spline. Use a flat-head screwdriver or awl to lift one end of the old rubber spline. Pull the spline out along the entire groove. Discard it—old spline won't hold tight.
  3. Remove the old mesh. With the spline out, the old screen lifts away freely. Clean the spline groove of any dirt or debris with a brush or cloth.
  4. Lay the new screen mesh. Cut a piece of new screen mesh 2 inches larger than the frame on all sides. Lay it over the frame, aligning the weave with the frame edges.
  5. Start the spline in one corner. Use the convex (rounded) wheel of the spline roller to press new spline into the groove, starting in one corner. Work one side at a time, keeping the screen slightly loose—don't pull tight yet.
  6. Roll in the spline on all four sides. Go around the entire frame, gently pushing the spline into the groove. Keep the mesh flat but don't stretch it taut yet.
  7. Tighten the screen. With the spline fully seated, use the concave (grooved) wheel to go around again, pressing the spline deeper and pulling the screen taut. The mesh should be drum-tight with no waves.
  8. Trim the excess mesh. Run a sharp utility knife along the outside edge of the spline groove, cutting away all excess screen material. Hold the blade flat against the frame to avoid cutting the spline.
  9. Reinstall the screen. Place the finished screen back into the window track and secure any clips or fasteners.

Pro Tips

Tip 1: Use fiberglass mesh for most windows—it's easier to work with, won't dent, and costs less than aluminum. Use aluminum for high-traffic areas like screen doors.
Tip 2: Match the spline diameter to the groove. Buy a spline sampler kit if you're unsure of the size. Common sizes are 0.125", 0.140", and 0.160".
Tip 3: On hot days, aluminum frames expand. Screen in the shade or indoors so the mesh stays tight when the frame cools and contracts.
Tip 4: Use clamps or weights to hold the frame in place while you work—it keeps the frame from sliding as you press in the spline.
