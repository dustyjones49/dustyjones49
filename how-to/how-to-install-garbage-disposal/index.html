How to Install a Garbage Disposal

Overview

Installing a garbage disposal takes about 1.5—2.5 hours and requires basic plumbing and electrical skills. The disposal mounts to a special sink flange and connects to the drain system. Most installations are straightforward replacements of an old unit, but new installations require adding a switched electrical outlet under the sink and a dishwasher air gap or high loop if you have a dishwasher.

Tools & Materials

  • Garbage disposal unit (with mounting hardware, gasket, and splash guard)
  • Electrical: 12/2 NM cable, switch, switch box, GFCI outlet (if new circuit), wire nuts or Wago connectors
  • Screwdriver set, pliers, channel-lock pliers, hammer
  • Plumber's putty, plumber's tape, pipe joint compound
  • Bucket, rags, flashlight, voltage tester

Step-by-Step

  1. Turn off power: Shut off the circuit breaker for the disposal circuit. Verify power is off with a non-contact voltage tester at the outlet or wiring junction. Never rely on just the wall switch.
  2. Install the sink flange: Remove the old drain assembly. Roll a rope of plumber's putty (1/2" thick) and place it under the new flange. Press the flange into the sink drain opening. From below, slide on the fiber gasket, backup ring, and snap ring. Tighten the three mounting screws evenly until the flange is snug and putty squeezes out. Wipe excess putty.
  3. Mount the disposal: Attach the mounting ring assembly to the sink flange under the sink. The disposal hangs from this ring. Align the three mounting tabs on the disposal with the ramp in the mounting ring, then rotate the disposal clockwise until it locks into place.
  4. Connect drain plumbing: Connect the disposal's discharge elbow to the drain pipe. If replacing an old unit, the existing P-trap may line up — otherwise, you may need to cut and reassemble PVC drain components. Ensure a 1/4" per foot downward slope on horizontal runs.
  5. Knock out dishwasher inlet (if needed): If connecting a dishwasher, insert a screwdriver into the dishwasher inlet on the disposal and tap firmly with a hammer to remove the knockout plug. Pull the plug out of the disposal chamber (don't leave it inside). Connect the dishwasher drain hose with a hose clamp.
  6. Wire the disposal: Connect the disposal's leads to the electrical supply: black to black (hot), white to white (neutral), and green/bare to green ground screw. Secure wire nuts tightly and tuck wires into the wiring compartment. Replace the cover plate.
  7. Test operation: Turn on the water, then turn on the circuit breaker. Run cold water and briefly switch on the disposal. Check for leaks at all connections — flange, discharge elbow, and drain pipe joints. Torque sink flange screws if you see drips.

Tips & Warnings

  • Always run cold water when using the disposal — hot water can melt fats that then solidify downstream in the pipes.
  • Use a high loop or air gap for dishwasher drain connections to prevent dirty sink water from backflowing into the dishwasher.
  • Disposals are heavy (12—18 lbs). Use a car jack or stack of books underneath to support it while aligning the mounting ring.
  • Run ice cubes and citrus peels through a new disposal monthly to clean the grinding chamber and keep blades sharp.
  • Never put fibrous materials (celery, corn husks), grease, or large bones in the disposal — they can jam the motor or clog pipes.
