How to Cut Tile

Overview

Cutting tile accurately is the most critical skill in tiling. Three main methods exist: a manual snap cutter for straight cuts, a wet saw for complex cuts and hard porcelain, and tile nippers for curves. Mastering all three gives you professional results with minimal wasted tile.

Tools & Materials

  • Manual snap tile cutter (rated for your tile size)
  • Wet saw with continuous-rim diamond blade
  • Tile nippers
  • Speed square & grease pencil
  • Measuring tape
  • Angle grinder with diamond blade (for notches)
  • Rubbing stone or tile file
  • Safety glasses & ear protection

Step-by-Step

  1. Measure and mark the cut line. Measure the space carefully. Place the tile in position and mark the cut with a pencil or grease marker. For L-shaped cuts around door jambs, make a cardboard template first.
  2. Score with a snap cutter (straight cuts). Place the tile on the cutter bed, aligning your mark with the cutting wheel. Press the handle down firmly and pull the wheel across the tile in one smooth, even pass. Never go back and forth—it creates a jagged break.
  3. Snap the tile. Position the breaker bar over the score line. Push the handle down with a quick, firm motion. The tile should snap cleanly along the score. Speed creates a clean break; slow pressure produces ragged edges.
  4. For wet saw cuts. Fill the saw reservoir with water to the fill line. Mark the tile. Turn the saw on and let the blade reach full speed. Feed the tile slowly and steadily through the blade, keeping the tile flat on the table. Let the blade do the work.
  5. For L-cuts and notches. Score the straight portion with the snap cutter. For the short leg, use an angle grinder with a diamond blade. Cut from the edge toward the score line, stopping just before intersection. Snap off the waste piece.
  6. For curved cuts (around pipes). Trace the curve onto the tile. Use tile nippers to nibble away small pieces along the curve. Work from the edge inward in small bites. Smooth the cut edge with a rubbing stone.
  7. Smooth every cut edge. Run a rubbing stone or the diamond side of a tile file along every cut edge to remove the sharp glass-like edge. This prevents cuts during handling and improves the finished look.
  8. Test-fit before mortaring. Always dry-fit the cut tile in place before spreading thinset. A rubbing stone can shave off small amounts if the fit is tight.

Tips

Tip 1: Always score the glazed side of the tile—scoring the back produces a ragged, uneven break.
Tip 2: Dull snap-cutter wheels cause chipping. Replace the cutting wheel after every 500–800 cuts for consistent results.
Tip 3: For porcelain tile, always use a wet saw. Porcelain is too hard and brittle for most manual snap cutters to score reliably.
Tip 4: When using a wet saw, wear ear protection and safety glasses. The noise exceeds 90 decibels and water sprays small chips.
Tip 5: Hide cut edges under baseboards or in corners whenever possible so only factory edges are visible.
