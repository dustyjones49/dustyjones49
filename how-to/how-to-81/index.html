How to Install Curtain Rods

Overview

Proper curtain rod placement dramatically changes how a room feels. Hanging the rod higher and wider than the window makes the window look larger and lets in more light. The right hardware for your wall type ensures the rod stays up under the weight of drapes.

Tools & Materials

  • Curtain rod and brackets (included in rod kit)
  • Stud finder
  • Level
  • Power drill with drill bits and screwdriver bits
  • Measuring tape and pencil
  • Wall anchors (toggle bolts or molly bolts for drywall-only mounting)
  • Screws (usually included with brackets)
  • Step ladder

Step-by-Step

  1. Determine rod height. The sweet spot is 4–6 inches above the top of the window trim, or about halfway between the top of the window and the ceiling. For 8-foot ceilings, mount at around 84–90 inches from the floor.
  2. Determine rod width. The rod should extend 8–12 inches beyond each side of the window frame. This allows curtains to stack fully off the glass when open, maximizing natural light.
  3. Mark the bracket positions. Measure out from the window edge on each side and mark the bracket height. Use a long level to ensure both marks are at the same height—a laser level makes this faster.
  4. Check for studs. Use a stud finder at each bracket location. If you find a stud, great—use the included wood screws. If not, mark the drywall anchor positions.
  5. Drill pilot holes. For studs: drill 1/8" pilot holes. For drywall anchors: drill the hole size specified on the anchor packaging.
  6. Install anchors or screws. Push toggle bolts through the drywall, or tap in threaded anchors. For studs, drive the screw directly through the bracket into the pilot hole.
  7. Mount the brackets. Screw brackets into place and check with a level before fully tightening. Slight adjustments are easier with screws partially driven.
  8. Install the rod and curtains. Thread curtains onto the rod. Place the rod onto the brackets and secure with set screws. Add finials (end caps) to each end.
  9. Adjust curtains. Arrange curtains evenly and let them hang for 24 hours before considering hemming—fabric relaxes and may drop slightly.

Pro Tips

Tip 1: For heavy blackout drapes (over 15 pounds), every bracket must hit a stud. Toggle bolts in drywall alone will fail over time with the daily force of opening and closing.
Tip 2: Use a center support bracket for rods spanning more than 60 inches, even if the manufacturer says it's optional. It prevents sagging.
Tip 3: Floor-length curtains should just kiss the floor or have a 1/2" gap. Measure from the rod (not the bracket) to the floor for exact length.
Tip 4: If mounting into brick or concrete, use a hammer drill with masonry bits and plastic sleeve anchors designed for your rod's weight.
