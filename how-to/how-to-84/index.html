How to Refinish Furniture

Overview

Refinishing furniture strips away old, damaged finish and replaces it with fresh stain and protective topcoat. This weekend project transforms garage-sale finds and worn family pieces into showroom-quality furniture. The process works on solid wood and wood veneer.

Tools & Materials

  • Chemical stripper (citrus-based for beginners, methylene chloride for professionals)
  • Plastic scraper and putty knife
  • Rubber gloves and safety goggles
  • Respirator mask (for solvent-based strippers)
  • Sandpaper: 80, 120, 150, 220 grit
  • Orbital sander (optional, speeds up the work)
  • Wood stain (oil-based or water-based)
  • Polyurethane or varnish topcoat
  • Natural bristle brush (for oil-based) or synthetic brush (for water-based)
  • Lint-free rags
  • Mineral spirits (for cleanup)
  • Drop cloth
  • Steel wool (#0000 for final smoothing)

Step-by-Step

  1. Set up a workspace. Work outdoors or in a well-ventilated garage. Lay down a drop cloth. Remove all hardware (knobs, pulls, hinges) and set aside.
  2. Apply chemical stripper. Brush on a thick, even coat of stripper using an old paintbrush. Let it sit for the time specified on the label (typically 15–30 minutes). The old finish will bubble and wrinkle.
  3. Scrape off the old finish. Use a plastic scraper to remove the loosened finish, working in the direction of the wood grain. Plastic won't gouge the wood like metal scrapers. Collect the gunk in a disposable container.
  4. Repeat for stubborn areas. Detailed areas (carvings, spindles, corners) may need a second application of stripper. Use steel wool or a stiff-bristle brush to clean out these spots.
  5. Neutralize the surface. Wipe the entire piece with mineral spirits on a rag to remove stripper residue and neutralize the surface. Let it dry completely.
  6. Sand progressively. Start with 80-grit to remove any remaining finish and smooth rough areas. Progress through 120-grit, then 150-grit, and finish with 220-grit. Always sand with the grain. Vacuum and wipe with a tack cloth between grits.
  7. Apply stain (optional). If you want to change the wood color, brush or wipe on wood stain with a rag. Apply evenly, wait 5–15 minutes, then wipe off the excess. Let it dry 24 hours. Apply a second coat for a deeper color.
  8. Apply the topcoat. Brush on a thin coat of polyurethane. Use long, even strokes following the grain. Let it dry per label instructions (typically 4–6 hours). Lightly sand with 320-grit or #0000 steel wool and wipe clean.
  9. Apply additional coats. Apply 2–3 coats total, sanding between each. The final coat should not be sanded. Allow 24 hours for the final coat to cure before use.

Pro Tips

Tip 1: Test the finish removal process on a hidden area first—bottom of a table, back of a drawer front—to understand how the old finish reacts to stripper.
Tip 2: Large, flat surfaces are ideal for an orbital sander. Use only fine grits (150+) and keep the sander moving—pausing even briefly creates dish-shaped depressions.
Tip 3: For veneer furniture, hand-sand only. Orbital sanders can burn through the thin veneer layer in seconds, exposing the substrate underneath.
Tip 4: Wipe-on polyurethane is more forgiving for beginners. Apply with a lint-free cloth in thin coats—it self-levels and avoids brush marks.
Tip 5: Dispose of stripper residue and solvent-soaked rags properly. Lay rags flat outdoors to dry before discarding—they can spontaneously combust when balled up.
