How to Reset a Garbage Disposal

Overview

When a garbage disposal hums but doesn't grind, or makes no sound at all, it has likely tripped its internal overload protector or become jammed. Resetting and unjamming it is a 5-minute fix that requires no disassembly.

Tools & Materials

  • Allen wrench (usually 1/4"—often attached to the disposal)
  • Flashlight
  • Tongs or long pliers (never put your hand in the disposal)

Step-by-Step

  1. Turn off the disposal. Make sure the wall switch is in the OFF position.
  2. Unplug or cut power. Unplug the disposal from under the sink, or flip the breaker if it's hardwired. Never work on a disposal with power connected.
  3. Check for visible obstructions. Shine a flashlight into the disposal opening. Use tongs or pliers to remove any foreign objects—silverware, bones, bottle caps, or fibrous food debris. Never reach in with your hand.
  4. Manually unjam the flywheel. Insert the Allen wrench into the hex socket on the bottom center of the disposal (under the sink). Work the wrench back and forth vigorously for 10–15 seconds until the flywheel moves freely.
  5. Press the reset button. Look for the small red or black reset button on the bottom of the disposal housing (near where the power cord enters). If it's popped out about 1/4 inch, press it firmly until it clicks and stays in. This resets the internal circuit breaker.
  6. Clear the grinding chamber. From above, use tongs to verify the impellers move freely. Spin them a full 360 degrees to ensure nothing is jammed.
  7. Restore power. Plug the disposal back in or flip the breaker on.
  8. Run cold water and test. Turn on cold water, then flip the disposal switch. It should spin up normally. Let it run for 30 seconds with water to flush any remaining particles.

Pro Tips

Tip 1: Always run cold water (not hot) while using the disposal. Cold water keeps fats solidified so they get chopped and flushed away rather than coating the pipes.
Tip 2: If the disposal trips repeatedly, the motor may be failing. A 10+ year old disposal that needs frequent resets is nearing end of life.
Tip 3: If the disposal won't spin even after manual unjamming, use a wooden broom handle from above as leverage to rotate the impellers—this gives more torque than the Allen wrench.
Tip 4: Never pour chemical drain cleaners into a disposal. If it doesn't drain, the disposal is separate from the clog—the issue is in the P-trap or drain line beyond.
