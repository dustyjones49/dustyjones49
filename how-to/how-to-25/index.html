How to Texture a Ceiling

Overview

Ceiling texture hides imperfections, dampens sound, and adds visual interest. The three most common textures are orange peel (sprayed fine droplets), knockdown (sprayed and troweled flat), and popcorn (heavy aggregate, now less popular). A hopper gun and air compressor make the job manageable. A 12x12-foot ceiling takes about 2 hours to texture. Cover absolutely everything—overspray is unavoidable.

Tools & Materials

  • Drywall texture hopper gun
  • Air compressor (minimum 5 CFM at 90 PSI)
  • Joint compound (thinned) or premixed texture compound
  • 18-inch or 24-inch knockdown knife (for knockdown finish)
  • Drill with mixing paddle & 5-gallon bucket
  • Plastic sheeting, drop cloths, painter's tape
  • Respirator, goggles, hat or hood
  • Extension pole for hopper gun

Step-by-Step

  1. Prep the room. Cover the floor, walls, and any remaining fixtures with plastic sheeting. Tape the plastic to the top of the walls, letting it drape down. Wear head-to-toe protection—texture splatters everywhere.
  2. Mix the texture compound. Thin joint compound with water until it reaches pancake-batter consistency. For orange peel, it should be thinner (like heavy cream). For knockdown, slightly thicker (like yogurt). Strain the mix through a mesh screen if using bucket mud to remove dried lumps.
  3. Set up the hopper gun. Fill the hopper about halfway. Connect to an air compressor set to 30–40 PSI. Test on a scrap piece of drywall or cardboard. Adjust the nozzle orifice and air pressure until you get the droplet size you want.
  4. Spray orange peel texture. Hold the gun about 18–24 inches from the ceiling. Spray in a steady, overlapping pattern, moving from one side of the room to the other. Apply in one pass, then make a second pass in the perpendicular direction for uniform coverage.
  5. For knockdown texture. Spray the texture at a slightly heavier rate—droplets should be about the size of a dime. Wait 5–15 minutes until the sprayed droplets lose their sheen and start to set (the slurry turns matte). Then lightly drag a clean knockdown knife across the surface, flattening the peaks into a mottled, stucco-like pattern. Wipe the knife clean after each pass.
  6. Work in manageable sections. For knockdown, spray a 4x4-foot section, then knock it down before moving to the next area. If the texture dries too much, the knife will not flatten it and the result will be lumpy.
  7. Let the texture dry completely. Allow 24 hours for the texture to dry. The wet look will turn to flat white. Do not paint or prime wet texture—it will smear.
  8. Prime before painting. Always apply a coat of PVA drywall primer before painting texture. The porous compound will absorb paint unevenly, leaving a blotchy finish. Without primer, the first coat of paint will practically disappear.

Tips

Tip 1: Practice on a large piece of cardboard or scrap drywall before touching the ceiling. Getting the spray pattern right takes a few tries.
Tip 2: For a consistent knockdown pattern across the whole ceiling, have two people: one sprays, the other follows behind with the knockdown knife.
Tip 3: If you are covering an existing popcorn ceiling, test it for asbestos if the house was built before 1980. Do not scrape without testing first.
Tip 4: Use a pancake compressor for small rooms but be prepared for the motor to run almost constantly—a larger tank compressor gives more consistent spray.
Tip 5: Do not try to texture a ceiling with aerosol cans from the hardware store. They produce an uneven, amateur-looking stipple that is impossible to match for patches.
