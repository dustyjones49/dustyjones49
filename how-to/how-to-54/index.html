How to Replace Door Hinges

Overview

Replacing door hinges refreshes the look, eliminates squeaks that lubrication can't fix, and restores proper door alignment. Swapping hinges one at a time means you won't need to remove the door.

Tools & Materials

  • New hinges (match size, corner radius, and screw hole pattern)
  • Phillips-head screwdriver or drill with driver bit
  • 2-inch screws for reinforcement
  • Utility knife
  • Wood filler or toothpicks and wood glue
  • Door shims or wedge
  • Pencil

Step-by-Step

  1. Inspect and measure old hinges. Note the size (usually 3-1/2" or 4"), corner type (square or rounded), and screw hole spacing. Purchase exact replacements.
  2. Support the door. Wedge a shim or wood block under the door to hold its position while you work.
  3. Replace one hinge at a time. Start with the bottom hinge. Unscrew it from both the door and the jamb, remove it, and immediately install the new hinge using the same screw holes.
  4. Tighten evenly. Drive screws in loosely at first. Once all screws are started, tighten them for a flush fit.
  5. Move to the middle hinge. Repeat the process, then finish with the top hinge.
  6. Check door alignment. Open and close the door to confirm it swings freely and latches. Adjust any tight screws.
  7. Fill old holes if needed. If new hinge holes don't match the old ones, fill old holes with wood filler or glue-dipped toothpicks and let dry before drilling new pilot holes.
  8. Touch up around hinges. If old hinges left unpainted rings around the plates, touch up with matching paint.

Pro Tips

Tip 1: Always replace hinges with the door closed and supported. This prevents the weight of the door from pulling on the remaining hinges.
Tip 2: Use a self-centering drill bit when drilling new pilot holes to keep screws perfectly centered in the hinge leaf holes.
Tip 3: Swap ordinary screws for 2-inch screws in the top hinge jamb side to prevent future sagging.
Tip 4: Lightly coat hinge pins with petroleum jelly or white lithium grease before inserting for smooth, squeak-free operation.
Tip 5: Trace around old hinges with a utility knife before removal to avoid peeling paint when the hinge lifts off.
