How to Clean AC Condenser Coils

Overview

Dirty condenser coils force your AC to work harder, driving up energy bills and shortening the unit's life. Cleaning the outdoor unit's coils annually keeps your system running at peak efficiency and can cut cooling costs by up to 15%.

Tools & Materials

  • Garden hose with spray nozzle
  • Coil cleaner spray (foaming, AC-specific)
  • Screwdriver or nut driver set
  • Soft-bristle brush
  • Fin comb or fin straightening tool
  • Shop vacuum
  • Work gloves
  • Safety glasses

Step-by-Step

  1. Turn off power. Shut off the AC at the thermostat, then flip the dedicated breaker in your electrical panel to OFF. Some units also have a disconnect switch near the outdoor unit—pull that too.
  2. Clear debris around the unit. Remove leaves, grass clippings, mulch, and vegetation within 2 feet of the condenser. Trim back bushes or plants that crowd the unit.
  3. Remove the outer casing. Unscrew and lift off the top grille and/or side panels. Most units have screws around the perimeter. Set screws aside where they won't get lost.
  4. Vacuum loose debris. Use a shop vac to remove leaves, seeds, and dirt from the bottom pan and interior of the unit. Don't touch the fan blades.
  5. Spray on coil cleaner. Apply a foaming AC coil cleaner to the coils, following the product instructions. The foam lifts embedded dirt. Let it soak for 5–10 minutes but don't let it dry.
  6. Rinse with a hose. Spray the coils from the inside out with a garden hose on moderate pressure. Spraying from inside pushes debris back out the way it entered. Avoid using a pressure washer—high pressure bends the delicate fins.
  7. Straighten bent fins. Use a fin comb to carefully straighten any bent or flattened aluminum fins. Work gently; the fins bend easily.
  8. Reassemble the unit. Replace the side panels and top grille. Tighten all screws.
  9. Restore power and test. Flip the breaker on, reinsert the disconnect, and turn the thermostat back to cooling. Confirm the fan spins freely and the compressor runs.

Pro Tips

Tip 1: Clean coils in spring before cooling season starts. Dirty coils can raise head pressure enough to trip the compressor's thermal overload.
Tip 2: Don't use household cleaners or bleach on coils. AC coil cleaner is formulated to rinse clean without leaving corrosive residue.
Tip 3: If the unit has two rows of coils, separate them gently to clean between the rows. Dirt packed between coil layers is a major airflow killer.
Tip 4: While the unit is open, pour a cup of bleach into any visible drain pan to clear algae and prevent clogged condensate drains.
