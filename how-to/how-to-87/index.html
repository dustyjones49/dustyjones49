How to Remove Carpet Stains

Overview

Most carpet stains can be removed with the right technique if treated promptly. The universal rule is: blot, don't rub. Rubbing pushes the stain deeper into carpet fibers and spreads it sideways. This guide covers the most common household carpet stains.

Tools & Materials

  • Clean white cloths or paper towels (white only—colored cloths can transfer dye)
  • White vinegar
  • Baking soda
  • Hydrogen peroxide (3%)
  • Dish soap (clear, non-moisturizing)
  • Club soda
  • Enzymatic pet stain cleaner (for pet urine)
  • Spray bottle
  • Wet/dry vacuum or carpet cleaner
  • Soft-bristle brush
  • Plastic scraper or dull knife

Step-by-Step

  1. Act immediately. The faster you treat a stain, the less likely it will set. Lift any solids with a dull knife or scraper. Blot (don't rub) as much liquid as possible with a clean, dry white cloth.
  2. Blot with cold water. Dampen a white cloth with cold water and blot the stain repeatedly. Hot water sets many stains, especially protein-based ones like blood, milk, and pet urine.
  3. Apply the cleaning solution. For general stains: mix 1 tablespoon dish soap + 1 tablespoon white vinegar + 2 cups warm water in a spray bottle. Spray the stain and blot. Repeat until no more color transfers to the cloth.
  4. Red wine and coffee: Blot excess, then apply club soda and blot. If the stain persists, mix 1 part hydrogen peroxide with 2 parts water, apply sparingly, and blot. Hydrogen peroxide can lighten dark carpets—test on a hidden area first.
  5. Pet urine: Blot up as much as possible. Apply an enzymatic cleaner (not just soap and water). Enzymes break down the uric acid crystals that cause odor. Saturate the area and let it air-dry slowly—the enzymes need time to work.
  6. Grease and oil: Sprinkle baking soda or cornstarch liberally on the stain. Let it sit for 30 minutes to absorb the oil, then vacuum. Apply a drop of dish soap (which cuts grease) to the spot, rub gently with your fingertips, and blot with a damp cloth.
  7. Mud: Let mud dry completely first—wet mud smears. Once dry, vacuum thoroughly, then blot with soap and water solution.
  8. Rinse the area. After the stain lifts, blot the area with plain cold water to remove any cleaning solution residue. Soap residue left in the carpet attracts dirt and causes rapid re-soiling.
  9. Dry thoroughly. Blot the area dry with a clean towel. Place a folded dry towel over the spot, weigh it down with a heavy object, and leave overnight. Residual moisture in the carpet pad causes mildew.

Pro Tips

Tip 1: Always test any cleaning solution on an inconspicuous area of carpet (corner of a closet) to check for colorfastness before treating a visible stain.
Tip 2: Work from the outside of the stain inward when blotting. This prevents the stain from spreading to a larger area.
Tip 3: Never use laundry detergent on carpet. It foams excessively and is almost impossible to rinse out completely.
Tip 4: If a carpet stain reappears after cleaning (wicking back), the carpet pad underneath is saturated. Use a wet/dry vacuum to extract from the pad, or call a professional with a truck-mounted extractor.
Tip 5: For set-in stains that don't respond to DIY methods, rent a carpet cleaning machine with hot water extraction before giving up. Steam and suction can lift stains that blotting can't reach.
