How to Unclog a Toilet

Overview

A clogged toilet is a plumbing emergency you can often resolve yourself in 5–15 minutes. The key is using the right tool for the job: a flange plunger (not a sink plunger) for most clogs, and a toilet auger (closet auger) for stubborn blockages. Never use chemical drain cleaners in a toilet—they generate heat that can crack the porcelain bowl. If water is rising toward the rim, quickly lift the tank lid and push the flapper down to stop the flow.

Tools & Materials

  • Flange plunger (with the extended rubber lip on the bottom)
  • Toilet auger (closet auger) with rubber bowl guard
  • Rubber gloves (long-cuff)
  • Old towels or rags (for drips and splashes)
  • Bucket
  • Dish soap (degreasing power helps break up organic clogs)
  • Hot (but not boiling) water
  • Wet/dry shop vac (for water removal if needed)

Step-by-Step

  1. Stop the water if the bowl is full. If water is near the rim, remove the tank lid and press the flapper down to stop more water from entering. If the valve is slowly leaking, turn off the shut-off valve behind the toilet.
  2. Try the dish soap and hot water method first. Squirt 3–4 tablespoons of dish soap into the bowl. Slowly pour a bucket of hot (not boiling—boiling water can crack porcelain) water from about waist height. The soap lubricates the clog and the water pressure can push it through. Wait 10 minutes.
  3. Use a flange plunger correctly. If the bowl water is low, add water to cover the plunger cup. Place the plunger into the bowl at an angle to fill the cup with water (not air). Fit the flange into the drain opening. Push down gently to create a seal, then plunge vigorously with an up-and-down motion for 15–20 seconds. The push stroke is gentle; the pull stroke creates the suction that dislodges the clog.
  4. Test the flush. After plunging, slowly pour a bucket of water into the bowl to test. If the water drains normally, the clog is cleared. Flush the toilet. If the water rises or drains slowly, repeat plunging or move to the auger.
  5. Use a toilet auger. Insert the auger cable into the drain opening while cranking the handle. The rubber guard protects the porcelain. Crank until you feel resistance—this is the clog. Keep cranking while applying gentle forward pressure to break through or hook the obstruction.
  6. Retrieve or clear the clog. If the auger hooks something (a toy, comb, or dental floss container), crank in reverse while slowly pulling the cable back. Have a trash bag ready. If the auger simply breaks the clog, you will feel the resistance suddenly give way.
  7. Flush and test. Flush toilet paper only. If the toilet still drains slowly, the clog may be in the main drain line (affecting multiple fixtures)—you need a plumber with a main-line snake. If it flushes normally, clean up and the job is done.

Tips

Tip 1: The flange on the bottom of a toilet plunger folds out; do not push it back in. Buy a flange plunger—cup plungers for sinks will not create a proper seal in a toilet drain.
Tip 2: Emergency prevention: if you flush and see water rising, lift the tank lid and push the flapper down rapidly. This prevents overflow in 90% of cases.
Tip 3: Never pour Drano or similar chemical drain cleaners into a toilet. They do not work on toilet clogs and the exothermic reaction can crack the bowl or damage the wax ring.
Tip 4: A toilet auger ($25–$40) is a one-time purchase that pays for itself the first time you avoid calling a plumber for a clog. Keep one in every bathroom.
Tip 5: If the toilet gurgles or bubbles when you run a sink or flush another toilet, the main drain vent is blocked or the septic system is full—call a plumber.
