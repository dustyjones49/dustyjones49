How to Install a Storm Door

Overview

A storm door adds insulation, ventilation, and a layer of security to your entryway. Most pre-hung storm doors are designed for DIY installation and come with a Z-bar mounting system that simplifies the process.

Tools & Materials

  • Storm door kit (door panel, Z-bars, closer, sweep, hardware)
  • Power drill with screwdriver bits
  • Level (2-foot)
  • Measuring tape
  • Hack saw (to trim Z-bars)
  • Drill bits
  • Caulk and caulk gun
  • Shims
  • Hacksaw
  • Safety glasses

Step-by-Step

  1. Check the opening. Measure the width and height of the door opening. Verify it is square with a level. Storm doors fit most standard openings (32", 34", or 36" wide).
  2. Assemble the Z-bars. Slide the hinge-side and latch-side Z-bars onto the door panel sides. These metal channels clamp the door to the frame.
  3. Drill the drain holes. The bottom expander sweep includes small drain holes. Make sure these face outward so water drains away from the house.
  4. Position the door in the opening. Set the assembled door and Z-bars into the rough opening. Use shims under the door panel to raise it so the bottom sweep clears the threshold.
  5. Plumb and level the hinge side. Place a level on the hinge-side Z-bar. Adjust the door until it is perfectly plumb, then drill pilot holes and screw the hinge Z-bar to the door jamb.
  6. Secure the latch-side Z-bar. With the door closed, align the latch-side Z-bar so there is an even 1/8" gap between the door and the Z-bar along its entire length. Screw it to the jamb.
  7. Attach the top Z-bar. Slide the top Z-bar down between the side bars. It should rest snugly on top of both. Screw it in place.
  8. Install the door closer. Mount the closer bracket to the door jamb at the top, then attach the closer cylinder to the door panel. Adjust the closing speed with the screw on the cylinder end.
  9. Attach the handle and sweep. Install the door handle and deadbolt per the manufacturer's instructions. Adjust the bottom sweep for a snug seal against the threshold.
  10. Caulk the exterior. Run a bead of exterior-grade caulk along the outside edge of each Z-bar to waterproof the installation.

Pro Tips

Tip 1: Install the door closer on the same side as the primary entry door handle so the two don't interfere.
Tip 2: If you have an out-swing entry door, you'll need a storm door designed for an out-swing installation.
Tip 3: Use a helper. Storm doors are awkward to hold while aligning and screwing.
Tip 4: Don't overtighten the Z-bar screws—this can bow the metal and cause the door to bind.
