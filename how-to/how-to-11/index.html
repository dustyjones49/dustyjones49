How to Paint Kitchen Cabinets

Overview

Painting kitchen cabinets costs a fraction of replacing them and can completely transform your kitchen. This is not a weekend project—expect to spend 4–5 days for proper results. The secret is meticulous prep: the paint job is only as good as the surface underneath. Remove all doors and hardware before starting.

Tools & Materials

  • High-quality bonding primer (oil-based or shellac)
  • Alkyd or urethane-enamel paint (Benjamin Moore Advance or similar)
  • 4-inch foam roller with high-density covers
  • High-quality angled brush (2½-inch)
  • TSP (trisodium phosphate) or Krud Kutter deglosser
  • 120-grit and 220-grit sandpaper or sanding sponges
  • Orbital sander (optional but recommended)
  • Painter's pyramids or screws for propping doors
  • Wood filler and putty knife

Step-by-Step

  1. Remove doors, drawers, and hardware. Label every door and drawer with numbered painter's tape so you know where each goes back. Remove all hinges, knobs, and pulls. Store screws in labeled bags.
  2. Clean thoroughly. Scrub all surfaces with TSP or a heavy-duty degreaser. Kitchen cabinets accumulate years of cooking grease that paint will not adhere to. Rinse with clean water and let dry completely.
  3. Sand every surface. Sand doors, drawer fronts, and cabinet frames with 120-grit sandpaper or an orbital sander. You are not sanding to bare wood—you are deglossing and creating a profile for primer to grip. Wipe all dust with a tack cloth.
  4. Fill imperfections. Fill dents, deep scratches, and old hardware holes with wood filler. Let dry completely, then sand smooth with 220-grit. Vacuum and tack-cloth everything again.
  5. Apply bonding primer. Prime all surfaces with a high-quality bonding primer. Oil-based or shellac primers block tannins from wood and old stains. Use a foam roller on flat panels and a brush on detailed profiles. Apply one even coat. Let dry overnight.
  6. Sand primer smooth. Lightly sand all primed surfaces with 220-grit sandpaper or a fine sanding sponge. This levels any raised grain and brush marks. Wipe with a tack cloth. The surface should feel glass-smooth.
  7. Apply the first finish coat. Use an alkyd or urethane-enamel paint. These self-level and cure to a hard, furniture-like finish. Lay doors flat on painter's pyramids. Roll flat panels with a foam roller; use the brush for detailed areas. Apply thin, even coats.
  8. Sand between coats. After the first coat dries (usually 16–24 hours for alkyd), sand lightly with 320-grit or a fine sanding sponge. Wipe clean. Apply the second coat. A third coat may be needed for a dark color over a light primer.
  9. Let cure fully before reinstalling. Alkyd paint dries to the touch in hours but takes 5–7 days to fully cure. Do not rush reinstalling—uncured paint is soft and will chip or smear when bumped.

Tips

Tip 1: Never use standard latex wall paint on cabinets—it stays soft, blocks together, and peels within months.
Tip 2: A sprayer gives the best factory finish, but a foam roller with alkyd paint comes close for DIYers without a spray booth.
Tip 3: Shellac primer (like Zinsser BIN) dries in 30 minutes and is the ultimate stain blocker—but it smells intensely, so ventilate well.
Tip 4: Number doors and drawers with tape on the back edge, not the face, so numbers are invisible after reinstallation.
Tip 5: Install soft-close hinges while the doors are off—they cost about $3 each and add a high-end feel to your refreshed cabinets.
