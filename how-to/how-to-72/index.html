How to Clean a Dishwasher Filter

Overview

A clogged dishwasher filter leads to dirty dishes, unpleasant odors, and standing water in the bottom of the tub. Cleaning the filter takes 5 minutes and should be done monthly. Most modern dishwashers have a removable cylindrical filter assembly in the bottom.

Tools & Materials

  • Soft-bristle brush or old toothbrush
  • Dish soap
  • Warm water
  • Paper towel or sponge
  • Flashlight (optional)

Step-by-Step

  1. Empty the dishwasher. Pull out the bottom rack completely for clear access to the filter.
  2. Remove standing water. If water is pooled in the bottom, soak it up with a sponge or paper towels so you can see the filter clearly.
  3. Locate the filter. The filter is on the tub floor, usually beneath the spray arm. It typically has a cylindrical top that twists off and a flat mesh screen underneath.
  4. Turn and remove the upper filter. Twist the cylindrical portion counterclockwise (usually a quarter-turn) and lift it out.
  5. Lift out the lower filter. Beneath the cylinder, there's usually a flat mesh filter plate. Lift it straight up.
  6. Rinse and scrub. Rinse both filter pieces under warm running water. Use a soft brush with dish soap to scrub away grease, food particles, and any mineral deposits. Don't use abrasive pads—they can damage the mesh.
  7. Clean the filter housing. Wipe out the filter cavity in the dishwasher tub with a damp paper towel. Remove any debris from the sump area.
  8. Reinstall the filters. Set the lower filter in place, then insert the upper filter and twist clockwise to lock it. Ensure it's seated snugly—an improperly installed filter lets debris bypass it.
  9. Run a cleaning cycle. Place a dishwasher cleaner tablet or a cup of white vinegar on the top rack and run an empty hot cycle to flush the system.

Pro Tips

Tip 1: If dishes come out with gritty residue, the filter is likely overwhelmed. Clean it immediately.
Tip 2: Scrape plates before loading but don't pre-rinse. Modern dishwasher detergents need food particles to work properly—the enzymes need something to react with.
Tip 3: Check your owner's manual—some older dishwashers use a hard-food disposer (grinder) instead of a removable filter and don't require manual filter cleaning.
Tip 4: If the filter has mold or mildew spots, soak it in a 50/50 vinegar and water solution for 15 minutes, then scrub.
