How to Stain a Deck

Overview

Staining a deck protects the wood from UV damage, moisture, and rot while enhancing its natural color. A deck should be stained every 2–3 years. This is a two-day project: one day to clean and dry, one day to stain. The most common mistake is staining damp wood—moisture in the wood prevents stain absorption.

Tools & Materials

  • Deck stain (oil-based penetrating or water-based solid)
  • Deck cleaner/brightener (oxalic acid-based)
  • Pressure washer or stiff-bristle push broom
  • Pump sprayer or 4-inch stain pad on a pole
  • Paint brush (3-inch for cutting in)
  • Safety glasses, rubber gloves, and knee pads
  • Drop cloths and painter's tape
  • Orbital sander with 60-grit (if sanding is needed)

Step-by-Step

  1. Inspect and repair the deck. Hammer down protruding nails or replace with deck screws. Replace any rotted or split boards. Sand any heavily splintered areas with 60-grit sandpaper.
  2. Clear the deck. Remove all furniture, planters, and grills. Sweep thoroughly. Cover plants and siding with plastic sheeting to protect them from cleaner and stain overspray.
  3. Apply deck cleaner. Mix deck cleaner per instructions and apply with a pump sprayer or roller. A brightener with oxalic acid removes gray weathered wood fibers and opens the grain. Scrub with a stiff brush. Let dwell 10–15 minutes.
  4. Power wash or rinse. If pressure washing, use a fan-tip nozzle at 500–800 PSI and hold the wand at least 12 inches from the wood. Too much pressure fuzzes the wood grain. Rinse thoroughly with a garden hose until all cleaner residue is gone.
  5. Let the deck dry thoroughly. Wait at least 48 hours after washing before staining. Test moisture by sprinkling water—if it beads up, the wood is too wet. Water should absorb within seconds on dry wood. Direct sun helps speed drying.
  6. Stir stain thoroughly. Oil-based stain settles. Stir with a stick frequently during application—every 10–15 minutes. Do not shake; shaking introduces air bubbles that prevent even penetration.
  7. Cut in edges. Use a brush to stain around posts, railings, and where decking meets the house. Brush stain into end grain of boards (cut ends absorb triple the stain). Do not let brush marks lap onto already-stained areas.
  8. Apply stain to the deck surface. Using a stain pad or pump sprayer, apply stain evenly to 2–3 boards at a time, running the full length of each board. Do not stop in the middle of a board—lap marks are nearly impossible to fix once the stain dries.
  9. Wipe excess stain. After 15–20 minutes, wipe any pooling stain with a rag. Oil-based stain must penetrate—excess that sits on the surface becomes a sticky, peeling mess. Back-brush any areas that look too heavy.

Tips

Tip 1: Never stain a deck in direct hot sun—the stain will flash-dry before penetrating, leaving a blotchy finish.
Tip 2: Oil-based penetrating stains last longer than water-based and allow the wood to breathe—critical for preventing rot.
Tip 3: The water-drop test is the only reliable way to check if wood is dry enough to stain. Do not trust touch—the surface can feel dry while the inside is still saturated.
Tip 4: Stain railings and spindles first, then the deck surface. This way, any drips onto the deck floor get covered when you stain the floor last.
Tip 5: Dispose of oil-soaked rags in a sealed metal can filled with water. Spontaneous combustion from drying oils is a real fire hazard.
