How to Install a Deadbolt Lock

Overview

Adding a deadbolt is one of the most effective ways to improve home security. This guide walks through installing a new deadbolt on a door that already has a knob set—either replacing an old deadbolt or cutting fresh holes in a new door.

Tools & Materials

  • Deadbolt lock kit
  • Hole saw kit (2-1/8" and 1" bits)
  • Power drill
  • Phillips-head screwdriver
  • 1-inch wood chisel
  • Hammer
  • Pencil and measuring tape
  • Safety glasses

Step-by-Step

  1. Choose the deadbolt height. Standard deadbolt placement is 6 to 12 inches above the door knob. Mark the centerline on the door face and edge at your chosen height.
  2. Drill the face hole. Using a 2-1/8" hole saw, drill through the door face at your marked center point. Drill from one side until the pilot bit exits, then finish from the opposite side to prevent splintering.
  3. Drill the edge hole. Switch to a 1" spade or hole saw bit. Drill into the door edge at the same center mark, going straight in until you meet the face hole.
  4. Chisel the strike plate mortise. Insert the latch into the edge hole and trace around the faceplate. Chisel out a recess so the plate sits flush with the door edge. Screw it in place.
  5. Assemble the deadbolt halves. Feed the exterior keyed cylinder through the face hole, aligning the tailpiece with the slot in the latch. Fit the interior thumb-turn piece over the tailpiece.
  6. Screw the halves together. Thread the included mounting screws through the interior plate into the exterior cylinder posts. Tighten until snug but not overtightened.
  7. Mark the strike plate on the jamb. Close the door and mark where the deadbolt bolt hits the door jamb. Outline the strike plate, then chisel a mortise so it sits flush.
  8. Drill the bolt recess. Drill a 1" hole about 1" deep into the jamb at the center of your strike plate outline so the bolt can fully extend.
  9. Screw in the strike plate. Secure the strike plate to the jamb with the provided long screws—these should reach into the framing stud for maximum security.
  10. Test the deadbolt. With the door open, turn the thumb-turn and key to confirm smooth operation. Close the door and verify the bolt extends fully into the jamb.

Pro Tips

Tip 1: Use a door lock installation jig (available at hardware stores) to ensure perfectly aligned holes every time.
Tip 2: Replace the short strike plate screws with 3-inch screws that bite into the wall stud behind the jamb for kick-in resistance.
Tip 3: Apply masking tape over the drilling area to reduce wood splintering when the hole saw exits.
Tip 4: If retrofitting, hold the lock body against the door and double-check that the tailpiece slot aligns with the latch before drilling.
