How to Caulk Windows

Overview

Caulking windows prevents air leaks that drive up heating and cooling costs and stops water intrusion that causes rot and mold. You should caulk both the exterior (where the window frame meets the siding) and interior (where the frame meets the drywall).

Tools & Materials

  • Caulk (exterior: silicone or polyurethane; interior: acrylic latex)
  • Caulk gun
  • Utility knife
  • Putty knife or 5-in-1 tool
  • Backer rod (for gaps wider than 1/4")
  • Rubbing alcohol and rag
  • Masking tape
  • Gloves

Step-by-Step

  1. Remove old caulk. Cut away dried, peeling caulk with a utility knife. Use a putty knife to scrape residue from both surfaces. Pull out any loose chunks.
  2. Clean the gap. Vacuum or brush out loose debris. Wipe both sides of the joint with rubbing alcohol to remove dust and oil. The caulk must bond to clean, dry surfaces.
  3. Fill large gaps. If the gap is wider than 1/4", press foam backer rod into the gap before caulking. This provides a base for the caulk and prevents it from sinking into the void.
  4. Tape the edges. Apply masking tape to both sides of the joint, 1/8" away from the gap, to create a sharp, clean bead line. This is optional but gives a pro look.
  5. Cut the caulk tube tip. Cut the nozzle at a 45-degree angle, with the opening sized slightly smaller than the gap you're filling. Puncture the inner foil seal with the poker on the gun.
  6. Apply the caulk bead. Hold the gun at a 45-degree angle and pull it along the joint at a steady pace. Push the bead (don't pull it). The bead should fill the gap without being excessive.
  7. Tool the bead. Immediately after applying, smooth the bead with a wet finger or a caulk-smoothing tool. Press the caulk into the gap and create a concave profile that sheds water.
  8. Remove the tape. Peel masking tape away while the caulk is still wet, pulling at a 45-degree angle away from the bead.
  9. Let it cure. Don't touch or paint the caulk until it's fully cured. Check the label—silicone cures in 24 hours, acrylic latex in 2–4 hours.

Pro Tips

Tip 1: Use 100% silicone for exterior window caulking—it's waterproof and stays flexible for 20+ years. Use paintable acrylic latex caulk indoors.
Tip 2: Caulk on a dry day above 40°F. Cold, damp surfaces prevent proper adhesion.
Tip 3: Never caulk the bottom of exterior window trim—the weep holes need to remain open to drain condensation and incidental water.
Tip 4: Keep a cup of water and paper towels nearby for tooling and cleanup. Silicone gets everywhere if you're not careful.
