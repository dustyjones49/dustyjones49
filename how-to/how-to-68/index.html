How to Bleed a Radiator

Overview

If your radiator is cold at the top but warm at the bottom, trapped air is blocking hot water from circulating. Bleeding releases the air and restores full heat output. This is routine maintenance for both hot water and steam radiator systems.

Tools & Materials

  • Radiator bleed key (or flat-head screwdriver for some models)
  • Small bowl or cup
  • Old rag or towel
  • Work gloves (radiator surfaces may be hot)

Step-by-Step

  1. Turn on the heating. Run the heating system for at least 30 minutes so the pump circulates and air collects at the top of radiators.
  2. Check which radiators need bleeding. Feel each radiator from bottom to top. Cold spots at the top indicate trapped air. Start with the lowest radiator in the house and work upward.
  3. Turn off the heating. Switch the system off and wait 10–15 minutes for the water to settle. This prevents the pump from drawing more air in while you bleed.
  4. Locate the bleed valve. Look for a small square or slotted fitting at the top of one side of the radiator. It's usually on the opposite end from the supply pipe.
  5. Position the bowl and rag. Hold a bowl under the bleed valve and wrap a rag around the area to catch drips. Radiator water is often rusty and can stain.
  6. Open the bleed valve. Insert the bleed key and turn it counterclockwise slowly—about a quarter to half turn. You'll hear a hissing sound as air escapes.
  7. Wait for water. Keep the valve open until a steady stream of water flows out with no air sputtering. The hissing will stop when the air is fully purged.
  8. Close the valve. Turn the bleed key clockwise until snug. Don't overtighten—you can strip the brass threads.
  9. Check the boiler pressure. After bleeding, check the pressure gauge on your boiler. It should be between 1.0 and 1.5 bar (cold). If pressure dropped, top up the system via the filling loop.
  10. Turn heating back on. Restart the system and check that previously cold radiators now heat evenly from top to bottom.

Pro Tips

Tip 1: Bleed radiators at the start of every heating season—air accumulates during the summer off-period.
Tip 2: If you're bleeding radiators frequently (more than once per season), you may have a leak or a faulty expansion tank introducing air into the system. Call a technician.
Tip 3: For steam radiators (one-pipe systems), the air vent is an automatic device on the side. Replace it if the radiator stays cold—don't try to bleed steam radiators manually.
Tip 4: Keep the bleed key on a string tied to the radiator or near the boiler so you never lose it.
