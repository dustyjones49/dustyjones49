How to Sand Hardwood Floors

Overview

Sanding hardwood floors removes old finishes, scratches, and unevenness to prepare for staining and sealing. This is the most physically demanding step in floor refinishing. Rent a drum sander, edge sander, and buffer from a tool rental store. Budget a full day for sanding a 300-square-foot room.

Tools & Materials

  • Drum sander (rental) with 36, 60, 80, and 100-grit belts
  • Edge sander (orbital or disk) with matching grit discs
  • Floor buffer with 120-grit screen (for final pass)
  • Hammer and nail set
  • Shop vac with fine-dust filter & broom
  • Tack cloths & mineral spirits
  • Respirator (N95 minimum), safety glasses, knee pads, ear plugs
  • Wood filler (trowelable, compatible with your finish)

Step-by-Step

  1. Prep the room. Remove all furniture. Take doors off hinges. Cover vents with magnetic covers and seal doorways with plastic sheeting—the dust is extreme. Vacuum dirt and debris from the entire floor.
  2. Set all nails. Walk the room with a nail set and hammer. Sink every exposed nail head at least 1/8 inch below the surface. A nail caught by the drum sander tears the sanding belt and gouges the floor.
  3. Start with the drum sander at 36-grit. Tilt the sander back to keep the belt off the floor while starting. Lower slowly and walk forward at a steady pace. Move with the grain only. Overlap each pass by half the belt width. Lift the drum at the end of each pass before it gouges.
  4. Edge the room. Use the edge sander to sand 4–6 inches along every wall, under cabinets, and in closets. Start with the same grit as the drum sander. Feather into the drum-sanded area so there is no visible line between the two.
  5. Vacuum between grits. After the 36-grit pass, vacuum every square inch. Switch to 60-grit and repeat the drum and edge sanding process, again moving with the grain. Vacuum again.
  6. Finish with 80-grit and then 100-grit. Repeat the process with 80-grit to remove 60-grit scratches, then 100-grit for the final pass. The floor should feel smooth and uniform. Skipping a grit leaves visible scratches under the finish.
  7. Fill gaps and cracks. Mix trowelable wood filler with some of the sanding dust from the final pass. Trowel the filler into gaps and over nail holes while the floor is still dusty—the dust acts as pigment for a perfect match. Let dry.
  8. Buff the floor. Using the buffer with a 120-grit screen, go over the entire floor to blend the field and edges seamlessly and buff off dried filler. This also scuffs the wood for better stain absorption.
  9. Final vacuum and tack. Vacuum the room top to bottom—walls, windowsills, light fixtures, and floor. Wipe the entire floor with a tack cloth dampened with mineral spirits to pick up every particle of dust. Let dry completely before staining.

Tips

Tip 1: A drum sander can chew through 1/16 inch of wood in two seconds if you pause. Always keep the machine moving when the drum is in contact with the floor.
Tip 2: Rent the sander on a Friday evening and return Monday morning—rental places typically allow a one-day rate for weekend rentals.
Tip 3: Test that you have enough wood thickness to sand. Look in a floor vent or closet edge—you need at least 3/32 inch above the tongue.
Tip 4: Change sanding belts frequently. A dull belt generates excessive heat that burns the wood oils, leaving dark streaks impossible to remove.
Tip 5: Keep the cord over your shoulder while drum sanding. The heavy cord dragging across fresh sanding mars the surface.
