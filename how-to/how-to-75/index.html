How to Level a Washing Machine

Overview

An unlevel washer vibrates excessively, walks during spin cycles, and can damage floors, cabinets, and internal components. Leveling the machine takes 15 minutes and usually resolves most noise and shaking issues.

Tools & Materials

  • Carpenter's level (12" or longer)
  • Adjustable wrench or pliers
  • Anti-vibration pads (optional, for tile or hardwood floors)
  • Flashlight

Step-by-Step

  1. Empty the washer. Remove all clothes and drain any remaining water. An off-balance load gives a false reading.
  2. Check side-to-side level first. Place the level across the front of the machine, just behind the door or lid. The bubble should be centered between the lines.
  3. Check front-to-back level. Place the level along the side edge of the machine, oriented front to back. Again, the bubble should be centered.
  4. Locate the leveling feet. All washers have four adjustable feet—two in front, two in back. The front feet usually have visible adjustment threads. The back feet are often self-leveling but may have jam nuts.
  5. Adjust the front feet first. Use an adjustable wrench to turn the lock nut above the foot. Turn the foot clockwise to lower that corner or counterclockwise to raise it. Adjust the lower side up or the higher side down.
  6. Lock the jam nuts. Once the machine is level side-to-side, tighten the jam nuts against the machine base with the wrench. This prevents the feet from vibrating loose.
  7. Level front-to-back. If the back feet need adjustment, you'll need to tilt the machine back carefully (have a helper). Adjust the rear feet, then gently set the machine down and recheck.
  8. Recheck both axes. After all adjustments, recheck side-to-side and front-to-back level readings. Make small tweaks if needed.
  9. Rock test. Push down on each corner of the machine. None of the four corners should move or rock. All four feet must bear weight equally.
  10. Place anti-vibration pads. If the washer sits on a hard, slick floor (tile, vinyl, hardwood), place rubber anti-vibration pads under each foot to absorb vibration and prevent walking.

Pro Tips

Tip 1: Use a digital level or a phone level app if your carpenter's level is too long to fit on the machine's top surface.
Tip 2: If the floor itself isn't level, use shims under the washing machine feet. Don't shim the floor—the machine's solid base must transfer load directly.
Tip 3: Check leveling after moving the machine for any reason (cleaning, repairs, new flooring). Even 1/8" off can cause significant vibration at 1,000+ RPM spin speeds.
Tip 4: If the washer is level but still vibrates, the load is likely unbalanced. Pause, redistribute clothes, and retry. Also check that the shipping bolts were removed—a common mistake with new machines.
