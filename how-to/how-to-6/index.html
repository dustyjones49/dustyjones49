How to Caulk a Bathtub

Overview

Caulking a bathtub properly prevents water damage, mold, and expensive repairs. A clean caulk line also makes your bathroom look fresh and well-maintained. This project takes about 1—2 hours including drying time. Use 100% silicone caulk for bathrooms—it is waterproof and mold-resistant.

Tools & Materials

  • 100% silicone caulk (tub & tile formula)
  • Caulk removal tool or utility knife
  • Caulk gun
  • Isopropyl alcohol (70% or higher)
  • Painter's tape (blue)
  • Rag & paper towels
  • Plastic putty knife (not metal—scratches tub)
  • Bucket of warm soapy water
  • Hair dryer (optional)

Step-by-Step

  1. Remove the old caulk. Score along both edges of the old caulk bead with a utility knife. Use a caulk removal tool or plastic putty knife to peel it away. Use needle-nose pliers to pull stubborn strips.
  2. Deep clean the joint. Scrub the gap with a stiff brush and bleach-based cleaner to kill any mold. Rinse thoroughly and let dry completely. Use a hair dryer on low heat to speed drying—the surface must be bone-dry.
  3. Wipe with alcohol. Dampen a rag with isopropyl alcohol and wipe both surfaces of the joint. This removes residue and ensures maximum adhesion. Let it evaporate fully.
  4. Fill the tub with water. Fill the tub halfway with cold water before caulking. A full tub weighs hundreds of pounds and pulls the tub down from the tile, opening the gap. Caulking under this load prevents the seal from tearing later.
  5. Apply painter's tape. Run blue painter's tape along both edges of the joint, leaving the gap exposed. Keep the tape lines parallel and about 1/8 inch from the edge on each side for a clean, uniform bead.
  6. Cut the caulk tube and load the gun. Cut the nozzle at a 45-degree angle with an opening slightly smaller than the gap. Puncture the inner foil seal with the built-in poker on the gun. Place the tube in the gun.
  7. Apply the caulk. Hold the gun at a consistent 45-degree angle and push the bead while pulling the gun toward you. Move at a steady speed—don't stop and start. Overfill slightly so the bead bridges both surfaces fully.
  8. Tool the bead. Immediately wet your finger with soapy water and run it along the bead in one smooth pass, pressing caulk into the joint. Remove the painter's tape right away while caulk is wet, pulling away at a 45-degree angle.
  9. Let cure and drain. Do not touch the caulk. Wait at least 24 hours before using the tub. Drain the water only after the curing time has passed.

Tips

Tip 1: Silicone caulk cures with humidity—run a hot shower for 5 minutes after caulking to accelerate curing if the room is dry.
Tip 2: Never use acrylic latex caulk in a bathtub; it will crack and peel within months from constant water exposure and flexing.
Tip 3: If you have never caulked before, practice on a scrap cardboard corner first to get a feel for the gun and tooling technique.
Tip 4: A caulk smoothing tool (under $5 at any hardware store) gives a more consistent finish than a finger for silicone caulk, which is sticky.
Tip 5: Store leftover silicone caulk with a screw or nail in the nozzle tip to seal it—but expect to use it within a few weeks.
