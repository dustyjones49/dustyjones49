How to Repair Sprinkler Head

Overview

A broken sprinkler head wastes hundreds of gallons of water and creates dry spots in the lawn. Common failures include cracked housings, clogged nozzles, stuck pop-up risers, and stripped gears. Most repairs take under 30 minutes.

Tools & Materials

  • Replacement sprinkler head (match the make, model, and spray pattern)
  • Spare nozzles
  • Shovel or trowel
  • Adjustable wrench or pliers
  • Screwdriver (flat-head and Phillips)
  • Riser removal tool (optional, but saves time)
  • Teflon tape (plumber's tape)
  • Bucket of water (for cleaning)
  • Work gloves
  • Dirt or topsoil (for backfilling)

Step-by-Step

  1. Diagnose the problem. Turn on the zone manually at the controller. Watch for water gushing up (cracked housing), no pop-up (stuck riser or broken spring), uneven spray (clogged nozzle), or a geyser that doesn't rotate (stripped gears in rotary heads).
  2. Dig around the head. Use a trowel or small shovel to carefully dig a 3–4 inch wide circle around the sprinkler head, about 6 inches deep. Keep the dirt on a tarp or in a bucket to use for backfilling.
  3. Unscrew the old head. Grip the body of the sprinkler and unscrew it counterclockwise from the riser pipe below. If it's stuck, use an adjustable wrench on the body (not the pop-up stem). The riser pipe may try to unscrew from the fitting below—hold it steady.
  4. Clean the threads. Remove any old Teflon tape, dirt, or debris from the riser pipe threads. A clean connection prevents leaks.
  5. Wrap the threads with Teflon tape. Wrap 3–4 turns of Teflon tape clockwise around the riser pipe threads. This seals the connection.
  6. Screw on the new head. Thread the new sprinkler head onto the riser pipe by hand, turning clockwise. Hand-tighten until snug. Don't overtighten with tools—plastic threads strip easily.
  7. Flush the line (before installing nozzle). Before screwing in the filter and nozzle, turn on the zone for 15–30 seconds to blast dirt out of the line. Debris inside the line will instantly clog a new nozzle.
  8. Install the nozzle. Screw in the filter and nozzle. Adjust the spray pattern by turning the center screw or adjusting the arc ring—follow the manufacturer's instructions for your specific model.
  9. Backfill and compact. Pack soil firmly around the base of the sprinkler head. The top of the head should be flush with the soil line. Water the soil to settle it, then add more if needed.
  10. Test and adjust. Run the zone and observe the new head. Adjust the arc, radius, and spray direction as needed using the adjustment screw or key.

Pro Tips

Tip 1: Buy a bag of spare nozzles in varying spray patterns (quarter, half, full, and specialty strips). Swap a nozzle in 30 seconds instead of replacing the whole head.
Tip 2: Match the brand and model exactly when replacing rotary heads. Different brands have different gear ratios, precipitation rates, and nozzle threads. Mixing brands on one zone causes uneven watering.
Tip 3: Keep a riser extractor tool ($10) on hand. If the riser pipe breaks off below the sprinkler head, this tool grips the inside and lets you back it out without digging a giant hole.
Tip 4: If the same head keeps clogging, check the filter screen below the nozzle. If the screen is torn or missing, debris from the water line is entering the nozzle directly—replace the filter screen.
