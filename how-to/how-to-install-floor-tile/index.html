How to Install Floor Tile

Overview

Installing floor tile is a moderately difficult DIY project that requires patience and attention to subfloor preparation. For a typical 100 sq ft bathroom, allow 2—3 days: one day for prep and layout, one day for setting tile, and one day for grouting and sealing. The quality of the subfloor preparation determines how long the tile lasts — a bouncy or uneven subfloor guarantees cracked tile and grout.

Tools & Materials

  • Tile (add 10—15% for cuts and waste), thinset mortar (modified polymer-fortified)
  • Cement backer board or uncoupling membrane (Ditra), backer board screws
  • Notched trowel (1/4" × 3/8" square notch for most floor tile)
  • Wet saw or snap cutter, tile spacers, level, mixing paddle and drill
  • Grout float, grout sponge, buckets, knee pads

Step-by-Step

  1. Evaluate and prepare the subfloor: The subfloor must be flat to within 1/8" over 10' and rigid (L/360 deflection rating minimum). For plywood subfloors, add a cement backer board layer embedded in thinset and screwed every 8". For concrete, fill cracks and level with self-leveling compound if needed.
  2. Dry layout and find center: Snap chalk lines to find the center of the room in both directions. Do a dry layout from the center outward in both directions. Adjust so you have no sliver cuts (less than 2") at the walls — shift the layout by half a tile if needed. Mark the reference lines.
  3. Mix thinset: Mix thinset mortar per manufacturer's instructions — consistency should be like creamy peanut butter. Let it slake (rest) for 10 minutes, then remix. Only mix what you can use in 2—3 hours. Do not add extra water to extend working time.
  4. Spread thinset and set tile: Spread thinset with the flat side of the trowel to key it into the substrate, then comb with the notched side at a consistent 45-degree angle. Set tiles firmly, pressing with a slight twisting motion. Insert spacers as you go. Check level frequently across multiple tiles.
  5. Cut edge and obstacle tiles: Measure and mark tiles for cuts around doorways, pipes, and walls. Use a wet saw for straight cuts and an angle grinder or tile nipper for curves. Dry-fit each cut tile before mortaring it in place. Allow 1/4" expansion gap at all walls.
  6. Remove spacers and clean joints: After 24 hours, remove all spacers. Clean thinset from grout joints with a utility knife — mortar in the joints will show through grout. Vacuum all dust from joints.
  7. Grout the tile: Mix grout to a thick paste consistency. Spread diagonally across joints with a grout float, pressing firmly to pack joints fully. Remove excess with the float held at a 45-degree angle. Wait 15—30 minutes until grout haze forms, then wipe with a damp (not wet) sponge. Rinse sponge frequently.
  8. Seal and cure: After grout cures per manufacturer's directions (typically 48—72 hours), apply grout sealer. For natural stone tiles, seal the entire tile surface as well. Avoid heavy traffic for 7 days. Install door thresholds and baseboards to cover expansion gaps.

Tips & Warnings

  • Do not tile directly over a plywood subfloor without a backer board or membrane — wood movement will crack the tile and grout.
  • Back-butter large-format tiles (12×12 and larger) by spreading a thin layer of thinset on the tile back to ensure full coverage.
  • Always check level as you lay each tile. It's 10 times easier to adjust a tile while the thinset is wet than to replace it the next day.
  • Buy all tile from the same dye lot number — different lots can have slight color variations that become obvious once installed.
  • Allow expansion joints (caulked, not grouted) at all changes in plane and perimeter — walls, door thresholds, and floor-wall transitions.
