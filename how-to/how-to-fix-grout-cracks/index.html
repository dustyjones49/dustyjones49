How to Fix Cracked or Crumbling Grout

Overview

Cracked grout is both an eyesore and a water-intrusion risk, especially in showers and wet areas. Most grout cracks result from substrate movement, improper initial mix ratio, or using unsanded grout in joints that require sanded grout. Small repairs take about 30 minutes plus drying time. Widespread cracking across many tiles indicates subfloor flex and may require a full regrout or retiling.

Tools & Materials

  • Grout (sanded for joints over 1/8", unsanded for joints under 1/8"), color-matched to existing
  • Grout saw or oscillating multi-tool with grout removal blade
  • Vacuum (shop vac preferred), stiff brush, damp sponge, bucket
  • Grout float, utility knife, rubber gloves
  • Grout sealer (penetrating type, not film-forming)

Step-by-Step

  1. Inspect the damage: Lightly tap tiles around the cracked grout with a screwdriver handle. A hollow or rattling sound means the tile is loose — you'll need to reset the tile, not just regrout. If multiple tiles sound hollow across a large area, the substrate is failing and spot repair won't last.
  2. Remove old grout: Use a grout saw (manual carbide tool) or oscillating multi-tool with a diamond or carbide grout blade to remove all loose and cracked grout. Remove grout to a depth equal to the joint width, or at least 2/3 the depth of the tile for full joint removal. Be extremely careful not to chip the tile edges — the oscillating tool works better and faster than manual saws.
  3. Clean the joints: Vacuum all dust and debris from the joints thoroughly — any dust left in the joint will weaken the grout bond. Wipe joints with a damp (not wet) sponge to remove fine dust. Let joints dry completely before applying new grout — moisture compromises the bond.
  4. Mix and apply new grout: Mix grout to a peanut-butter consistency. For repairs, mix only what you can use in 15—20 minutes. For sanded grout, let it slake (rest) for 10 minutes then remix. Force grout into the cleaned joints with a grout float held at a 45-degree angle, overfilling slightly. Pack firmly — air pockets create weak spots that will crack again.
  5. Tool and clean: Wait 15—20 minutes until the grout surface stiffens but the joints are still impressionable. Use a damp (well-wrung) sponge to wipe excess grout from the tile faces and smooth the joints. Wipe in a diagonal direction so you don't drag grout out of the joints. Rinse the sponge after every pass. After 2—3 hours, buff the light haze off the tiles with a dry microfiber cloth.
  6. Cure and seal: Let the new grout cure for 48—72 hours (check manufacturer's instructions). Apply a penetrating grout sealer with a small brush or applicator bottle, wiping excess off the tile faces immediately. Two thin coats provide better protection than one thick coat. Re-seal grout annually in wet areas.

Tips & Warnings

  • The most common cause of recurring grout cracks is floor movement under tile. If cracks reappear, install a crack isolation membrane or replace the grout in that joint with color-matched sanded caulk instead — caulk allows movement without cracking.
  • At corners and changes in plane (wall-to-floor, tub-to-tile, bench edges), use color-matched sanded caulk instead of grout. Grout in these locations will always crack.
  • Buy pre-mixed grout for repairs under 10 linear feet — it eliminates mixing ratio errors that cause weak grout.
  • Regrout will often be a slightly different shade than existing grout due to age, wear, and sealer differences. Color-matched grout and a fresh sealer coat over the entire area minimizes the appearance of the repair.
