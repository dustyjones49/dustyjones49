How to Install a Pet Door

Overview

A pet door gives your dog or cat freedom to come and go while keeping weather and pests out. Installation in an exterior door takes 1–2 hours. You can also install one through a wall with a tunnel kit.

Tools & Materials

  • Pet door kit (size based on your pet's shoulder height)
  • Power drill with 3/8" bit
  • Jigsaw
  • Measuring tape and pencil
  • Masking tape
  • Screwdriver
  • Caulk and caulk gun (exterior-rated)
  • Level
  • Safety glasses

Step-by-Step

  1. Choose the right size. Measure your pet's shoulder height from floor to top of shoulders. The top of the flap opening should be at least 1 inch taller than this measurement. Measure pet width and add 2 inches.
  2. Position the template. Most kits include a cutting template. Tape it to the door at the correct height—usually 1 to 3 inches above the bottom rail of the door. Level the template.
  3. Drill starter holes. Drill a 3/8" hole inside each corner of the template outline so you can insert the jigsaw blade.
  4. Cut the opening. Use a jigsaw to cut along the template lines. Cut from the exterior side if the door has a smooth surface. Tape the cut line to reduce splintering.
  5. Test-fit the frame. Insert the inner and outer frame halves into the opening to confirm they fit. Trim the opening slightly if needed.
  6. Seal the edges. Apply a thin bead of exterior caulk around the inner edges of both frame halves where they contact the door surface.
  7. Assemble the frame. Press the exterior and interior frames together through the cutout. Drive the provided screws through the interior frame into the exterior frame posts. Tighten evenly.
  8. Attach the flap. Snap or slide the flexible flap into the frame, making sure it swings freely and seals against the inner lip.
  9. Caulk the exterior perimeter. Run a bead of caulk around the outside of the exterior frame to block water and drafts.

Pro Tips

Tip 1: For metal doors, use a bimetal jigsaw blade. For fiberglass, use a fine-tooth carbide blade to avoid chipping.
Tip 2: Never cut into the structural rails or stiles of a door—stay within the panel area. Cutting a rail weakens the door.
Tip 3: Train your pet with treats on both sides before expecting independent use. Prop the flap open for the first few days.
Tip 4: Choose a pet door with a rigid sliding security panel so you can lock it when not in use.
Tip 5: In cold climates, get a double-flap or insulated model to reduce heat loss.
