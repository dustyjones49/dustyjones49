How to Paint a Room

Overview

Painting a room is the most transformative DIY project for the least amount of money. A typical 12x12-foot room takes 4–6 hours of active work spread over a day including prep and dry time. The difference between an amateur and pro job is in the prep—never skip it.

Tools & Materials

  • Paint (1 gallon covers about 350 sq ft per coat)
  • Primer (if going light-to-dark or over stains)
  • 9-inch roller frame with 3/8-inch nap covers (2)
  • 2½-inch angled sash brush
  • Painter's tape (blue or green)
  • Drop cloths (canvas, not plastic)
  • Extension pole for roller (2–4 ft)
  • Spackle, putty knife, sanding sponge (120-grit)
  • Paint tray with liners

Step-by-Step

  1. Clear and prep the room. Move furniture to the center and cover with plastic. Remove switch plates, outlet covers, and window hardware. Lay canvas drop cloths—plastic is slippery and does not absorb drips.
  2. Repair walls. Fill nail holes and dents with spackle. Let dry, then sand flush with 120-grit sanding sponge. Wipe all walls with a damp cloth to remove dust, cobwebs, and grease, especially in kitchens and around light switches.
  3. Tape edges. Apply painter's tape along baseboards, door frames, window trim, and ceiling edges. Press the tape edge down firmly with a putty knife for a tight seal. Use green tape (FrogTape) in bathrooms—it blocks moisture better.
  4. Prime if needed. Cut in edges with a brush and roll primer on walls. Priming is mandatory when painting over dark colors, raw drywall, stains, or when switching from oil to latex paint. Let dry per label instructions.
  5. Cut in the edges. Dip the angled brush about 1/3 into the paint and tap off excess. Paint a 2–3 inch band along all taped edges—ceiling line, baseboards, corners, and around trim. Work in manageable sections before the cut-in paint dries.
  6. Roll the walls. Load the roller evenly by rolling it through the paint tray and over the ribbed ramp a few times. Apply paint in a W or M pattern across a 3x3-foot section, then fill in with vertical strokes. Maintain a wet edge by overlapping the previous section while still wet.
  7. Apply a second coat. Wait for the first coat to dry per the can label (typically 2–4 hours for latex). Lightly sand any rough spots with a fine sanding sponge. Apply the second coat using the same cut-in and roll method.
  8. Remove tape and clean up. Pull tape off at a 45-degree angle while the paint is still slightly tacky (not fully cured) for the sharpest line. If paint has dried, score along the tape edge with a utility knife first.

Tips

Tip 1: Do not dry-brush the roller—reload as soon as the roller starts making a sticky sound. A dry roller pulls paint off the wall.
Tip 2: Mix all gallons of the same color together in a 5-gallon bucket (called boxing) for perfect color consistency across the room.
Tip 3: A satin or eggshell sheen hides wall imperfections better than flat and is more washable in high-traffic rooms.
Tip 4: Never paint over wallpaper—the moisture in paint reactivates the glue and causes bubbling and peeling.
Tip 5: Wrap your roller and brush in plastic wrap and store in the fridge between coats instead of cleaning—good for up to 24 hours.
