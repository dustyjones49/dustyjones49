How to Change Lawn Mower Oil

Overview

Changing mower oil annually (or every 50 hours of use) is the single most important maintenance task for engine longevity. Old oil loses viscosity, accumulates abrasive particles, and leads to premature engine wear. The process takes 20 minutes.

Tools & Materials

  • SAE 30 or 10W-30 motor oil (check your manual—most push mowers use 18–20 oz)
  • Oil drain pan or container
  • Socket wrench (for drain plug models)
  • Oil extractor pump or siphon (optional, for top-fill mowers)
  • Funnel
  • Rags or paper towels
  • Work gloves
  • Cardboard or drop cloth

Step-by-Step

  1. Warm up the engine. Run the mower for 5 minutes. Warm oil flows better and suspends more contaminants, so you drain more of the dirty oil out.
  2. Disable the mower. Disconnect the spark plug wire. This is non-negotiable—you'll be tipping and rotating the blade area.
  3. Prepare the drain area. Spread cardboard or a drop cloth under the mower. Oil spills on concrete leave permanent stains.
  4. Drain the oil (drain plug method). Place the oil pan under the drain plug (located on the side or bottom of the engine block). Remove the plug with a socket wrench. Let all oil drain completely, then replace and tighten the plug.
  5. Drain the oil (tilt method). If your mower has no drain plug, remove the dipstick and tip the mower on its side with the dipstick tube facing down over your drain pan. Oil will pour out through the dipstick tube. Tip with the air filter and carburetor facing UP.
  6. Drain the oil (extractor method). For mowers that can't be tipped easily, insert an oil extractor pump tube down the dipstick tube. Pump or siphon the oil out into your container.
  7. Check the old oil. Look for metal flakes (abnormal wear), milky color (water contamination from bad gas or condensation), or burnt smell. These are warning signs of engine problems.
  8. Refill with fresh oil. Insert a funnel into the oil fill tube (usually the dipstick tube). Pour in the manufacturer-specified amount of fresh oil slowly. Check the dipstick frequently—wipe, insert without threading, and read. The oil level should be at the FULL mark but not over it.
  9. Check for leaks. Wipe around the drain plug and dipstick. Reconnect the spark plug and run the mower for 2 minutes. Shut it off, wait 1 minute, and recheck the oil level. Look under the mower for leaks.
  10. Dispose of old oil properly. Pour the used oil into a sealed container and take it to an auto parts store or recycling center. Never pour motor oil down a drain or onto the ground.

Pro Tips

Tip 1: Change oil at the end of the mowing season, not the beginning. Used oil contains acids that corrode engine internals during winter storage.
Tip 2: Don't overfill. Overfilled oil gets whipped into foam by the crankshaft, which doesn't lubricate properly and can cause engine seizure.
Tip 3: Check your owner's manual for the correct oil type. Most push mowers use SAE 30 in warm weather and 5W-30 in cold. Riding mowers with pressure-lubricated systems often use 10W-30. Using the wrong viscosity can cause hard starting and excessive wear.
Tip 4: Write the date and hours on the oil fill cap with a permanent marker or keep a maintenance log so you know when the next change is due.
