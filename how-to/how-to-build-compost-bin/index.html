How to Build a Compost Bin

Overview

A three-bin composting system allows you to cycle material continuously: one bin for fresh material, one for active composting, and one for finished compost ready to use. Building it from cedar or reclaimed pallets costs $30—100 and takes an afternoon. The key design features are slatted sides for airflow and removable front slats for easy access when turning the pile.

Tools & Materials

  • 11 pieces of 2×4 lumber (8' long, cedar or pressure-treated for ground contact in posts), and 10 pieces of 1×6 cedar boards (8' long for slats)
  • 3" and 1-1/4" exterior deck screws, 16d galvanized nails
  • Circular saw or miter saw, drill with driver bits and countersink bit
  • Level, measuring tape, speed square, post hole digger or shovel
  • Wood glue (exterior-rated), galvanized corner brackets (optional), gravel for post holes

Step-by-Step

  1. Layout and set posts: Mark out three bins, each approximately 3' × 3'. You'll need 8 posts (4 corners plus 2 intermediate posts × 2 per intermediate wall). Dig post holes 18"—24" deep. Set posts in 6" of gravel for drainage, fill holes with soil/gravel mixture, and compact firmly. Check each post is plumb (vertical) in both directions.
  2. Assemble the back and sides: Cut 1×6 cedar slats to span between posts (about 36" for the back and 34" for sides, allowing for post width). Attach slats horizontally to the posts using 1-1/4" deck screws, two per slat end. Leave a 1/2" gap between each slat for airflow — a scrap piece of 1/2" plywood works as a consistent spacer. Air is the magic ingredient in composting.
  3. Build the front slat rails: For the front of each bin, screw two vertical 2×2 or ripped 2×4 rails to the inside of the front posts, creating a 3/4" channel on each side. The 1×6 front slats will slide into these channels. This removable front design lets you easily access the compost pile with a pitchfork.
  4. Cut and install front slats: Cut 1×6 slats to slide into the front channels. They should drop in without force but not rattle. As the pile fills, add slats one by one from the bottom up. When you need to turn or remove compost, lift out the slats from the top first. Store extra slats nearby.
  5. Build divider walls: For the walls between bins, attach 1×6 slats to both sides of the intermediate posts. This creates solid, airflow-friendly dividers that keep piles separate but allow air to circulate through all three bins.
  6. Add lids (optional but recommended): Build a simple frame from 1×3 lumber sized to rest on top of each bin. Cover with exterior-grade plywood or corrugated plastic roofing. Hinge with two exterior hinges per lid. Lids control moisture — compost needs to be about as wet as a wrung-out sponge. Too much rain will waterlog it; too much sun will dry it.

Tips & Warnings

  • Locate the bin in partial shade if possible — full sun dries the pile and requires frequent watering; full shade slows decomposition significantly.
  • The ideal compost ratio is roughly 3 parts brown (dried leaves, straw, cardboard, sawdust) to 1 part green (grass clippings, vegetable scraps, coffee grounds) by volume.
  • Turn the pile every 1—2 weeks with a pitchfork. Active composting generates heat (130°F—160°F) which kills weed seeds and pathogens — if the pile isn't hot, add more green material and turn more frequently.
  • If using pressure-treated lumber for ground-contact posts, line the interior with landscape fabric to prevent chemical leaching into the compost.
  • Chop or shred materials before adding — smaller pieces decompose faster. Run over leaves with a lawnmower, and chop kitchen scraps into 1—2" pieces.
