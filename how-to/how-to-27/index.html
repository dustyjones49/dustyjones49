How to Replace a Light Switch

Overview

Replacing a worn, noisy, or unattractive light switch is a 15-minute job that requires no prior electrical experience. This guide covers a standard single-pole switch (one switch controls one light). Always turn off the power at the breaker and verify with a voltage tester before touching any wires. A switch costs $2–$5 and dramatically updates the look of a room.

Tools & Materials

  • Single-pole light switch (15A or 20A)
  • Non-contact voltage tester
  • Flathead & Phillips screwdrivers
  • Wire strippers
  • Needle-nose pliers
  • Electrical tape
  • Outlet/switch tester (optional, for verification)
  • New cover plate (standard or screwless decor style)

Step-by-Step

  1. Turn off the power. Go to the breaker panel and switch off the breaker for the circuit. Return to the room and flip the switch—the light should not turn on. Confirm with a non-contact voltage tester on the switch wires.
  2. Remove the old switch plate and switch. Unscrew the cover plate. Unscrew the two mounting screws holding the switch to the electrical box. Gently pull the switch out about 3–4 inches by the mounting ears—do not pull by the wires.
  3. Document the wiring. Take a photo of the existing wiring before disconnecting anything. A single-pole switch has two brass screw terminals (and sometimes a green ground screw). Note which wire is on which screw.
  4. Disconnect the old switch. Loosen the terminal screws and remove the wires. If the wires are back-stabbed (pushed into holes in the back), press a small flathead into the release slot to free them. Cut and re-strip wires that were back-stabbed—the clamped portion may be damaged.
  5. Prep the wires. Inspect each wire for nicks or corrosion. Re-strip to 3/4 inch of bare copper if needed. Form a clockwise hook or leave straight for the pressure-plate terminals on newer switches.
  6. Connect to the new switch. Single-pole switches have two brass screws—it does not matter which hot wire goes to which screw. Hook the wires clockwise around each brass screw. Connect the bare copper ground wire to the green ground screw. If the box has multiple ground wires, pigtail them together with a wire nut and run a pigtail to the switch ground screw.
  7. Tape and fold. Wrap electrical tape around the body of the switch, covering all terminal screws. This is an extra safety barrier. Gently fold the wires back into the box accordion-style—do not force or kink them. Push the switch flush to the box.
  8. Secure and test. Screw the switch mounting screws into the box. Install the cover plate. Turn the breaker back on. Test the switch. Leave it in the ON position and verify the screw terminals are not energized with the non-contact tester—only the hot wire should be live.

Tips

Tip 1: If the new switch has a different feel (decor/rocker vs. toggle), buy a matching cover plate—they are not interchangeable.
Tip 2: If you see two black wires, one red wire, and a ground, you likely have a 3-way switch. Buy a 3-way switch and match the common terminal wire carefully.
Tip 3: A switch that crackles or feels warm to the touch has loose internal contacts and is a fire hazard. Replace it immediately.
Tip 4: If the switch controls nothing and the hot wire is spliced through with a wire nut to pass power onward, do not bypass it—that outlet or light downstream may depend on it.
Tip 5: If the electrical box is metal, it must be grounded. Ensure the ground wire is connected to both the switch and the box via a ground screw or grounding clip.
