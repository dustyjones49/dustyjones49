How to Clean Dryer Vent

Overview

Clogged dryer vents cause over 15,000 house fires annually in the US. Cleaning the vent duct and exterior exhaust once or twice per year reduces fire risk, speeds up drying times, and lowers energy costs.

Tools & Materials

  • Dryer vent cleaning brush kit (flexible rods, lint brush)
  • Power drill (to spin the brush)
  • Vacuum with hose and crevice tool
  • Screwdriver or nut driver
  • Dust mask
  • Gloves
  • Flashlight

Step-by-Step

  1. Unplug the dryer. If it's a gas dryer, also shut off the gas supply valve.
  2. Pull the dryer out. Carefully slide the dryer away from the wall to access the vent connection.
  3. Disconnect the vent duct. Loosen the clamp at the dryer outlet and at the wall connection. Remove the duct.
  4. Vacuum the lint trap housing. Use the crevice tool to vacuum inside the dryer's lint trap cavity. Reach in and remove any compacted lint.
  5. Clean the flexible duct. Shake out the duct, then run the brush through it end to end. Vacuum out the loosened debris.
  6. Clean the wall duct. Attach the lint brush to the flexible rods and connect to a power drill. Feed the brush into the wall duct while spinning at low speed. Add rod sections as you go deeper.
  7. Clean the exterior vent. Go outside and remove the vent cover or flapper. Pull out any lint clog from the exterior end. Vacuum around the opening.
  8. Reassemble. Reconnect the duct to the dryer and wall, tightening the clamps. Slide the dryer back. Plug in and test with an empty cycle—air should blow forcefully from the exterior vent.

Pro Tips

Tip 1: If your dryer is against an exterior wall, replace the flexible foil duct with rigid metal duct. It collects less lint and is far safer.
Tip 2: Clean the vent every 6 months if you do 4+ loads per week. Homes with long vent runs (over 10 feet) need cleaning more often.
Tip 3: Replace plastic or foil accordion ducts immediately—they are fire hazards and are banned by most building codes.
Tip 4: If the brush gets stuck, reverse the drill direction rather than forcing it. Forcing can break the rod inside the duct.
