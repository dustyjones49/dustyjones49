How to Install a Dishwasher

Overview

Replacing or installing a dishwasher involves connecting water supply, drain, and electrical lines. The job takes about 2—4 hours for a first-timer. Make sure the rough-in opening is 24 inches wide, 24 inches deep, and 34 inches tall. Always shut off water and power before starting.

Tools & Materials

  • New dishwasher
  • Adjustable wrench & channel-lock pliers
  • Phillips & flathead screwdrivers
  • Wire strippers & wire nuts
  • Teflon tape
  • Level (2-foot)
  • Bucket & towels
  • 90-degree elbow fitting (3/8" compression x 3/4" hose)

Step-by-Step

  1. Turn off power and water. Switch off the dishwasher breaker and close the hot water shut-off valve under the sink.
  2. Remove the old dishwasher. Disconnect drain hose, water supply line, and wiring. Remove the mounting screws beneath the countertop. Slide the old unit out.
  3. Prep the new dishwasher. Tip the unit on its back. Attach the 90-degree elbow to the water inlet valve using Teflon tape on the threads. Tighten snugly.
  4. Connect the drain hose. Loop the corrugated drain hose up high inside the cabinet (an air gap or high loop prevents backflow). Secure it to the garbage disposal inlet or sink drain tailpiece with a hose clamp.
  5. Wire the unit. Open the junction box on the dishwasher. Connect black to black (hot), white to white (neutral), and green/bare to the green ground screw. Secure wires with wire nuts and close the cover.
  6. Slide the dishwasher in. Feed the drain hose and water supply through the cabinet access hole. Carefully push the unit into the opening, pulling slack through as you go. Do not pinch wires or hoses.
  7. Level and secure. Place a level on the door panel. Adjust the front leveling legs until the unit is plumb and flush with cabinets. Drive mounting screws through the side brackets into the underside of the countertop.
  8. Connect the water supply. Attach the water supply line to the 90-degree elbow and tighten. Open the shut-off valve slowly and check for leaks.
  9. Test the installation. Turn the breaker back on. Run a short rinse cycle and check for leaks under the unit. Adjust leveling legs if the door rattles during operation.

Tips

Tip 1: Always use a new stainless-steel braided water supply line—never reuse an old rubber or plastic line.
Tip 2: If the floor is higher than the cabinet opening, remove the leveling legs entirely to gain extra clearance.
Tip 3: Run the drain hose as high as possible under the counter and secure it with a hose clamp to prevent siphoning.
Tip 4: Tighten the 90-degree elbow hand-tight plus a quarter turn with a wrench—over-tightening can crack the plastic inlet valve.
Tip 5: Before sliding the dishwasher in, lay a towel across the floor to prevent scratches on tile or wood.
