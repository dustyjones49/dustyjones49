How to Install Attic Stairs

Overview

Pull-down attic stairs replace a simple access panel with a folding ladder, making attic storage practical. Most kits fit between standard 24-inch on-center joists and install in 3–4 hours with basic tools.

Tools & Materials

  • Pre-built attic stair kit (fits your rough opening)
  • Circular saw or reciprocating saw
  • Power drill with screwdriver bits
  • 2x4 lumber (for framing if cutting a new opening)
  • 1x4 or 1x6 pine boards for trim
  • Finishing nails or trim screws
  • Level
  • Measuring tape
  • Shims
  • Caulk and expanding foam
  • Safety glasses and dust mask

Step-by-Step

  1. Choose your location. Attic stairs need at least 54 to 60 inches of landing space below the opening and enough swing clearance. Hallways and garages are ideal locations.
  2. Frame the rough opening. If replacing an existing access panel, measure and cut ceiling drywall to the size specified by the stair kit (typically 22.5" by 54"). If cutting a new opening, cut one joist and frame a box with doubled headers.
  3. Confirm the opening is square. Measure both diagonals; they should be equal. If not, adjust the framing until the opening is perfectly square.
  4. Hoist the stair unit into the opening. With a helper, lift the folded stair unit from below and set it into the rough opening. Let the stair frame rest on the ceiling joists.
  5. Shim and level the frame. Use shims between the stair frame and the rough opening to level the frame in all directions. The door panel must sit flush with the ceiling when closed.
  6. Screw the frame to the joists. Drive screws through the pre-drilled holes in the stair frame into the joists. Use at least 3-inch screws and hit solid wood.
  7. Test the stairs. Pull down the ladder and confirm all sections lock into place. Check that the feet rest flat on the floor. If not, the instructions will guide you on trimming the bottom ladder section.
  8. Seal around the frame. From the attic side, seal any gaps between the stair frame and the rough opening with expanding foam or caulk to prevent air leaks.
  9. Install trim. From below, nail or screw 1x4 trim boards around the perimeter of the opening to cover the drywall cut edges. Miter the corners.
  10. Add weatherstripping. Apply adhesive-backed foam weatherstrip to the door panel edges so it seals tight when closed. This is critical for energy efficiency.

Pro Tips

Tip 1: Choose an insulated stair unit with an R-value of at least 10. Uninsulated attic stairs are a major energy leak.
Tip 2: Before cutting any joists, verify they are not carrying a structural load from above. Consult a structural engineer if unsure.
Tip 3: Temporarily screw 1x2 cleats to the joists at the rough opening corners to support the stair unit while you shim and screw it in place.
Tip 4: Paint the trim and door panel to match the ceiling before installing. It's much easier than painting overhead after installation.
