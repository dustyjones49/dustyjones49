How to Build a Deck

Overview

Building a deck is a major structural project that adds outdoor living space and home value. A ground-level deck (under 30 inches above grade) with a ledger board attached to the house is the most common DIY build. This requires a building permit, plan approval, and inspections in most jurisdictions. Budget 3—5 weekends for a 12x16-foot deck with two capable DIYers. Check local codes for footing depth, joist span, and railing requirements before buying materials.

Tools & Materials

  • Pressure-treated 2x8 or 2x10 joists and beams
  • Pressure-treated 5/4-inch decking boards or composite decking
  • Concrete footings (sonotubes or precast deck blocks)
  • Galvanized or stainless steel joist hangers & structural screws
  • Ledger board with ledgerLok screws (for house attachment)
  • Circular saw, miter saw, drill/driver with star-drive bits
  • 4-foot level, post level, string line, stakes
  • Post-hole digger or auger, wheelbarrow, concrete mix in bags
  • Joist tape (self-adhesive butyl tape for tops of joists)

Step-by-Step

  1. Design and obtain a permit. Draw a plan showing dimensions, joist spacing, beam placement, and footing locations. Submit to your local building department. They will check for structural adequacy and zoning setbacks. Do not skip this—an unpermitted deck can block a home sale.
  2. Attach the ledger board to the house. The ledger attaches to a vertical band joist, not to siding. Cut siding away and flash the top with Z-flashing. Fasten with 1/2-inch ledgerLok screws into house framing every 12—16 inches staggered. Remove siding to attach directly to the band joist or blocking for structural integrity.
  3. Set the footings. Mark footing locations per your plan. Dig holes to frost depth (24—48 inches depending on climate). Set sonotubes, level, and pour concrete footings. Insert a galvanized post anchor into the wet concrete at each footing. Let cure for 48 hours.
  4. Set posts and beams. Cut 4x4 or 4x6 pressure-treated posts to height. Set in post anchors and bolt in place. Place a doubled 2x beam on top of the posts, joining beam sections with staggered splices over posts. Use structural screws to fasten beams to posts. Check for level and square.
  5. Install floor joists. Attach joist hangers to the ledger board at each joist location. Set 2x joists into the hangers at one end and rest them on the beam at the other. Space joists per your plan (usually 16 inches on center for 2x8s). Install blocking between joists at the beam line to prevent twisting.
  6. Square the frame. Measure diagonally from corner to corner. Adjust until both diagonals are equal. Temporarily brace the outer rim joist to hold the square while you install remaining hangers and hardware. Install structural screws at every joist-to-rim connection.
  7. Apply joist tape. Apply butyl joist tape to the top of every joist, beam, and the ledger. This self-sealing membrane prevents water from sitting on the tops of framing and rotting the wood from the top down—especially important around fasteners.
  8. Install decking boards. Start at the house and work outward. Use a spacer (16D nail or plastic spacer) to maintain a consistent 1/8-inch gap between boards. Fasten each board with two screws per joist—stainless steel for treated wood. Stagger end joints and pre-drill near board ends to prevent splitting.
  9. Trim edges and install fascia. Snap a chalk line and trim overhanging decking to a uniform overhang (typically 1 inch past the rim). Attach fascia boards around the perimeter to cover the rim joist and framing.
  10. Build and install railing. Cut 4x4 posts and bolt them to the inside of the rim joist with through-bolts. Install top and bottom rails between posts, then install balusters spaced no more than 4 inches apart (code requirement). Test railing by pushing outward with 200 pounds of force on the top rail.

Tips

Tip 1: Crown your joists. Sight down each joist and install it with the crown (bow) facing up. Gravity and load will flatten the crown. If installed crowned down, it will sag.
Tip 2: Joist tape is not optional. This $30 roll of butyl tape adds 10+ years to the life of your deck framing by preventing water intrusion into screw holes and joints.
Tip 3: Composite decking expands and contracts significantly with temperature. Leave wider gaps at butt joints on hot days; narrower on cold days. Follow manufacturer gap specs exactly.
Tip 4: Never notch guardrail posts—notches concentrate stress and cause post failure. Bolt full-dimension 4x4s to the inside face of the rim joist with 1/2-inch through-bolts.
Tip 5: Install decking groove-side down when using grooved composite boards—the grooved underside improves drainage and reduces cupping.
