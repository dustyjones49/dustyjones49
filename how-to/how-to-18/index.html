How to Remove Carpet

Overview

Removing carpet is a straightforward but dusty, physically demanding job. You will discover what is underneath the pad—sometimes a pleasant surprise like hardwood, sometimes not. A 12x12-foot room takes about 1 hour to demo. The hidden work is pulling hundreds of staples and tack strips. Wear a respirator—carpet pad holds decades of dust.

Tools & Materials

  • Utility knife with plenty of extra blades
  • Pry bar or flat crowbar
  • Hammer
  • Pliers (needle-nose or fencing pliers for staples)
  • Heavy-duty staple remover or floor scraper
  • Respirator (N95 minimum) & safety glasses
  • Work gloves & knee pads
  • Heavy-duty trash bags (contractor bags)
  • Duct tape

Step-by-Step

  1. Clear the room. Remove all furniture. Take doors off their hinges for full access. Remove quarter-round or shoe molding with a pry bar—go slow to avoid splitting it if you plan to reuse it.
  2. Cut carpet into manageable strips. Using a utility knife (with a fresh blade always—carpet dulls blades almost instantly), cut the carpet into 3-foot-wide strips. Cut from the back side for easier blade travel. The blade should cut the carpet and pad but not the subfloor.
  3. Peel back and roll up strips. Starting at one corner, grab the carpet with pliers and pull. Fold each strip onto itself and duct-tape the bundle tightly. This makes hauling manageable—a full room of carpet is surprisingly heavy.
  4. Remove the carpet pad. The pad underneath is usually stapled every few inches. Cut it into strips like the carpet. Pull up the padding—it tears easily. Leave the staples in place for now; you will pull them in a separate pass.
  5. Remove tack strips. Tack strips run along the perimeter of the room, nailed into the subfloor. Position a flat pry bar under the strip, slide it to the nail location, and pry up gently. The nails will either pull out or the strip will split—either is fine. Wear heavy gloves; the tacks are sharp.
  6. Pull every staple. This is the most tedious step. Use needle-nose pliers or fencing pliers to pull each staple. A long-handled floor scraper with a sharp blade can shear staples off at the surface if you cannot pull them. Count on 200–500 staples per room.
  7. Remove any glued-down pad. If the pad was glued, you will see yellow foam residue on the subfloor. Scrape it with a 4-inch floor scraper. For stubborn glue, use a citrus-based adhesive remover and let it soak before scraping.
  8. Clean and inspect the subfloor. Sweep and vacuum the entire subfloor. Check for squeaks—drive 2-inch deck screws into any loose sheathing. Sand down any high spots or remaining adhesive ridges. The floor is now ready for new flooring.

Tips

Tip 1: Change utility knife blades every 10 feet of cutting—carpet fibers are highly abrasive and dull blades faster than any other material.
Tip 2: Cut carpet from the back side (flip it over). The blade cuts the backing material cleanly while the fibers part, avoiding a ragged edge.
Tip 3: Fencing pliers are the best tool for staple removal—they grip and lever staples out with minimal effort and are designed for repeated use.
Tip 4: Before renting a dumpster, call your municipal waste service. Most cities allow one bulk pickup per year included with trash service.
Tip 5: If you discover hardwood underneath, protect it immediately with ram board or cardboard. Subfloor staples leave tiny dark holes that are impossible to fill invisibly.
