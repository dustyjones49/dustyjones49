How to Refinish Hardwood Floors

Overview

Refinishing hardwood floors breathes new life into tired, scratched wood. The complete process consists of sanding (covered in How to Sand Hardwood Floors), staining (optional), and applying protective topcoats. This guide focuses on the staining and finishing stages. Budget 4–6 days from start to full cure for a 300-square-foot room.

Tools & Materials

  • Oil-based or water-based polyurethane (2–3 gallons for 300 sq ft)
  • Wood stain (oil-based, if coloring)
  • Lambswool applicator pad on a pole (for oil-based poly)
  • Synthetic applicator pad (for water-based poly)
  • Natural-bristle brush (3-inch, for cutting edges)
  • Mineral spirits & rags
  • Respirator with organic vapor cartridges
  • 220-grit sanding screen & buffer (for between coats)
  • Tack cloths & shop vac

Step-by-Step

  1. Ensure the floor is perfectly clean. After final sanding (see How to Sand Hardwood Floors), vacuum every surface including walls, windowsills, and light fixtures. Wipe the floor with a tack cloth dampened with mineral spirits. Any speck of dust becomes locked under the finish forever.
  2. Apply stain (if desired). Using a lambswool applicator or rag, apply oil-based stain with the grain in a manageable section (about 3x3 feet). Within 5–10 minutes, wipe off all excess with clean rags—the longer it sits, the darker it gets. Work quickly so sections blend without lap marks.
  3. Let stain dry completely. Oil-based stain typically needs 24 hours to dry. Test dryness by wiping a rag on an inconspicuous spot—any color transfer means it is not ready for topcoat. Rushing this step causes blushing and adhesion failure.
  4. Apply the first polyurethane coat. Stir poly gently—never shake (bubbles). Pour a small puddle and spread with a lambswool pad, working with the grain in long, overlapping strokes. Keep a wet edge. If using water-based poly, work faster—it dries quickly and lap marks set fast.
  5. Cut edges with a brush. Before or immediately after spreading poly in the field, use a brush to cut a 3-inch swath along walls. Brush with the grain. Do not let the cut-in dry before the field poly reaches it.
  6. Let dry and screen between coats. Wait per instructions (8–24 hours for oil, 2–4 hours for water-based). Lightly buff the entire floor with a buffer and 220-grit sanding screen. This scuffs the surface for inter-coat adhesion and knocks down dust nibs. Vacuum and tack cloth thoroughly.
  7. Apply the second coat. Repeat the application process. Two coats is the minimum; three coats is standard for high-traffic areas. The floor should look glassy after the third coat.
  8. Apply the final coat. The last coat goes on without any sanding afterward. Apply with extra care—this is the surface that will be seen and walked on for years. Use a fresh, clean applicator pad.
  9. Let cure fully. Wait at least 24 hours before light foot traffic (socks only) and 72 hours before replacing furniture. Full cure takes up to 30 days for oil-based poly—avoid rugs during this period, as they trap moisture and leave permanent marks.

Tips

Tip 1: Water-based poly dries fast and stays crystal clear but raises the grain more—you may need 4 coats instead of 3.
Tip 2: Oil-based poly gives a warm amber tone and self-levels beautifully, but it off-gasses for weeks—plan to stay out of the house if possible.
Tip 3: When staining, apply with one hand and wipe with the other on the same pass. Stopping to wipe after applying a whole row leaves a visible ghost line.
Tip 4: Never use a lambswool pad with water-based poly—the lanolin in lamb's wool reacts with water-based finishes and causes adhesion failure. Use synthetic.
Tip 5: Do the fingernail test before the final coat: try to scratch the floor with your thumbnail. If it leaves a mark, you need at least one more coat.
