Standard Window Sizes

Common Window Dimensions by Type

Standard window sizes follow conventions established by major manufacturers to keep costs down and lead times short. Most residential windows are 24 to 72 inches wide and 36 to 72 inches tall in increments of 2 inches. Single-hung windows have a fixed upper sash and operable lower sash. Double-hung windows allow both sashes to tilt in for cleaning. Casement windows crank outward on side hinges and provide the best ventilation because the entire opening can be cleared. Sliding windows have one fixed panel and one panel that glides horizontally. Picture windows are fixed glass units with no operable parts, maximizing views and daylight. Bay and bow combinations use multiple smaller windows ganged together. Egress windows in bedrooms must provide a minimum 5.7-square-foot clear opening (5.0 for grade floor) with at least 20 inches of clear width and 24 inches of clear height. The sill height for egress windows cannot exceed 44 inches above the finished floor.

Rough Opening Guidelines

The rough opening for a window is typically 0.5 inch wider and 0.5 inch taller than the window's nominal frame size. Most manufacturers list exact rough opening dimensions in their product literature. Always add shim space around the perimeter for leveling and squaring. Structural headers above windows must be sized according to span and load; consult local building codes. For replacement windows, measure the inside of the existing frame from jamb to jamb in three places (top, middle, bottom) for width and three places (left, center, right) for height, then use the smallest measurement.

  • Rough opening = nominal size + 0.5 in each direction
  • Header required above all openings in load-bearing walls
  • Sill must slope to shed water (IRC R703.4)
  • Flashing is required at head, sill, and jambs
  • Weep holes at sill for drainage in framed windows
