HOA Rules Common Restrictions

Understanding HOA Covenants

Homeowners associations enforce covenants, conditions, and restrictions that govern property use, appearance, and modifications within a community. These rules are legally binding documents recorded with the property deed. Before purchasing in an HOA community, prospective buyers should review the CC&Rs carefully because they can significantly limit renovation plans, exterior modifications, and lifestyle choices. Violations can result in fines, liens, and in extreme cases, foreclosure proceedings.

Paint color restrictions are among the most common and contentious HOA rules. Many associations maintain an approved color palette from which homeowners must select exterior paint colors, siding materials, and trim colors. Some HOAs require pre-approval of any color even within the approved palette, while others mandate specific colors for each building elevation to maintain neighborhood uniformity. Fence restrictions typically limit height to 4 to 6 feet and specify materials such as wood, vinyl, or wrought iron, often prohibiting chain link entirely. Front-yard fences may be prohibited or restricted to low decorative designs to preserve the open streetscape character. Fence styles, colors, and placement relative to the property line may all be regulated.

Shed and outbuilding restrictions limit size, typically to 80 to 120 square feet, and often require materials and roof pitches matching the primary house. Some associations prohibit detached structures entirely. Parking restrictions commonly prohibit parking commercial vehicles, RVs, boats, or trailers in driveways or on the street, requiring them to be stored in enclosed garages or off-site. Landscaping rules may require minimum percentages of turf grass, restrict vegetable gardens to rear yards, prohibit certain invasive plant species, and mandate minimum tree counts per lot. Holiday decoration restrictions limit the timing and duration of displays. Most HOAs require architectural review committee approval before any exterior modification, including painting, roofing, window replacement, solar panels, satellite dishes, and landscaping changes.
