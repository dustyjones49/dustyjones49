Carbon Monoxide Detector Placement

CO Detector Location Requirements

Carbon monoxide is an invisible, odorless gas produced by incomplete combustion from furnaces, water heaters, gas stoves, fireplaces, and vehicles. Because CO mixes evenly with air and does not concentrate at any particular height, detectors can be mounted at any level. The National Fire Protection Association standard 720 and International Residential Code section R315 establish placement requirements that most local codes follow. Proper placement is the single most important factor in whether a CO detector will protect occupants.

CO detectors must be installed outside each separate sleeping area in the immediate vicinity of the bedrooms. In homes where bedrooms are clustered together in a hallway, one detector centrally located in that hallway satisfies this requirement. If bedrooms are on opposite ends of the house or on different floors, additional detectors are needed. A CO detector should also be installed on every habitable level of the home, including basements but excluding crawl spaces and uninhabitable attics. If the home has an attached garage, a CO detector is strongly recommended in the room adjacent to the garage door, as vehicle exhaust can infiltrate living spaces through gaps around the door and through shared wall cavities.

Detector placement relative to fuel-burning appliances requires balance. Install detectors 5 to 20 feet away from fuel-burning appliances to avoid nuisance alarms from small amounts of CO produced during startup, but close enough to detect appliance malfunctions. Avoid placement in dead air spaces such as the junction of wall and ceiling, in turbulent air near ceiling fans or supply registers, or in areas of high humidity such as bathrooms. Do not install CO detectors within 15 feet of cooking appliances to avoid nuisance alarms from smoke and steam. Interconnected detectors, whether hardwired or wireless, provide the best protection by activating all alarms in the home when any single detector senses CO. Detectors should be replaced every 5 to 7 years depending on manufacturer specifications, as sensors degrade over time.
