Ladder Types and Maximum Working Heights

Ladder selection depends on the working height required, the ladder's capacity (duty rating), and the environment (indoor, outdoor, electrical hazard). Step ladders are self-supporting A-frames available in sizes from 2-foot to 20-foot. The maximum safe standing level on a step ladder is the second rung from the top — never stand on the top cap or the top rung. The "reach height" is roughly 4 feet above the ladder's platform height, assuming a user height of 5 feet 9 inches. A 6-foot step ladder provides a working reach of approximately 10 feet (to the gutter line of a single-story home). Extension ladders are non-self-supporting and consist of a base section and one or more fly sections that extend via a rope and pulley system. Extension ladder lengths range from 16 to 60 feet in the standard Type I (250 lb) and Type IA (300 lb) duty ratings. The ladder must extend a minimum of 3 feet above the roofline or landing surface when used for roof access. The base of the ladder must be placed 1 foot away from the wall for every 4 feet of working height (the 4-to-1 rule). Multi-position ladders (articulating ladders) can be configured as a step ladder, extension ladder, stairway ladder, or scaffolding base. They are versatile but heavier and more expensive than single-purpose ladders. Platform ladders have a large standing platform and are OSHA-compliant for sustained work in commercial environments; they range from 2 to 18 feet. Telescoping ladders collapse down for storage and transport, popular for trade workers who need a compact extension ladder. Fiberglass ladders are non-conductive and required for all electrical work; aluminum ladders are lighter but conduct electricity and should never be used near power lines.

Ladder Safety and Duty Ratings

ANSI and OSHA define ladder duty ratings based on maximum load capacity including the user plus tools and materials. Type III (200 lb) is light-duty household use. Type II (225 lb) is medium-duty commercial. Type I (250 lb) is heavy-duty industrial, the most common tradesperson rating. Type IA (300 lb) is extra heavy-duty, and Type IAA (375 lb) is special-duty for the heaviest users and loads. The 3-point contact rule requires maintaining two hands and one foot, or two feet and one hand, in contact with the ladder at all times. Overreaching is the leading cause of ladder falls — the belt buckle should stay between the ladder rails. Extension ladders must be tied off at the top when used for roof access and must have non-slip rubber feet on firm, level ground.

  • 4-to-1 rule: base = 1 ft away per 4 ft working height
  • 3 ft minimum extension above roofline for roof access
  • 3-point contact: 2 hands + 1 foot or 2 feet + 1 hand always
  • Top step/cap: never stand on (step ladders)
  • Fiberglass: required for electrical work (non-conductive)
  • Inspect before each use: check rungs, rails, spreaders, feet
