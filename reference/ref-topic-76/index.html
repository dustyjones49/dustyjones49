Proper Plant Spacing

Proper plant spacing is one of the most common mistakes in residential landscaping. The temptation to create an instant, full appearance by planting too closely together leads to overcrowding within three to five years as plants reach mature size. Overcrowded plants compete for light, water, and nutrients, resulting in stunted growth, increased susceptibility to disease due to poor air circulation, and the eventual need to remove or severely prune plants that outgrow their space. Spacing decisions should be based on the mature spread of each plant, not the size at purchase.

Perennials typically reach their mature spread within two to three growing seasons. Small perennials with a mature spread of 12 to 18 inches, such as coral bells, lavender, and dianthus, should be spaced 12 to 18 inches apart center-to-center. Medium perennials reaching 18 to 36 inches, including coneflowers, black-eyed susans, and ornamental grasses, need 18 to 30 inches of spacing. Large perennials over 36 inches, such as Joe Pye weed, Russian sage, and large hostas, require 30 to 48 inches. When planting perennials in masses or drifts, stagger the rows for a natural appearance and plant at the closer end of the spacing range for fuller coverage.

Shrubs have longer establishment periods and larger mature spreads. Compact shrubs like boxwood, dwarf holly, and spirea reaching 3 to 4 feet wide should be spaced 3 to 4 feet apart for a continuous hedge or 5 to 6 feet apart as individual specimens. Medium shrubs like hydrangea, viburnum, and forsythia need 5 to 8 feet spacing. Large shrubs including lilac, rhododendron, and butterfly bush require 8 to 12 feet. Foundation plantings need special consideration: allow at least half the mature spread for clearance from the house wall. Trees require the most careful spacing. Small ornamental trees like dogwood and redbud need 15 to 25 feet. Medium shade trees including maple, oak, and birch require 30 to 50 feet. Large trees such as sycamore, cottonwood, and walnut need 50 feet or more and should never be planted within 20 feet of a foundation.
