Flooring Material Comparison

Hardwood, Laminate, LVP, Tile, and Carpet Head-to-Head

Flooring choice is one of the highest-impact design decisions because it covers every square foot of the living space and must withstand daily wear for decades. Solid hardwood flooring (3/4 inch thick, nail-down installation) adds permanent value to a home and can be sanded and refinished 4-7 times over a 50-100+ year lifespan. It is priced at $6-12 per square foot installed for domestic species. The primary weakness of solid hardwood is moisture sensitivity: it cannot be installed below grade (basements) or directly on concrete slabs, and water spills must be cleaned promptly to prevent cupping and staining. Engineered hardwood uses a plywood or HDF core with a real hardwood veneer wear layer (1.2 mm to 4+ mm thick). It is dimensionally more stable than solid wood, can be floated or glued over concrete, and is suitable for basements and over radiant heat systems. The number of times it can be refinished depends on veneer thickness. Laminate flooring is a photographic image layer bonded to a high-density fiberboard core with a clear melamine wear layer. At $2-5 per square foot, it is the most affordable wood-look flooring. Laminate is highly scratch-resistant (AC3 to AC5 rating), fade-resistant, and installed as a floating floor with click-lock edges over a foam underlayment. However, laminate is not waterproof: standing water causes edge swelling and core disintegration, making it unsuitable for bathrooms, laundry rooms, and below-grade applications. Luxury Vinyl Plank (LVP) and Luxury Vinyl Tile (LVT) have surged in popularity over the past decade because they are 100% waterproof, dimensionally stable, and can be completely DIY-installed as a click-floating, glue-down, or loose-lay floor. LVP pricing spans a wide range: $2-6 per square foot for SPC (Stone Plastic Composite) or WPC (Wood Plastic Composite) core products. The wear layer thickness (typically 6 to 30 mil) determines scratch and abrasion resistance. Higher-end LVP is nearly indistinguishable from real wood or stone in appearance and texture. Ceramic and porcelain tile is the undisputed champion for water resistance and durability in wet areas. At $5-15+ per square foot installed, tile lasts 50+ years with proper subfloor preparation and installation. Tile is cold underfoot (radiant heating recommended) and unforgiving — dropped dishes break, and grout requires periodic sealing. Carpet is the softest, quietest, and warmest flooring option at $3-8 per square foot installed, but it has the shortest lifespan (5-15 years), traps allergens and dust, stains permanently without prompt treatment, and is unsuitable for wet areas. Nylon carpet is the most durable fiber (10-15 years); polyester (PET) is stain-resistant but crushes faster; wool is natural and luxurious but expensive.

Subfloor Preparation and Installation Factors

The flooring material is only as good as the subfloor beneath it. Solid hardwood requires a 3/4-inch plywood or OSB subfloor to staple through. Engineered hardwood and LVP floating floors require a flat subfloor within 1/8 inch over 6 feet (or 3/16 inch over 10 feet). High spots are ground down, and low spots are filled with leveling compound. A 6-mil polyethylene vapor barrier is required over concrete before installing engineered hardwood or laminate. For tile, a decoupling membrane (Schluter Ditra, Laticrete Stratamat) or cement backer board over the subfloor prevents cracks from transferring through the tile. Carpet requires a separate pad (rebond, memory foam, or rubber) that contributes to comfort and extends carpet life. Floor transitions between materials require T-molding, reducer, or threshold strips that accommodate the height difference.

  • Flatness tolerance: 1/8 in over 6 ft (floating floors), 3/16 in over 10 ft (tile)
  • Vapor barrier: 6 mil poly required over concrete for wood, laminate
  • Acclimation: solid hardwood must acclimate 5-14 days before installation
  • Moisture test: concrete slab must pass calcium chloride test (≤ 3-5 lbs/1000 sq ft/24 hr)
  • Tile decoupling: membrane or cement board prevents cracked tile and grout
  • Carpet pad: 6-8 lb density for residential, 8+ lb for commercial
