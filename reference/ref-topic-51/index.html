Concrete Slab Thickness Guide

Overview

Selecting the proper concrete slab thickness is critical for long-term durability and structural performance. The right thickness depends on the intended use, expected loads, soil conditions, and local building codes. A slab that is too thin will crack under load, while one that is too thick wastes material and money. This guide covers the most common residential and light commercial slab applications, along with reinforcement recommendations and subgrade preparation tips.

Patio slabs typically require a minimum of 4 inches of concrete, which is sufficient for foot traffic and light furniture. However, if the patio will support a hot tub or heavy masonry features, thickness should increase to 6 inches. Driveways demand at least 6 inches to withstand vehicle loads, including delivery trucks and SUVs. Garage slabs match the driveway at 6 inches, with thickened edges where vehicle tires rest near the door. Basement slabs can be 4 inches since they bear only on compacted granular fill, but 5 inches provides a margin for storage and mechanical equipment loads.

Reinforcement significantly extends slab life. Welded wire mesh (6x6 W2.9/W2.9) is standard for 4-inch slabs, while rebar grids at 18-inch centers are recommended for 6-inch slabs. Fiber mesh additive provides secondary reinforcement and reduces surface cracking. Control joints should be cut at intervals of 2 to 3 times the slab thickness in feet and to a depth of 25% of slab thickness. Proper subgrade compaction to 95% standard proctor density and a 4-inch gravel base are essential regardless of thickness to prevent settlement cracking.
