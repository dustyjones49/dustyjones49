Grass Types by Region

Choosing the Right Turfgrass

Turfgrass selection is determined primarily by climate zone. Grasses are classified as cool-season or warm-season based on their temperature range for active growth. Cool-season grasses thrive in air temperatures between 60 and 75 degrees Fahrenheit and are best suited for the northern half of the United States. Warm-season grasses grow most actively between 80 and 95 degrees and dominate southern lawns. The transition zone through the middle of the country presents challenges because neither type is perfectly adapted, requiring careful cultivar selection or a blend of both types.

Kentucky bluegrass is the iconic cool-season lawn grass, prized for its fine texture, rich green color, and ability to spread via rhizomes to fill in bare spots. It requires full sun, consistent moisture, and regular fertilization to maintain its appearance. Bluegrass goes dormant and turns brown during extended summer heat and drought if not irrigated. Tall fescue is the most drought-tolerant and shade-tolerant cool-season grass, with a deep root system reaching 2 to 3 feet. Its coarse texture makes it less desirable for fine lawns but ideal for low-maintenance yards, parks, and utility turf. Tall fescue stays green longer into summer than bluegrass and tolerates moderate foot traffic.

Bermuda grass is the most widely used warm-season grass, valued for its aggressive growth, fine texture, and excellent wear tolerance. It spreads rapidly via both stolons and rhizomes, making it the standard for sports fields, golf courses, and high-traffic lawns across the southern states. Bermuda goes dormant and turns straw-brown after the first frost, remaining dormant until soil temperatures reach 65 degrees in spring. Zoysia grass produces a dense, carpet-like lawn with moderate shade tolerance and good wear resistance. Its slow establishment requires patience, but once established, zoysia resists weeds and requires less mowing than Bermuda. St. Augustine grass has the best shade tolerance of any warm-season grass, making it the go-to choice for southern lawns with mature tree canopies. It requires more water than Bermuda and is susceptible to chinch bugs and fungal diseases in humid conditions.
