Concrete Crack Types

Identifying and Understanding Concrete Cracks

All concrete cracks. The saying is so common it has become an industry maxim, but not all cracks indicate a problem. Distinguishing between cosmetic and structural cracks is the first step in determining whether repair is necessary and what method to use. Crack width, pattern, location, and whether the crack is actively moving all inform the diagnosis and repair approach.

Hairline cracks are the most common and least concerning. Typically less than 1/16 inch wide, they form as concrete shrinks during the curing process. Plastic shrinkage cracks occur when the surface dries faster than the concrete below, often on hot or windy days. These cracks are generally cosmetic and can be sealed with a surface coating or left untreated if appearance is not a concern. Settlement cracks result from soil compaction failure beneath the slab. When the supporting ground gives way, the concrete follows, producing cracks wider than 1/8 inch, often with vertical displacement between slab sections.

Heaving cracks move upward rather than downward, caused by frost action or expansive soils. In cold climates, water in the soil freezes and expands, lifting the slab unevenly. Clay-rich soils swell when wet, producing similar uplift. These cracks require addressing the root cause: improving drainage, installing frost-protected footings, or removing expansive soils. Structural cracks are the most serious, often appearing as wide gaps exceeding 1/4 inch with significant vertical or horizontal displacement. They indicate overloading, design deficiencies, or foundation movement. Structural cracks demand professional evaluation and may require underpinning, slab replacement, or carbon fiber reinforcement.
