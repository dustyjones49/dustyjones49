Emergency Preparedness Kit

72-Hour Emergency Supplies

A well-stocked emergency preparedness kit enables a household to survive for at least 72 hours without external assistance following a natural disaster or other emergency. FEMA and the American Red Cross recommend that every household maintain an emergency kit with supplies customized for the specific risks in their geographic area, including hurricanes, earthquakes, tornadoes, wildfires, floods, and winter storms. The kit should be stored in a portable container that can be quickly accessed and transported if evacuation is necessary.

Water is the highest priority item. Store at least one gallon per person per day for a total of three gallons per person for a 72-hour supply. This water covers both drinking and basic sanitation needs. For a family of four, that means 12 gallons minimum. Commercially bottled water is the most reliable source, but food-grade water storage containers can be used with proper sanitization and rotation every six months. Food supplies should consist of non-perishable items that require no refrigeration, minimal preparation, and no cooking. Canned meats, fruits, and vegetables, protein bars, dry cereal, peanut butter, and shelf-stable milk are good choices. Include a manual can opener and disposable plates and utensils. Rotate food supplies annually by using items approaching expiration and replacing them.

First aid supplies should include adhesive bandages in assorted sizes, sterile gauze pads, adhesive tape, antiseptic wipes, antibiotic ointment, scissors, tweezers, non-latex gloves, a thermometer, and over-the-counter pain relievers, antacids, and anti-diarrheal medications. Include a seven-day supply of any prescription medications. Tools and supplies should include a battery-powered or hand-crank radio, a flashlight with extra batteries, a multi-tool or wrench to shut off utilities, duct tape, plastic sheeting, dust masks, and local maps. Documents should include copies of insurance policies, identification, bank account records, and emergency contact information stored in a waterproof container. Additional items include pet food, baby supplies, blankets, a change of clothing, and cash in small denominations since ATMs and card readers may be inoperative during power outages.
