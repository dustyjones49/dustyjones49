Underlayment Types

Underlayment Selection Guide

Underlayment is the layer installed between the subfloor and the finished flooring material. It serves multiple critical functions: smoothing minor subfloor irregularities, providing acoustic insulation, managing moisture, and in the case of floating floors, providing a slip surface for the flooring to expand and contract. Choosing the wrong underlayment can void flooring warranties, amplify footfall noise, or trap moisture that destroys the floor from below.

Foam underlayment is the most common and economical choice for laminate and engineered wood floating floors. It is available in rolls of polyethylene or polypropylene foam in thicknesses from 2mm to 5mm. Standard foam provides basic sound reduction and subfloor cushioning but offers no moisture protection unless manufactured with an attached vapor barrier. Cork underlayment is a premium natural material valued for its excellent acoustic properties. Cork's cellular structure absorbs impact noise and reduces sound transmission between floors, making it the preferred choice for multi-story buildings, condominiums, and any application where footfall noise is a concern. Cork naturally resists mold and mildew but must be paired with a separate vapor barrier over concrete subfloors.

Felt underlayment, made from recycled fibers, provides superior sound insulation and a denser feel underfoot than foam. It is commonly specified for luxury vinyl plank where a quieter, more solid feel is desired. Rubber underlayment offers the highest impact sound ratings and is often required by condominium associations for upper-floor installations. Plywood underlayment serves a different purpose: it is a structural layer, typically 1/4-inch to 1/2-inch thick, installed over OSB or plank subflooring to create a perfectly smooth, dimensionally stable surface for vinyl sheet flooring or tile backer board. It is not used under floating floors.
