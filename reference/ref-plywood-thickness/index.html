Plywood Thickness — Nominal vs. Actual

Understanding Plywood Thickness

Like dimensional lumber, plywood thickness is sold by a nominal measurement that differs from the actual sanded thickness. Most plywood undergoes sanding during manufacturing, which removes approximately 1/32" per face. APA-rated panels follow thickness standards defined in PS 1 and PS 2.

OSB vs. Plywood

Oriented Strand Board (OSB) is manufactured to the same nominal thicknesses as plywood but uses compressed wood strands instead of veneers. OSB is generally 5—10% cheaper than plywood, has more consistent density, but is heavier and more susceptible to moisture swelling at edges. Both products carry APA span ratings that dictate proper joist spacing.
