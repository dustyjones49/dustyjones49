Soil Classification for Building

The soil beneath a foundation is the most critical variable in structural performance. Soil bearing capacity determines the type and size of foundation required, while drainage characteristics dictate waterproofing and grading requirements. A geotechnical investigation, including soil borings and laboratory testing, is standard practice for commercial projects and recommended for residential construction on suspect soils. Understanding the five basic soil classifications helps builders anticipate foundation design requirements before test results are available.

Sand is composed of coarse particles between 0.05mm and 2mm in diameter. Its granular structure provides excellent drainage and resists volume changes from moisture variation. Compacted sand offers bearing capacities of 2,000 to 3,000 pounds per square foot, suitable for most residential foundations without special engineering. Silt particles range from 0.002mm to 0.05mm, finer than sand but coarser than clay. Silt has moderate bearing capacity but becomes unstable when saturated, potentially liquefying under seismic loading. It retains water and is susceptible to frost heave in cold climates. Clay consists of the smallest soil particles, less than 0.002mm, with electronically charged surfaces that attract and hold water molecules.

Clay's water affinity causes it to swell when wet and shrink when dry, producing volume changes that can crack foundations, heave slabs, and bow basement walls. Bearing capacity for clay ranges from 1,000 to 4,000 PSF depending on moisture content and density. Expansive clays, including montmorillonite and bentonite, are among the most problematic soils and require specialized foundation designs such as post-tensioned slabs, pier-and-grade beam systems, or chemical soil stabilization. Loam is an ideal soil blend of approximately 40% sand, 40% silt, and 20% clay plus organic matter. It provides good bearing capacity, moderate drainage, and minimal volume change. Gravel, the coarsest soil type with particles exceeding 2mm, offers the highest bearing capacity at 5,000 to 12,000 PSF and virtually no moisture-related volume change, making it the premier foundation substrate.
