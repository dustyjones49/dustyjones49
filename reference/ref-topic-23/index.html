Light Bulb Color Temperature

Correlated Color Temperature (CCT) Scale

Color temperature, measured in Kelvins (K), describes the color appearance of light emitted by a bulb. Lower Kelvin values produce warm, amber, yellowish light similar to candlelight or a sunrise, while higher Kelvin values produce cool, bluish, simulated daylight. The standard choices for residential lighting are 2700K (soft white), 3000K (warm white or bright white), and 3500K (neutral white). At 2700K, light mimics the familiar glow of traditional incandescent bulbs and is most flattering to skin tones, making it the preferred choice for living rooms, dining rooms, and bedrooms. The 3000K option is a crisper white still in the warm range, ideal for kitchens, bathrooms, and task areas where slightly better color rendering is beneficial without feeling clinical. At 3500K, the light begins to take on a neutral tone that works well in workspaces, home offices, and laundry rooms. Stepping up to 4000K delivers cool white light with a bluish tint, preferred in garages, workshops, basements, and commercial retail to increase perceived brightness and alertness. Daylight 5000K and 6500K produce the coolest, most blue-rich light, simulating bright midday sun. These are used in task-intensive environments like art studios, hospital operating rooms, and high-detail industrial assembly. Full-spectrum bulbs at these temperatures offer the highest Color Rendering Index (CRI) — above 95 — making colors appear most natural. Photographers, painters, and crafters benefit from 5000K-6500K bulbs with CRI 95+. The color temperature also influences circadian rhythm: warm light in the evening supports melatonin production; cool light in the morning promotes wakefulness.

CRI and Tunable White Technology

Color Rendering Index (CRI) measures how accurately a light source reveals colors compared to natural daylight of the same color temperature, on a scale of 0 to 100. Standard LED bulbs typically have a CRI of 80-85, which is adequate for general use. High-CRI LEDs (90+) are recommended for kitchens, bathrooms, closets, and anywhere color distinction matters. The newest "tunable white" LED fixtures allow the user to dial color temperature from warm (2700K) to cool (6500K) using a smartphone app, wall controller, or automated schedule. This technology supports wellness-focused lighting that follows the natural daylight cycle.

  • CRI 80-85: standard LED, adequate for general lighting
  • CRI 90-95: recommended for kitchens, baths, closets, artwork
  • CRI 95+: professional photography, museums, medical
  • Tunable white: 2700K - 6500K adjustable via app or switch
  • Warm dim: dims to warmer color as brightness reduces (mimics incandescent)
  • Kelvin = thermal radiation color; LED mimic this with phosphors
