Cabinet Door Styles

Choosing Cabinet Door Styles

Cabinet doors are the most visually dominant element in any kitchen, bathroom, or built-in project. The door style defines the design era, sets the tone for the room, and accounts for a significant portion of the cabinetry budget. Construction method, material, and finish all influence cost, durability, and maintenance. Understanding the characteristics of each door style helps match cabinet selections to both aesthetic preferences and practical needs.

Shaker doors are defined by their five-piece construction: a flat recessed center panel surrounded by a simple, square-edged frame. Originating from the Shaker religious communities of the 19th century, this style emphasizes simplicity, functionality, and quality craftsmanship. Shaker doors are the most versatile cabinet style, working equally well in traditional, transitional, and contemporary kitchens. Their clean lines avoid the dust-catching crevices of more ornate styles, and the flat center panel is easy to clean. Shaker doors are typically priced in the mid-range, making them the go-to choice for both budget-conscious renovations and high-end custom builds.

Raised panel doors feature a center panel that stands proud of the surrounding frame, often with a beveled or contoured profile. This style conveys traditional formality and is associated with Colonial, Victorian, and classic Georgian architecture. Raised panel doors require more material and machining steps than flat panel designs, placing them in a higher cost tier. Flat panel or slab doors use a single piece of material for the entire door face with no frame or visible joints. This minimalist style defines modern and contemporary kitchens. Flat panel doors can be produced inexpensively from MDF with a thermofoil or laminate finish, or at premium prices from book-matched veneers. Glass insert doors replace the center panel with clear, frosted, or textured glass, often with mullion dividers. Beadboard doors add vertical V-grooves to the center panel for a cottage or farmhouse character.
