Thread Types

Understanding Thread Geometry

Thread design determines how a fastener engages with the substrate material, how much holding power it develops, and whether it can be removed without damaging the material. The thread pitch (distance between threads), thread depth, and thread profile all contribute to performance characteristics. Selecting the correct thread type for the material being fastened is critical: using a coarse-thread wood screw in metal will strip, while using a self-tapping screw where a self-drilling screw is needed will waste time and break screws.

Coarse threads have wider spacing between thread crests, providing greater material engagement in softer substrates like wood, particle board, and drywall. The large thread surface area resists pull-out effectively and tolerates the irregular grain structure of natural wood. Coarse threads also drive faster since fewer rotations are required to seat the screw. Fine threads have tighter spacing, producing more threads per inch of fastener length. They are designed for harder materials like hardwoods, metal, and machine-threaded holes. The increased thread count provides superior vibration resistance and allows finer torque adjustment. Fine thread screws are less likely to split hardwoods than coarse thread screws of the same diameter.

Self-tapping screws cut or form their own mating threads in the material as they are driven. Type A and AB sheet metal screws are the most common self-tapping fasteners with spaced threads and sharp points that cut into thin metal, plastic, and fiberglass. Self-drilling screws, also called TEK screws, have a drill-point tip that eliminates the need for pre-drilling. The drill tip length is numbered: TEK 3 for 14-20 gauge steel, TEK 5 for 12-14 gauge. Hi-Lo threads feature alternating high and low thread profiles, allowing the screw to seat quickly through the outer layer with the low threads while the high threads provide strong engagement in the base material. Hi-Lo screws are popular for fastening drywall to steel studs and for plastic-to-metal assemblies.
