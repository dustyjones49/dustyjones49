Anchor Bolt Types

Mechanical and Chemical Concrete Anchors

Anchor bolts are critical for securing structural components, equipment, and fixtures to concrete or masonry. The selection of the right anchor type depends on the base material (concrete vs. masonry), the applied load (tension, shear, or both), whether the load is static or dynamic, and the environmental conditions. Wedge anchors (also called expansion anchors) are one of the most common heavy-duty anchors. They consist of a threaded stud with a tapered mandrel at the bottom and a separate expansion clip. When the nut is tightened, the clip is forced up the taper and expands against the walls of a pre-drilled hole in the concrete, creating a mechanical interlock. Wedge anchors are used in solid concrete only (not block or brick) and are available in diameters from 1/4 to 1-1/4 inch with embedment depths from 1-1/8 to 5-1/2 inches. The minimum concrete thickness must be at least 1.5 times the embedment depth. Sleeve anchors are similar but have an expansion sleeve that runs the full length of the embedment, making them suitable for both concrete and masonry (brick and block). Drop-in anchors are internally threaded female anchors that are hammer-set into a pre-drilled hole with a setting tool that expands an internal plug; a threaded rod or bolt is then threaded into the anchor. They are used for flush-surface applications and overhead installations. Epoxy anchors use a two-part adhesive injected into a clean, drilled hole before inserting threaded rod or rebar. Epoxy achieves the highest load values of any anchor type, can be placed close to edges and in cracked concrete, and provides excellent resistance to vibration and dynamic loading. However, epoxy must cure at specific temperature ranges and requires a longer installation time. In threaded rod hold-down systems for seismic shear walls, epoxy-set rods have become the standard over wedge anchors due to their superior performance in cracked concrete.

Installation and Edge Distance Requirements

All concrete anchors require minimum edge distances and spacings to achieve their published load values. Per ACI 318 and manufacturers' data, wedge anchors typically require a minimum edge distance of 5 to 10 anchor diameters and spacing of 10 anchor diameters. Anchors placed closer to an edge have reduced capacity due to concrete breakout failure. The hole must be drilled with a carbide-tipped masonry bit in a hammer drill to the specified diameter and depth, and the hole must be cleaned of all dust and debris (blow out with compressed air, brush, blow again). Epoxy anchors add an additional requirement that the hole must be absolutely clean for proper bond. In overhead installations, special injection pistons ensure the epoxy fills the hole without air pockets. Torque wrenches are essential for setting mechanical anchors to the manufacturer's torque specification without over-tightening and stripping the concrete.

  • Wedge anchor edge distance: min 5x diameter (often 10x for full capacity)
  • Hole cleaning: blow, brush, blow (wet/dry vacuum if using epoxy)
  • Minimum concrete thickness: 1.5x embedment depth
  • Torque: follow manufacturer spec (typically 10-90 ft-lbs depending on size)
  • Galvanized or stainless required for exterior or wet environments
  • Never use wedge anchors in hollow block (will split the web)
