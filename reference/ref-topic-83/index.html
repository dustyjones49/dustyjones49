Building Permit Requirements

When Permits Are Required

Building permits exist to ensure that construction meets minimum safety standards established by adopted building codes. The International Residential Code, adopted with amendments by most jurisdictions, defines the scope of work that requires a permit. While exact requirements vary by local jurisdiction, the following general rules apply across most of the United States. Failing to obtain required permits can result in stop-work orders, fines, difficulty selling the property, and potential liability for code violations.

Structural changes almost always require a permit. This includes any modification to load-bearing walls, beams, columns, roof framing, or foundation elements. Removing a wall, even an interior non-load-bearing wall, requires a permit in many jurisdictions if electrical or plumbing is involved. Additions that increase the building footprint or conditioned floor area require permits regardless of size. New window or door openings cut into exterior walls, and enlarging existing openings, require structural review and permits. Roof replacements typically require a permit, though some jurisdictions exempt like-for-like shingle replacements of less than two layers.

Electrical work of any significance requires a permit. The National Electrical Code is enforced through local permitting and inspection. Replacing a light fixture or outlet generally does not require a permit, but running new circuits, adding subpanels, or upgrading service capacity always does. Plumbing permits are required for any work that modifies the drainage, waste, vent, or water supply system. Replacing a faucet or toilet does not require a permit, but moving fixtures, adding new fixtures, or replacing water heaters does. Mechanical permits cover HVAC equipment: replacing a furnace, adding air conditioning, or installing new ductwork all require permits. Decks above a certain height, typically 30 inches above grade, require permits with structural plans. Fences over 6 feet, retaining walls over 4 feet, and any swimming pool deeper than 24 inches all trigger permit requirements in most jurisdictions.
