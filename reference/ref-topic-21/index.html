Outlet Types Reference

Common Residential Receptacle Types

Electrical receptacles (outlets) come in many configurations to meet different safety, functional, and code requirements. The standard 15-amp duplex receptacle (NEMA 5-15R) has two vertical parallel slots (hot and neutral) plus a round U-ground hole and is the most common outlet in North American homes. Its face is typically rated for 15 amps, but code allows 15A receptacles on 20A circuits if more than one receptacle is on the circuit. True 20-amp receptacles (NEMA 5-20R) have one of the vertical slots replaced with a T-shaped slot that accepts both 15A parallel and 20A horizontal blade plugs, and must be served by a dedicated 20A circuit breaker with 12 AWG wire. GFCI (Ground Fault Circuit Interrupter) receptacles are identifiable by their TEST and RESET buttons and are required in wet or damp locations; they can protect downstream regular receptacles through the LOAD terminals. Tamper-resistant (TR) receptacles have internal spring-loaded shutters that block insertion of a single object (like a child's key or paperclip) into one slot while allowing a normal two-prong plug to enter simultaneously; the NEC has required TR receptacles in all dwelling unit locations since 2008. Weather-resistant (WR) receptacles add corrosion-resistant components and UV-stabilized bodies for outdoor use and are required for all outdoor receptacles, including those in weatherproof covers.

Specialty and High-Voltage Receptacles

Beyond standard 120-volt receptacles, several specialty configurations serve specific appliances and equipment. A 240-volt dryer uses a NEMA 14-30R (4-prong) receptacle on a dedicated 30A circuit. Electric ranges use a NEMA 14-50R (4-prong, 50A) receptacle. Older installations with 3-prong (NEMA 10-30R and 10-50R) are grandfathered but should be upgraded to 4-prong during renovation per current code. RV hookups use a NEMA TT-30R (120V 30A) for smaller RVs and a NEMA 14-50R for larger units. EV chargers typically use NEMA 14-50R or L14-30R locking receptacles. Twist-lock receptacles (L5, L6 series) are used in commercial kitchens and industrial settings where vibration could disconnect straight-blade plugs. Receptacles in wet locations must have an in-use weatherproof cover that remains weatherproof with a cord plugged in. Receptacles in damp locations (under an overhang) may use a weatherproof cover that is weatherproof when closed only.

  • Dryer: NEMA 14-30R, 30A, 4-prong (modern standard)
  • Range: NEMA 14-50R, 50A, 4-prong
  • RV: NEMA TT-30R (30A 120V) or 14-50R (50A 240V)
  • EV Charger: NEMA 14-50R, 50A, or hardwired
  • Countertop spacing: no point more than 24 in from a receptacle
  • AFCI or GFCI receptacle can protect downstream outlets via LOAD terminals
  • 2020 NEC: GFCI required for 250V receptacles in certain wet/damp locations
