Paint Coverage Rates

Coverage Per Gallon by Paint Type

Paint coverage varies significantly depending on the sheen, substrate texture, application method, and the color being covered. Manufacturers list theoretical coverage on the label (typically 350-400 square feet per gallon), but real-world application nearly always yields less due to surface porosity, texture, and the need for two coats. Flat and matte paints provide the best coverage — approximately 350 to 400 square feet per gallon on smooth drywall — because their higher pigment-to-binder ratio fills surface imperfections better. Eggshell sheen covers about 300 to 350 square feet per gallon, satin covers 300 to 350, and semi-gloss covers 250 to 300 square feet per gallon. High-gloss paints have the lowest coverage, about 200 to 250 square feet per gallon, because the glossy binder reduces pigment loading. Primer covers roughly 250 to 350 square feet per gallon, with drywall primer at the higher end and stain-blocking primer at the lower end. Textured surfaces like stucco, brick, or rough-sawn wood can reduce coverage by 30 to 50% compared to smooth drywall. Spray application typically wastes 10 to 20% more material through overspray compared to rolling. Dark color changes over light or vice versa will almost always require two full coats plus a tinted primer for best results.

How to Estimate Paint Quantity

To estimate gallons needed, calculate the wall square footage (perimeter of room times height), subtract door and window areas, then divide by the coverage rate for your chosen sheen. A 12x12-foot room with 8-foot ceilings has 384 square feet of wall area. With flat paint at 350 square feet per gallon, one coat requires 1.1 gallons — so buy 2 gallons for one coat, 4 for two coats. Ceilings are calculated separately: multiply room length by width. For trim and doors, figure 15 to 20 square feet per standard door (both sides) and about 2 square feet per linear foot of baseboard. Always round up to the nearest gallon, as leftover paint is useful for touch-ups, and tinted paint cannot be returned. One gallon covers approximately the same area as two 5-gallon buckets of joint compound (mud) cover during drywall finishing — a helpful parallel to remember on-site.

  • Wall area = perimeter x height - openings
  • Ceiling area = length x width
  • Door (both sides) = ~20 sq ft each
  • Baseboard = ~2 sq ft per linear foot
  • Spray application: add 10-20% waste factor
  • Textured surface: reduce coverage by 30-50%
  • One gallon = weight approx 10-12 lbs
