Kitchen Layout Types

The Five Classic Kitchen Layouts

The kitchen layout is the single most important factor determining workflow, efficiency, and enjoyment of the space. Professional kitchen designers and the National Kitchen and Bath Association (NKBA) recognize five primary layout configurations, each suited to different room dimensions and usage patterns. The galley kitchen consists of two parallel runs of cabinets with a walkway between them, typically 36 to 48 inches wide. This layout maximizes efficiency by placing all work zones within a few steps and is the standard for restaurant kitchens and compact apartment kitchens. The minimum room width for a functional galley kitchen is approximately 7 feet (84 inches) to accommodate 24-inch-deep base cabinets on each side plus a 36-inch walkway. An L-shaped kitchen uses two perpendicular walls, creating an open corner. The natural work triangle (sink, cooktop, refrigerator) is easily established by placing the sink on one leg and the range/refrigerator on the other. An L-shaped layout requires a room at least 8 feet wide on each leg to accommodate cabinets and workspace. With an island added at the center, the L-shaped kitchen becomes highly versatile, providing additional preparation area, casual seating, and a buffet serving surface. A U-shaped kitchen wraps cabinets around three walls, creating a highly efficient workspace with counters and storage within easy reach. The minimum width for a U-shaped kitchen is approximately 8 feet (96 inches), and the NKBA recommends a minimum of 42 inches between opposing base cabinets for a single-cook kitchen. For the most spacious configuration, 48-inch aisles allow multiple cooks to work simultaneously. The island kitchen places a freestanding cabinet in the center of the room, surrounded by workspace on all sides. The island becomes the social and functional hub of the kitchen, typically housing the sink or cooktop plus seating for informal dining. Minimum clearance around an island is 42 inches on all sides (48 inches preferred). An island should be at least 4 feet long and 2 feet wide to be functional; islands smaller than this become little more than a cart. The peninsula kitchen is a variation where one leg of a U-shape or L-shape extends into the room like a half-island attached to a wall or run of cabinets. Peninsulas create an open social space while defining the kitchen boundary from the adjacent living or dining area without the full floor space commitment of a stand-alone island. Peninsula overhangs for seating must be at least 12 to 15 inches deep with a finished countertop height of 36 inches (for standard counters) or 42 inches (for bar-height raised sections).

The Work Triangle and NKBA Guidelines

The kitchen work triangle, developed at the University of Illinois School of Architecture in the 1940s, connects the three primary work centers: the sink (preparation and cleanup), the cooktop/range (cooking), and the refrigerator (food storage). The triangle principle holds that the sum of the three legs of the triangle (the distances between the centers of each zone) should be between 12 and 22 feet total — close enough to minimize walking but far enough to allow two people to work without crowding each other. No leg of the triangle should be less than 4 feet or more than 9 feet. The triangle should not be intersected by major traffic paths or an island by more than 12 inches. In modern kitchen design, the single work triangle has evolved into "work zones" that accommodate multiple cooks, baking stations, coffee bars, and smart appliances. Countertop landing areas are required adjacent to each major appliance: at least 15 inches of countertop on the handle side of the refrigerator, 12 to 15 inches on one or both sides of the cooktop, and 18 to 24 inches on one side of the sink. The NKBA publishes comprehensive planning guidelines covering every dimension and clearance in the kitchen, from minimum countertop frontage to microwave placement height (ideally 3 inches below the principal user's shoulder).

  • Work triangle: 12-22 ft total, legs 4-9 ft each
  • Walkway clearance: 42 in (single cook), 48 in (multi-cook)
  • Island clearance: 42 in all sides (48 in preferred)
  • Countertop landing: 15 in beside fridge, 15 in beside cooktop, 24 in beside sink
  • Seating overhang: 12-15 in at 36 in height, 15-18 in at 42 in height
  • Dishwasher placement: within 3 feet of sink, no blocking by open doors
