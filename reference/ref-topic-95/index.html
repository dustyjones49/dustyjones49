Fire Extinguisher Types

Fire Classes and Extinguisher Selection

Fire extinguishers are classified by the types of fires they can safely extinguish. Using the wrong type of extinguisher on a fire can be ineffective at best and dangerous at worst, such as using water on a grease fire, which causes the burning oil to splatter and spread. Every extinguisher is labeled with pictograms indicating the fire classes it is rated for. Multi-purpose extinguishers, typically rated for Classes A, B, and C, are the standard for residential and general commercial use.

Class A fires involve ordinary combustible materials: wood, paper, cloth, rubber, and many plastics. These are the most common fires in homes and offices. Class A extinguishers use water or dry chemical agents to cool the burning material below its ignition temperature. The numerical rating indicates the equivalent water volume. Class B fires involve flammable liquids and gases: gasoline, oil, grease, paint, propane, and solvents. Class B extinguishers use dry chemical agents or carbon dioxide to smother the fire by excluding oxygen. Never use water on a Class B fire. Class C fires involve energized electrical equipment: appliances, wiring, circuit breakers, and power tools. The extinguishing agent must be non-conductive to prevent electrical shock. Once the power source is disconnected, a Class C fire typically becomes a Class A or B fire depending on the burning material.

Class D fires involve combustible metals: magnesium, titanium, sodium, and potassium. These fires burn at extremely high temperatures and require specialized dry powder extinguishing agents. Class D extinguishers are found in laboratories, machine shops, and industrial facilities, not in residential settings. Class K fires involve cooking oils and fats in commercial kitchens. Class K extinguishers use a wet chemical agent that saponifies the burning oil, creating a soapy foam layer that smothers the fire and prevents re-ignition. Class K extinguishers are required in commercial kitchens with deep fat fryers. For home use, an ABC multi-purpose dry chemical extinguisher rated 2A:10B:C is the recommended minimum, with one extinguisher per floor and an additional unit in the kitchen.
