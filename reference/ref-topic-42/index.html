Plywood Grade Guide

Plywood Face Grades and Exposure Durability

Plywood grading uses a letter system for face and back veneers (A through D) combined with an exposure durability classification (Exterior, Exposure 1, Exposure 2, Interior). The face grade indicates the veneer's appearance quality on a descending scale from A (best, virtually defect-free) to D (lowest, allowing large knots and splits). Grade A face veneers are sanded smooth, free of knots, patches, and repairs, and are used for cabinet-grade and furniture-grade plywood where the surface will be stained or clear-finished. Grade B is similar to A but allows small tight knots and minor wood or synthetic repairs (football-shaped patches). Grade C allows tight knots up to 1.5 inches, limited splits, and synthetic or wood repairs; this is the standard grade for construction sheathing's better face. Grade C-Plugged is an improved C grade with all open defects repaired with plugs, used for underlayment (the sub-surface beneath vinyl or tile flooring). Grade D permits knots up to 2.5 inches, open splits, and unrepaired defects; it is the back face of most CDX sheathing panels. The panel grade is written as a two-letter code: the first letter is the face veneer grade and the second is the back veneer grade. AC plywood has an A face with a C back, ideal for cabinet carcasses and shelving where only one side shows. BC plywood has a B face with a C back, also used for cabinets and painted millwork. CDX is C face, D back, with Exposure 1 glue, used for wall and roof sheathing. The exposure durability rating indicates the glue bond's ability to resist moisture. Exterior glue uses fully waterproof phenol-formaldehyde adhesive and can withstand permanent exposure to weather. Exposure 1 uses the same waterproof glue and is rated for limited moisture exposure during construction delays, but the wood veneers themselves are not treated for weather exposure. Exposure 2 uses moisture-resistant glue for occasional dampness but is not rated for sheathing left exposed during construction. Interior glue bonds will fail with any significant moisture exposure.

Structural Performance and Span Ratings

Structural plywood panels carry an APA span rating that indicates the maximum roof or floor joist spacing in inches. For example, a 32/16 span rating means the panel is rated for a maximum 32-inch support spacing when used as roof sheathing and 16-inch support spacing when used as subfloor. The panels are also thickness-rated (19/32, 23/32, etc.) to correspond with these structural ratings. Tongue-and-groove edges lock adjacent subfloor panels together to span without blocking. For subfloor applications with 16-inch OC joist spacing, a minimum 19/32 inch (5/8 CAT) T&G plywood is required.

  • Span rating (e.g., 32/16): roof max 32 in OC / floor max 16 in OC
  • Subfloor minimum: 19/32 in (5/8 in) T&G for 16 in OC joists
  • PS1: voluntary product standard for construction and industrial plywood
  • PS2: performance standard for wood-based structural panels (includes OSB)
  • Marine-grade: A-A or A-B faces with waterproof glue, no core voids
  • Hardwood plywood: graded per HPVA/ANSI standards (different system)
