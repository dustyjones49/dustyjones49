Decking Material Comparison

Cost, Lifespan, and Maintenance by Material

Choosing the right decking material involves balancing initial cost against long-term maintenance, appearance, and lifespan. Pressure-treated (PT) southern yellow pine is the most economical option at approximately $2-4 per square foot for the decking material. It requires annual or biennial maintenance including cleaning with a deck brightener and application of a penetrating stain or water-repellent sealer. Without maintenance, PT pine will weather to a gray, checked, and splintered surface within 2-3 years. With proper care, PT pine decks last 15-20 years. Cedar and redwood are the premium natural wood options at $4-8 per square foot. They are naturally resistant to decay and insects due to their heartwood extractives (thujaplicins in cedar, tannins in redwood), and they are dimensionally more stable than PT pine, producing a smoother, splinter-free surface. They require similar maintenance (cleaning and clear sealer or semi-transparent stain every 1-3 years) and last 20-25 years in above-ground applications. Composite decking (Trex, TimberTech, Fiberon) at $5-10 per square foot is made from recycled wood fibers or rice hulls mixed with polyethylene or polypropylene plastic. First-generation composites (1990s-2000s) were notorious for mold staining, fading, and swelling, but modern capped composites feature a PVC or ASA polymer outer shell co-extruded over the composite core that provides superior stain, fade, and scratch resistance. Composite decks require only periodic soap-and-water cleaning and last 25-30+ years, with the primary trade-off being a higher initial cost offset by near-zero annual maintenance expense and no splinters. PVC decking (Azek, Wolf, Zuri) at $7-12 per square foot is 100% synthetic with no organic fillers, making it completely impervious to moisture and mold. PVC decks are the lightest-weight option, have the highest color retention, and stay cooler underfoot than dark composites. Lifetime warranties of 30-50 years are common. Aluminum decking at $12-18+ per square foot is the premium option with zero maintenance, the highest strength-to-weight ratio, and complete resistance to rot, insects, and fire. Aluminum is used primarily in high-end coastal and wildfire-prone areas. The coating system (powder-coat) provides long-term color with minimal fading. The downside is a higher cost, an industrial look that not everyone prefers, and potential for denting from heavy impacts.

Installation and Structural Considerations

Regardless of the decking material, the substructure (footings, posts, beams, joists) carries the full load and determines the structural lifespan. PT pine joists remain the standard substructure, but ground-contact rated lumber (UC4A or UC4B) must be used for joists within 6 inches of grade. Joist spacing is typically 16 inches OC for wood decking running perpendicular. Composite and PVC manufacturers often require 12 inches on center for diagonal or stair installations, and some high-end products require only 16 inches for perpendicular layouts. Joist tape (butyl self-adhering flashing tape) applied to joist tops dramatically extends substructure life by preventing water from penetrating screw holes and sitting on horizontal surfaces.

  • Joist spacing: 16 in OC (wood), 12-16 in OC (composite per manufacturer)
  • Joist tape: highly recommended on all joist tops, beams, and ledger
  • Hidden fasteners: standard for groove-edge composite and PVC
  • Fascia boards: match or complement decking for finished perimeter
  • Stair stringers: treated wood (UC4A min), composite/PVC treads
  • Permit: required for any deck over 30 in above grade (IRC requirement)
