LVL and Glulam Beam Maximum Spans

Laminated Veneer Lumber (LVL) and glued-laminated timber (glulam) are engineered wood products that provide superior strength and stiffness compared to solid sawn lumber of the same dimensions. They are manufactured by bonding thin wood veneers (LVL) or dimension lumber laminations (glulam) with moisture-resistant adhesives under heat and pressure. LVL beams are typically available in 1.75-inch thicknesses with depths ranging from 5.5 inches (2-ply 2x6 equivalent) to 24 inches or more, with plies nailed or bolted together to create wider beams. A double 1.75x9.25-inch LVL (equivalent to a built-up 2x10) can span an opening up to 15 feet for a typical residential floor load. Glulam beams are available in larger dimensions and longer spans and are often used as exposed architectural beams in exposed ridge and cathedral ceiling designs. The span capacity of beams depends on the load being supported, which can include roof load, floor load, or concentrated loads from columns and posts. The values below are approximate maximum spans for LVL beams supporting a tributary width of 20 feet of floor load (40 PSF live + 10 PSF dead) at L/360 deflection. Spans for roof-only loads (with no floor load) are typically 25-40% longer. Always consult the manufacturer's specific engineering tables for the exact beam product being used, as LVL properties vary by manufacturer (LP SolidStart, GP Lam, Boise Cascade, Weyerhaeuser Microllam, etc.) Glulam beams follow APA standards and are graded by layup combination symbols (e.g., 24F-V4 for Douglas Fir).

Beam Bearing and Connection Requirements

Beams must have adequate bearing area at each end support. The minimum end bearing for LVL beams is 1.75 inches (3 inches for glulam). The bearing plate or sill must distribute the beam reaction load across the wood bearing area without exceeding the allowable compression perpendicular to grain of the wall plates (typically 425 PSI for SPF, 565 PSI for SYP). Floor beams supporting multiple joist spans may require a column or post at intermediate points, sized per load. Built-up beams using nailed 2x lumber must follow IRC fastener schedules — typically 10d nails in two rows staggered at 16 inches OC top and bottom, with a minimum of three plies where joists bear on the beam. Beams supporting concentrated loads (posts bearing on the beam mid-span) must be checked rigorously using the manufacturer's engineering software or an engineer's calculation.

  • LVL bearing minimum: 1.75 in on wood, 3 in on masonry
  • Glulam bearing minimum: 3 in on wood, 4 in on masonry
  • Built-up 2x beam nailing: 10d nails, 2 rows, 16 in OC staggered
  • Bearing capacity: SPF = 425 PSI, SYP = 565 PSI (compression perpendicular)
  • Beam reaction = load area (sq ft) x total load (PSF)
  • Flush beams: use hangers sized for the joist and beam depth
