Home Inspection Checklist

What Inspectors Evaluate

A professional home inspection is a visual examination of the physical structure and systems of a home, from roof to foundation. Inspectors follow standards of practice established by organizations such as the American Society of Home Inspectors and the International Association of Certified Home Inspectors. A thorough inspection typically takes 2 to 4 hours for an average-sized home and results in a detailed written report with photographs documenting conditions and defects. Understanding what inspectors check helps sellers prepare and buyers anticipate potential issues.

The roof inspection examines the roof covering material for damage, wear, and remaining service life. Inspectors check shingles for curling, cracking, missing granules, and moss growth. They examine flashing around chimneys, vents, and valleys for proper installation and seal. Gutters and downspouts are checked for proper slope, secure attachment, and functional drainage. Inside the attic, inspectors look for evidence of roof leaks, adequate ventilation, proper insulation depth and coverage, and any structural issues with trusses or rafters. The foundation inspection covers the visible portions of the foundation walls and floor slab, looking for cracks exceeding 1/4 inch, signs of water intrusion, efflorescence indicating moisture migration, and any evidence of structural movement such as sticking doors or sloping floors.

The electrical inspection verifies that the service entrance, main panel, and branch circuits are properly sized and safely installed. Inspectors check for double-tapped breakers, aluminum branch wiring, ungrounded three-prong outlets, missing GFCI protection in wet areas, and evidence of overheating. The plumbing inspection examines water supply piping for leaks, corrosion, and adequate pressure. Drain lines are checked for proper slope and venting. The water heater is inspected for proper installation, temperature-pressure relief valve, and signs of leakage. The HVAC inspection covers the furnace, air conditioner, and ductwork: age, condition, temperature differential across the coil, air filter condition, and evidence of combustion gas backdrafting in fossil fuel appliances.
