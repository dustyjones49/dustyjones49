Rafter Span Tables

Rafter Maximum Spans by Size and Snow Load

Rafter span tables published in the IRC provide maximum allowable horizontal projection lengths for roof rafters based on lumber species, grade, size, spacing, and ground snow load. Unlike floor joists, rafters carry dead load (roofing, sheathing, insulation) plus a variable live load determined by the ground snow load (Pg) in pounds per square foot for the building location. Dead load is typically assumed to be 10 PSF for asphalt shingles and 15 PSF for heavier tile or slate roofing. The values below assume #2 grade Douglas Fir-Larch (common in western U.S.) or Southern Yellow Pine (common in eastern U.S.) with a ceiling not attached to the rafters (non-load-bearing ceiling joists). If ceiling joists are attached and the attic is used for storage, they must be designed as a combined rafter-ceiling joist system or use engineered trusses. Rafter span is measured as the horizontal projection from the exterior wall bearing point to the ridge board bearing point. The actual rafter length along the slope is the horizontal span divided by the cosine of the pitch angle, then multiplied by the pitch multiplier. All spans assume a minimum bearing length of 1.5 inches. Heavier snow loads dramatically reduce allowable rafter spans: a 2x8 rafter at 16 inches OC can span 13 feet 6 inches under a 20 PSF snow load but drops to only 10 feet 9 inches under a 70 PSF snow load. In high-snow load regions, 2x10 and 2x12 rafters become standard, and engineered trusses are the norm for new construction.

Rafter Design Details

Rafters are typically supported at the ridge by a ridge board (non-structural, 1x or 2x member used to align rafters) or a ridge beam (structural beam designed to carry roof load). In a ridge board system, rafters are paired across the ridge and secured to each other with collar ties in the upper third of the rafter span and ceiling joists or rafter ties in the lower third to resist outward thrust. Rafter ties must be sized per the IRC Rafter Span Table R802.4 for resisting horizontal thrust at the wall top plates. Birdsmouth cuts at the rafter heel bearing on the wall top plate typically remove a wedge of 1.5 to 1.75 inches vertically. The remaining "heel height" of the rafter above the birdsmouth must be at least 3.5 inches to maintain structural integrity at the bearing point. Eaves projections beyond the exterior wall are limited to 2 feet for 2x6 rafters and up to 4 feet for 2x12 rafters per the IRC.

  • Dead load assumption: 10 PSF (asphalt), 15 PSF (tile/slate)
  • Birdsmouth depth: max 1/3 of rafter depth; heel min 3.5 in
  • Collar ties: upper third of rafter height, min 1x4
  • Rafter ties: lower third, resist wall thrust
  • Ridge board: non-structural alignment only (1x or 2x)
  • Ridge beam: structural, designed per IRC R802.3
  • Eaves overhang max: 2 ft for 2x6, 3 ft for 2x8, 4 ft for larger
