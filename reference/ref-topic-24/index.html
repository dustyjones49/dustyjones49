Pipe Material Types

Residential Plumbing Pipe Materials

Plumbing pipe selection depends on the application (supply vs. drain), water quality, local code, and cost. Copper has been the premium standard for residential water supply lines for decades. Type M (thinnest wall, red stripe) is used for residential above-ground supply, Type L (medium wall, blue stripe) for residential and commercial, and Type K (thickest wall, green stripe) for underground water service. Copper is joined by sweat-soldering using lead-free solder, or with press-connect (ProPress) fittings. PEX (cross-linked polyethylene) has rapidly gained market share since the 1990s because it is flexible, freeze-resistant, and dramatically faster to install. PEX comes in three types: PEX-A (the most flexible, highest cross-linking), PEX-B (most common, good chlorine resistance), and PEX-C (least expensive). A PEX home-run manifold system uses a central distribution panel with individual lines to each fixture for balanced pressure and no hidden fittings behind walls. PVC (polyvinyl chloride) is used for cold water supply, DWV (drain, waste, vent), and irrigation. Schedule 40 is the standard, with Schedule 80 having thicker walls for higher pressure. CPVC (chlorinated polyvinyl chloride) is the PVC variant rated for hot water (up to 200 degrees F). Galvanized steel was common before 1960 but rusts internally over time, reducing flow and causing water discoloration. Cast iron service weight pipe is the traditional noise-dampening drain and sewer material used in multi-story buildings for its superior sound isolation compared to PVC.

Code and Sizing Considerations

Water supply pipe sizing follows the UPC or IPC fixture unit method, starting at the water meter and working back through branch lines to each fixture. Minimum supply line sizes: main line typically 3/4 inch or 1 inch, branched fixtures at 1/2 inch. PEX maintains full flow better than copper because its smooth interior resist scaling. Copper is susceptible to pinhole leaks in acidic water (pH below 6.5). PEX should not be exposed to direct sunlight for extended periods as UV degrades the material. CPVC must use the correct solvent cement specifically rated for CPVC (typically yellow/orange glue), not PVC cement. Galvanized pipe is no longer permitted for new residential water distribution by most codes due to internal corrosion. ABS (acrylonitrile butadiene styrene) black pipe is an alternative to PVC for DWV in some regions but is banned in others due to fire performance concerns.

  • Main supply: 3/4 in or 1 in minimum
  • Branch lines: 1/2 in minimum for most fixtures
  • PEX cannot be used within 18 in of a water heater
  • Copper requires dielectric union when connecting to steel
  • PEX requires plastic or brass fittings (metal clamps must be approved)
  • PVC exposed above grade must be painted for UV protection
  • No-lead: all potable water pipe and fittings must contain less than 0.25% lead
