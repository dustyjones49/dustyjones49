Roof Pitch Common Values

Roof Pitch Ratios, Angles, and Applications

Roof pitch is expressed as the ratio of vertical rise (in inches) to horizontal run (in inches) over a 12-inch span. A 4/12 pitch means the roof rises 4 inches for every 12 inches of horizontal distance. The pitch determines water-shedding ability, material compatibility, attic space, and structural loading. Low-slope roofs between 0.25/12 and 3/12 are considered flat or low-slope and require membrane roofing such as EPDM, TPO, or modified bitumen because shingles rely on gravity to shed water and are not rated for pitches below 2/12. The minimum pitch for asphalt shingles is 2/12, provided a double layer of underlayment is installed per manufacturer specifications. For standard shingle warranty, 4/12 is the minimum. Steep-slope roofs above 8/12 shed snow efficiently but are more dangerous and expensive to install due to increased safety measures, higher material waste from cuts, and additional engineered bracing. The most common roof pitches in American homes are between 4/12 and 8/12. Very steep pitches (10/12 to 12/12) are found on Cape Cod, Victorian, and A-frame designs where usable attic space is desired. The pitch angle in degrees is found using the arctangent of (rise / run).

Material Requirements by Pitch

Roofing material manufacturers set minimum slope requirements for warranty validation. Metal roofing (standing seam) can typically be installed on slopes as low as 1/12 with proper sealants, or 3/12 without sealant in the seams. Wood shakes and shingles require 3/12 minimum pitch. Clay and concrete tile require 2.5/12 to 4/12 depending on underlayment type and whether a batten system is used. Slate requires 4/12 minimum. Roofers use a pitch multiplier to calculate the actual roof area from the plan (footprint) area. The multiplier is the inverse cosine of the pitch angle. For example, a 6/12 pitch (26.6 degrees) has a multiplier of 1.118, meaning the roof area is 11.8% larger than the plan area.

  • Asphalt shingles: 2/12 min (double underlayment), 4/12 standard
  • Metal (standing seam): 1/12 min, 3/12 without sealants
  • Wood shakes/shingles: 3/12 min
  • Clay/concrete tile: 2.5/12 - 4/12 min
  • Slate: 4/12 min
  • Built-up roofing (BUR): flat to 3/12
  • Membrane (EPDM/TPO): flat to 2/12
