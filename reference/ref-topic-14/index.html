ASTM C270 Mortar Types and Applications

Mortar for masonry is classified by ASTM C270 into five types — M, S, N, O, and K — using the mnemonic "MaSoN wOrK" from highest to lowest strength. Type M is the strongest at 2,500 PSI minimum and is used for heavy loads, below-grade applications, and retaining walls. Type S (1,800 PSI) is the workhorse for structural masonry, foundations, and areas with high wind or seismic loads. Type N (750 PSI) is the general-purpose mortar for above-grade walls, veneers, and chimneys; it provides the best balance of strength and workability. Type O (350 PSI) is low-strength mortar used for non-load-bearing interior partitions and historic restoration because it is softer than the masonry units, allowing it to absorb movement. Type K (75 PSI) has very low strength and is rarely used in new construction, reserved for historic preservation where the mortar must be sacrificial to the brick or stone. Each type can be specified by proportion (recipe) or by property (performance). Proportion specification uses the ratio of Portland cement to hydrated lime (or lime putty) to sand. Masonry cement and mortar cement are factory-blended alternatives that replace Portland cement and lime with a single bagged product meeting the same ASTM requirements.

Selecting the Right Mortar

Mortar selection must balance strength, bond, and workability. The mortar should always be weaker than the masonry units so that any cracking occurs in the mortar joints rather than the brick or block — this is the "sacrificial" principle of masonry design. For most residential veneer walls, Type N is the correct choice. For retaining walls and foundations, Type S provides the needed strength and durability. In freeze-thaw climates, air-entrained mortar with 7-12% air content improves frost resistance. Mortar must be used within 2.5 hours of mixing (retempering with water is allowed within that window) and must not be used after the initial set has begun. Hot weather may reduce the working window to 1.5 hours.

  • General rule: mortar must be weaker than the masonry unit
  • Type N: best all-around for residential above-grade
  • Type S: required for foundations, retaining walls, seismic zones D-F
  • Type M: engineered structures, high compressive loads
  • Type O: historic restoration, interior non-load-bearing
  • Pot life: 2.5 hours max (ASTM C270), retemper with water only once
  • Sand must be clean, well-graded, ASTM C144 compliant
