Hinge Types

Selecting Door Hinges

Hinges are the critical interface between a door and its frame, bearing the full weight of the door through thousands of open-close cycles over their service life. The hinge type must be matched to the door weight, material, frequency of use, and aesthetic requirements. Undersized or improperly specified hinges lead to sagging doors, binding, and premature wear on both the hinge and the door frame.

Butt hinges are the standard for interior and exterior residential doors. They consist of two rectangular leaves joined by a removable pin, with one leaf mortised into the door edge and the other into the frame. Standard residential butt hinges measure 3.5 inches for interior doors and 4 inches for exterior doors. A typical interior door uses three hinges, while solid-core or tall doors may require four to prevent warping. Concealed hinges, also called European hinges or cup hinges, are invisible when the door is closed. They mount into a 35mm bored hole in the back of the door and attach to a base plate on the cabinet frame. Concealed hinges offer three-way adjustment for perfect alignment and are the standard for frameless and face-frame cabinetry.

Piano hinges, also called continuous hinges, run the full length of the door edge, distributing weight evenly and providing exceptional strength for heavy or frequently used doors. They are common on tool chests, desk lids, and commercial doors. Strap hinges have long decorative leaves that extend across the door face, providing both function and rustic aesthetic. Historically made of wrought iron, modern strap hinges are common on barn doors, gates, and farmhouse-style cabinetry. Spring hinges contain a spring mechanism that automatically closes the door without a separate door closer. They are required by code on fire-rated doors between garages and living spaces. Pivot hinges mount to the top and bottom of the door rather than the side edge, allowing for floor-to-ceiling doors and heavy commercial entry doors that would overwhelm conventional side-mounted hinges.
