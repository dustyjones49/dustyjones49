Rebar Size Chart

Rebar Sizes from #3 to #18

Reinforcing bar (rebar) is sized using a designation number that corresponds to the nominal diameter in eighths of an inch for sizes #3 through #8. A #3 bar is nominally 3/8 inch in diameter, #4 is 4/8 inch (1/2 inch), #5 is 5/8 inch, and so on up to #8 (1 inch diameter). For bars #9 and larger, the designation does not directly equal eighths of an inch; instead the bar number approximates the cross-sectional area in square inches divided by 0.79. These larger bars are used in heavy commercial, industrial, and infrastructure projects like bridge piers, retaining walls, and high-rise column cages. The weight per linear foot is directly proportional to the cross-sectional area since steel has a density of 490 pounds per cubic foot. A practical field formula is: weight (lbs per ft) = (diameter in inches) squared divided by 2.92. Rebar grades indicate the minimum yield strength: Grade 40 (40,000 PSI yield) was the historical standard but is now largely replaced by Grade 60 (60,000 PSI yield), which is the universal standard for most residential and commercial concrete reinforcement. Grade 75, 80, and 100 are available for high-strength applications. Rebar is identified by markings rolled into the bar surface at the mill: the main rib pattern identifies the producing mill, the bar size, the steel type (S = carbon steel, W = low-alloy), and the grade (60 = 60,000 PSI, or a single line for Grade 60). Epoxy-coated rebar (green in color) provides corrosion protection for bridge decks, parking structures, and marine environments. Stainless steel rebar offers the ultimate corrosion resistance at a premium price.

Rebar Placement and Cover Requirements

The Concrete Reinforcing Steel Institute (CRSI) and ACI 318 code specify minimum concrete cover over rebar to protect against corrosion and provide adequate fire resistance. For concrete cast against and permanently exposed to earth, minimum cover is 3 inches. For concrete exposed to weather or earth but formed: 1.5 inches for #5 and smaller, 2 inches for #6 and larger. For interior slabs, walls, and joists not exposed to weather: 3/4 inch for the slab top and bottom. Rebar laps (splices) must overlap by a calculated development length, typically 30 to 60 bar diameters depending on concrete strength, bar grade, and whether the bar is in tension or compression. For Grade 60 #4 bar in 3,000 PSI concrete, a Class B tension lap splice is approximately 30 inches. Rebar is supported on chairs or dobies at the correct cover depth before placing concrete.

  • Grade 60 rebar: 60,000 PSI yield (standard)
  • Cast against earth: 3 in cover
  • Exterior formed: 1.5 in cover (#5 and smaller), 2 in (#6+ up)
  • Interior slab: 3/4 in cover
  • Lap splice: ~30 in for #4 Grade 60 in 3000 PSI concrete (tension)
  • Chairs/dobies required to maintain cover during pour
