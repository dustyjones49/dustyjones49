Deck Board Spacing Guide

Recommended Gaps by Material

Deck board spacing serves two essential functions: allowing water drainage through the deck surface and accommodating the natural expansion and contraction of the boards with changes in moisture and temperature. Proper spacing prevents cupping, warping, and buckling over the life of the deck. For traditional kiln-dried pressure-treated pine, a gap of 1/8 inch is standard when installed, but the actual installed gap depends on the moisture content at the time of installation. If PT lumber is installed wet (fresh from the lumber yard, often with 19%+ moisture content), the boards should be butted tightly together (no gap) because they will shrink significantly as they dry. If the PT lumber is kiln-dried after treatment (KDAT), install with a 1/8-inch gap. For cedar and redwood, which are naturally dimensionally stable but still expand and contract with humidity, a 1/8 to 3/16-inch gap is recommended. Hardwood decking (ipe, cumaru, tigerwood) requires a smaller gap of 1/16 to 1/8 inch due to its inherent stability and density. Composite decking (Trex, TimberTech, Fiberon) has specific manufacturer spacing requirements that must be followed exactly to maintain warranty. Most composite manufacturers require a 3/16 to 1/4 inch gap between boards and 1/8 to 3/16 inch at the ends (butt joints). PVC decking (Azek, Wolf) typically requires a 1/4 inch gap due to its higher coefficient of thermal expansion. Composite and PVC decks should be fastened with hidden fastener systems or color-matched face screws with a spacing tool to ensure consistent gaps. Butt joints on composite boards should be reinforced with a double joist or splice block beneath.

Installation Best Practices

Consistent spacing is achieved with a carpenter's pencil (about 3/8 inch), a 16d nail as a temporary spacer, or purpose-made plastic spacers. For face-screwed wood decks, the screw pattern should be two screws per joist intersection, placed about 1 inch from each board edge. Pre-drilling and countersinking screw heads in dense hardwoods prevents "mushrooming" around the screw. Hidden fastener systems for grooved composite and PVC boards use clips that automatically set the gap, simplifying installation. Always read the manufacturer's installation guide for the specific product being installed — composite manufacturers void warranties for incorrect gapping. Deck boards should be laid perpendicular to joists; diagonal patterns require joist spacing not exceeding 12 inches on center. Run boards with the best-looking face upward and slightly crown up (like framing lumber). Stagger butt joints in adjacent rows so no two joints on the same joist bay align within four rows.

  • Face screws: 2 per joist intersection, 1 in from board edges
  • Hidden fasteners: set gap automatically (best for composite)
  • Diagonal decking: joist spacing 12 in OC max
  • Stagger butt joints: minimum 4 rows offset
  • Airflow under deck: minimum 18 in clearance to grade (PT wood), 12 in (composite)
  • Board orientation: "bark side up" (growth rings curve up) minimizes cupping
