Washer Types

Washer Functions and Selection

Washers are deceptively simple components that perform several critical functions in a fastened assembly. They distribute the clamping load of a bolt or nut across a larger surface area, preventing the fastener head from embedding into soft materials like wood or aluminum. They protect the material surface from marring during tightening. And, in the case of lock washers, they resist vibration-induced loosening by maintaining tension on the fastener threads. Choosing the correct washer type can mean the difference between a joint that stays tight for decades and one that fails after months of service.

Flat washers, also called plain washers, are the most common type. They are thin, disc-shaped plates with a central hole, available in steel, stainless steel, brass, and nylon. Their primary purpose is load distribution, creating a larger bearing surface that reduces the pressure exerted on the clamped material. Flat washers are specified with USS (wider) or SAE (narrower) dimensions. Fender washers are oversized flat washers with an outer diameter significantly larger than standard washers of the same bolt size. They are designed specifically for distributing load over thin or soft materials like sheet metal, fiberglass, and plastic where a standard washer would pull through.

Lock washers prevent rotational loosening under vibration. Split lock washers, also called helical spring lock washers, have a split ring design that compresses flat during tightening, creating spring tension that bites into both the nut face and the material surface. Their effectiveness is debated in engineering circles; they work best on softer materials where the edges can dig in. Internal tooth lock washers have serrations around the inside diameter that grip the fastener shank, while external tooth washers have teeth on the outside that grip the material. Finishing washers, also called countersunk washers or cup washers, have a recessed top that accepts a flat-head screw, creating a clean, finished appearance for visible fasteners in furniture and cabinetry.
