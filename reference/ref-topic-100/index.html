Universal Design Principles

Designing for All Abilities

Universal design creates environments that can be used by all people, regardless of age, size, ability, or disability. Unlike accessibility standards that set minimum requirements for people with disabilities, universal design takes a broader approach, making spaces inherently usable by everyone. In residential construction, universal design supports aging in place, allowing homeowners to remain in their homes through changing life stages without costly renovations. The Americans with Disabilities Act sets legal requirements for public accommodations, and while single-family homes are exempt from ADA requirements, its accessibility guidelines provide a proven reference for universal design features.

Zero-step entry eliminates the barrier of a threshold step at all exterior doors, making the home accessible to wheelchair users, people using walkers, and parents pushing strollers. ADA guidelines specify a maximum threshold height of one-half inch with a beveled edge. The entry should be covered to prevent water and ice accumulation at the door. Interior doors should have a minimum clear opening width of 32 inches, which typically requires a 36-inch door. Pocket doors and barn doors eliminate the swing clearance requirement entirely, freeing floor space in smaller rooms. Lever door handles require no grasping or twisting motion, unlike round doorknobs, and can be operated with an elbow when hands are full. They comply with ADA requirements for operable parts.

Bathrooms present the greatest accessibility challenges and benefit most from universal design. A curbless shower with a linear drain or a sloped floor eliminates the trip hazard of a curb while containing water. The shower should be a minimum of 36 by 60 inches with a built-in bench or space for a portable shower chair. Blocking for grab bars should be installed in walls around toilets and showers during framing even if bars are not immediately installed, making future addition simple. Grab bars must support 250 pounds and should be mounted 33 to 36 inches above the floor. Reinforced walls labeled on as-built drawings save thousands in renovation costs later. At least one bedroom and a full bathroom on the main floor allow single-level living. Kitchen design should include varied counter heights, pull-out shelves in lower cabinets, and task lighting. Rocker-style light switches at 42 to 48 inches above the floor and electrical outlets at 18 to 24 inches are accessible from both standing and seated positions.
