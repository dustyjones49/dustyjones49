CMU Block Types

Concrete Masonry Unit Overview

Concrete Masonry Units, commonly called CMU or concrete blocks, are a fundamental building material for foundations, retaining walls, and structural walls in both residential and commercial construction. Modern CMU manufacturing produces a wide variety of shapes, sizes, and surface textures to meet structural, aesthetic, and budget requirements. The standard nominal size is 8x8x16 inches, though actual dimensions are 3/8 inch less to accommodate mortar joints.

Standard gray CMU is the most economical and widely used block type. It features a plain, slightly rough surface and is intended for applications where the block will be covered with stucco, siding, or interior finishes. The surface texture provides good mechanical bond for plaster and mortar coatings. Split face block is manufactured by molding two blocks face-to-face and mechanically splitting them apart after curing, revealing a rough, fractured surface that resembles natural stone. It is popular for exposed foundation walls and exterior accent walls where a textured architectural finish is desired.

Ground face block undergoes a grinding process after curing that removes the outer surface, exposing the aggregate within and producing a smooth, polished appearance. The aggregate color and size can be controlled for consistent decorative effects. Scored block has vertical grooves molded into the face, typically one, two, or three grooves per block, mimicking the appearance of multiple smaller units. Bullnose block features one or more rounded edges and is used at wall ends, corners, and openings where a finished edge is required. Specialty blocks include lintel blocks with U-shaped channels for horizontal reinforcement and bond beam blocks for continuous rebar runs.
