Pressure Treated Lumber Types

AWPA Use Categories UC1 Through UC5

Pressure-treated lumber is classified by the American Wood Protection Association (AWPA) into Use Categories (UC) that define the required chemical retention level (in pounds per cubic foot, PCF) for specific applications and decay hazard exposure. These categories simplify prescriptive specification so builders can order "UC4B" lumber and receive wood treated for ground contact heavy-duty use without needing to specify the exact chemical and retention level. UC1 is for interior dry construction where the wood is protected from weather and moisture — this includes sill plates on foundations, interior wall framing, and furring strips. UC1 requires the lowest retention level (typically 0.15 PCF for MCA or CA-C preservative). UC2 is for interior damp applications such as sill plates in contact with masonry or concrete but not exposed to weather; it prevents decay from occasional condensation or limited moisture. UC3A is above-ground exterior use with rapid water runoff and good ventilation, such as deck boards, railing balusters, fence pickets, and exterior trim that does not touch the ground. UC3B is above ground but with poor ventilation or potential for water trapping, such as deck joists and beams, fence rails, and sloped surfaces that collect water. UC4A is for ground contact in general use — the classic "ground contact rated" lumber for fence posts, landscape timbers, and low decks where lumber touches the ground or is within 6 inches of soil. UC4B is for heavy-duty ground contact or fresh water contact, with higher retention for critical structural components like retaining walls, posts in wet soils, and permanent wood foundations (PWF). UC4C is extreme duty ground contact or tropical environments where decay hazard is exceptionally high, such as termite-prone zones. UC5 is for marine applications: saltwater or brackish water exposure, including bulkheads, piers, and docks. UC5A is for northern waters (colder, less marine borer activity), and UC5B and UC5C are for warmer waters where marine borers (teredo worms, gribbles) are active and require dual treatment with preservative plus creosote or other heavy-duty protection.

Modern Preservatives and Fastener Compatibility

Since 2004, residential pressure-treated lumber has been treated with copper-based preservatives instead of CCA (chromated copper arsenate), which was phased out for residential use due to arsenic concerns. The two primary modern preservatives are Micronized Copper Azole (MCA) and Alkaline Copper Quaternary (ACQ). Both are copper-based and both are more corrosive to steel fasteners than the old CCA. Hot-dipped galvanized (meeting ASTM A153) or stainless steel (Type 304 or 316 for marine) fasteners and connectors are required for all pressure-treated lumber. Electro-galvanized fasteners do not provide adequate corrosion protection. Simpson Strong-Tie ZMAX or double-barrier coated connectors are specifically approved for ACQ and MCA lumber. General-use PT lumber (above ground, UC3) should not be confused with ground-contact PT lumber (UC4). After the 2016 AWPA standard update, lumber used for deck joists and beams within 6 inches of the ground requires ground-contact treatment (UC4A), even if physically above the soil.

  • Fasteners: hot-dipped galvanized (min G185) or stainless steel (304/316)
  • Connectors: ZMAX or double-barrier coated (standard galvanized not sufficient)
  • Aluminum: never in direct contact with treated lumber (galvanic corrosion)
  • UC3 vs UC4: deck joists within 6 in of ground require UC4A
  • End cuts/notches: must be field-treated with copper naphthenate (end-cut preservative)
  • Borate treatment (UC1/UC2 only): interior, non-leaching, for termites and powder post beetles
