Grout Types Comparison

Sanded, Unsanded, Epoxy, and Urethane Grout

Selecting the correct grout type is essential for a durable, crack-free tile installation. Cementitious grout is the most common and comes in two forms: sanded and unsanded. Sanded grout contains fine silica sand particles that minimize shrinkage and provide superior compressive strength for joints 1/8 inch and wider. The sand fills the void, preventing the grout from cracking as it cures. Unsanded grout is a smooth Portland cement and pigment blend intended for narrow joints of 1/16 to 1/8 inch, such as those between polished marble or glass tile, where sand would scratch the tile surface. Epoxy grout is a two-part system consisting of epoxy resin and hardener mixed with a filler powder; it is nearly waterproof, stain-proof, and chemical-resistant, making it ideal for commercial kitchens, swimming pools, and showers. Unlike cementitious grout, epoxy grout never requires sealing. The trade-off is a much shorter working time (about 45-60 minutes) and higher cost. Urethane grout is a single-component, pre-mixed alternative that bridges the gap between cementitious and epoxy products. It offers good stain resistance, color consistency, and flexibility without the mixing hassles of epoxy. It works for joints from 1/16 to 1/2 inch and is rated for wet areas. Furan grout is an industrial-grade chemical-resistant grout used primarily in laboratories and food processing plants.

Grout Joint Width Guidelines

The ANSI A108 standards recommend that the grout joint width be at least three times the variation in tile facial dimensions. For example, if a ceramic tile varies by 1/32 inch from piece to piece, the minimum joint should be 3/32 inch. Rectified tiles (precision-cut for sharp edges) can be installed with 1/16-inch joints and unsanded grout. Large-format tiles (greater than 15 inches on any side) require wider joints — typically 1/8 to 3/16 inch — and flexible grout or mortar to accommodate thermal expansion. Movement joints (soft joints filled with color-matched sealant) must be placed at all changes in substrate plane, around the perimeter of the tile field, and every 20 to 25 feet in interior installations. Grout should be mixed with latex additive instead of water in wet areas for improved performance.

  • Joint width = 3x tile facial variation (ANSI A108.02)
  • Rectified tile: 1/16 in min, unsanded
  • Large-format tile: 1/8 - 3/16 in min
  • Movement joints: every 20-25 ft interior, 8-12 ft exterior
  • Perimeter joints: 1/4 in at all walls and transitions
  • Cure time before traffic: 24 hours (cementitious), 24-72 hours (epoxy)
