Concrete Mix Ratios

Concrete Proportions and Compressive Strength

Concrete mix design is specified by the ratio of cement to fine aggregate (sand) to coarse aggregate (gravel or crushed stone) by volume, along with the water-to-cement ratio (w/c). The w/c ratio is the single most important factor determining concrete strength and durability — a lower w/c ratio produces stronger, denser concrete but is harder to work and place. Standard residential concrete for slabs, footings, and walls is typically 3,000 to 3,500 PSI, achieved with approximately a 1:2.5:3.5 mix ratio and a w/c ratio of 0.45 to 0.50. High-strength concrete for structural columns, bridge decks, and pre-stressed members can exceed 5,000 PSI and requires a w/c ratio below 0.40 along with supplementary cementitious materials like fly ash or silica fume. Air entrainment (4-8%) is required for exterior concrete exposed to freeze-thaw cycles. An 80-pound bag of ready-mix concrete yields about 0.6 cubic feet of cured concrete. A typical 4-inch-thick 10x10-foot slab requires about 1.24 cubic yards of concrete, or approximately 56 bags of 80-pound mix.

Water-Cement Ratio and Curing

The water-cement ratio law established by Abrams in 1918 states that concrete strength is inversely proportional to the w/c ratio. Every extra gallon of water per cubic yard increases slump but reduces strength by roughly 500 PSI. Proper curing is equally critical: concrete should be kept continuously moist for at least 7 days to achieve design strength. Curing compounds, wet burlap, or plastic sheeting can all be used. Concrete reaches approximately 50% of its 28-day strength in 7 days and about 70% in 14 days. Hot weather accelerates setting and increases water demand; cold weather slows strength gain and requires heated enclosures or insulating blankets when temperatures drop below 40 degrees F.

  • Minimum cure: 7 days wet (ACI 308), 3 days with curing compound
  • Strength gain: 40% at 3 days, 65% at 7 days, 90% at 14 days, 100% at 28 days
  • Air entrainment: 4-8% for exterior freeze-thaw exposure
  • Slump: 2-4 in for slabs, 4-6 in for walls, 6-8 in for pumped concrete
  • 80 lb bag yields ~0.6 cu ft
  • 1 cubic yard = 27 cu ft = approx 45 bags of 80 lb mix
