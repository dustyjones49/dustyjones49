Circuit Breaker Types

Breaker Types and NEC Requirements

Circuit breakers have evolved far beyond simple thermal-magnetic overcurrent protection. Standard single-pole breakers (15A and 20A) protect against short circuits and overloads by combining a bimetallic strip (thermal element for overloads) with an electromagnet (magnetic element for instantaneous short-circuit trips). Ground Fault Circuit Interrupter (GFCI) breakers add a differential current transformer that monitors the balance between the hot and neutral conductors and trips within 25 milliseconds if a ground fault of 4-6 milliamps is detected. GFCI protection is required by the NEC for bathrooms, garages, outdoor outlets, crawl spaces, unfinished basements, kitchens (countertop receptacles), laundry areas, and within 6 feet of any sink. Arc Fault Circuit Interrupter (AFCI) breakers use advanced electronics to detect the unique waveform signature of dangerous electrical arcing and are required for virtually all 120-volt 15A and 20A branch circuits supplying outlets in dwelling unit living areas: living rooms, bedrooms, dining rooms, family rooms, hallways, closets, and similar spaces. Combination AFCI (CAFCI) breakers protect against both series arcs (loose connection at a single point) and parallel arcs (line-to-neutral or line-to-ground arcing). Dual-function breakers (DFCI) combine GFCI and AFCI protection in a single device. The 2020 NEC expanded AFCI requirements and added GFCI requirements for 250-volt receptacles in certain locations.

Breaker Selection and Installation

Breakers must match the panel brand and type — mixing brands (e.g., Square D breaker in a Siemens panel) is a NEC violation (NEC 110.3(B)) and a fire hazard because bus stab dimensions and contact pressures differ between manufacturers. Classified breakers (like Eaton CL) are listed for use in specific competing panels. Breaker amperage ratings for continuous loads (running 3+ hours) must be sized at 125% of the load: a 16-amp continuous load requires a 20A breaker. The 80% rule is the inverse: a 20A breaker should not have more than 16 amps of continuous load. Arc-fault breakers must be tested monthly using the built-in test button. GFCI breakers require a neutral pigtail connected to the neutral bus bar, and the circuit's neutral must land on the GFCI breaker, not the neutral bar.

  • Match breaker brand to panel brand (NEC 110.3(B))
  • Continuous load: breaker = 125% of load (80% rule)
  • 15A circuit: max 12A continuous, 14 AWG wire minimum
  • 20A circuit: max 16A continuous, 12 AWG wire minimum
  • GFCI test interval: monthly
  • AFCI must have dedicated neutral terminal on breaker
  • Tandem breakers: only in panels listed for them
