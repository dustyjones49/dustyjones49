Mold Prevention Tips

Mold Prevention Fundamentals

Mold requires three conditions to grow: moisture, a food source, and temperatures between 40 and 100 degrees Fahrenheit. In homes, the food source is nearly always present in the form of drywall paper, wood framing, carpet fibers, and dust. Temperature is rarely a controlling factor in occupied homes. Moisture control is therefore the only practical strategy for mold prevention. Eliminating water sources, managing humidity, and responding quickly to leaks and spills prevents mold from establishing a foothold in the building.

Moisture control begins outside the home. Proper grading around the foundation should slope away from the building at a minimum of 6 inches of fall over the first 10 feet. Gutters and downspouts must be kept clear of debris and should discharge water at least 5 feet from the foundation via downspout extensions or underground drain pipes. Basement and crawl space moisture is controlled through exterior waterproofing, interior drain tile with a sump pump, and vapor barriers over exposed earth in crawl spaces. Crawl spaces should be ventilated or conditioned with a dehumidifier depending on climate zone. Interior moisture sources include bathrooms, kitchens, laundry rooms, and basements. Exhaust fans vented to the exterior, not into the attic, should run during and for 20 minutes after showering or cooking.

Humidity control indoors should target 30 to 50 percent relative humidity. Levels below 30 percent can cause respiratory discomfort and static electricity; levels above 60 percent support mold growth. Dehumidifiers are essential in basements, particularly during summer months when warm, humid outdoor air condenses on cool foundation walls and floors. Air conditioning reduces humidity as a byproduct of cooling, but oversized AC units cool quickly without running long enough to dehumidify, so proper sizing is important. Plumbing leaks, roof leaks, and window condensation must be addressed within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mold colonization. Porous materials saturated with clean water can typically be dried and saved if addressed within 48 hours; materials saturated with sewage or floodwater should be removed and replaced.
