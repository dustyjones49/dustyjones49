Sustainable Building Materials

Green Building Material Selection

Sustainable building materials reduce the environmental impact of construction through renewable sourcing, recycled content, low embodied energy, or superior durability that extends replacement cycles. Green building certifications including LEED, Green Globes, and the Living Building Challenge award points for material selection. The most effective sustainable material strategies combine multiple benefits: rapidly renewable resources, local sourcing to reduce transportation emissions, and products with low or zero volatile organic compound emissions for healthy indoor air quality.

Bamboo is the fastest-growing plant on Earth, with some species growing up to 3 feet per day and reaching harvestable maturity in 3 to 5 years, compared to 20 to 120 years for hardwood trees. Bamboo flooring and panel products are harder than red oak and maple, with Janka hardness ratings often exceeding 1,400. Strand-woven bamboo, manufactured by compressing bamboo fibers with low-VOC resins, achieves hardness ratings above 3,000. Bamboo sequesters carbon rapidly during growth and its extensive root system prevents soil erosion. Cork is harvested from the bark of the cork oak tree without killing the tree, with the bark regenerating every 9 to 12 years. Cork flooring is naturally antimicrobial, sound-absorbing, and provides thermal insulation. Its cellular structure, consisting of millions of air-filled pockets, creates a resilient surface that recovers from compression.

Recycled steel uses approximately 75 percent less energy to produce than virgin steel from iron ore. Structural steel in North America typically contains 90 percent or more recycled content. Steel framing is dimensionally stable, fire-resistant, and immune to termites and rot. Reclaimed wood salvaged from barns, factories, and old-growth logs recovered from river bottoms provides unique character with fully matured grain patterns no longer available from new lumber. Reclaimed wood carries a significant cost premium but offers unmatched aesthetic value and the highest sustainability credentials. Hempcrete is a bio-composite of hemp hurds, lime binder, and water that forms a lightweight, insulating wall material. It is not structural and must be used as infill within a timber or steel frame. Hempcrete sequesters carbon as it cures, provides excellent thermal and acoustic insulation, and regulates indoor humidity. It is fire-resistant, pest-resistant, and fully biodegradable at end of life.
