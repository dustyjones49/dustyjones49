Standard Door Sizes

Interior and Exterior Door Dimensions

Standard door sizes are essential for framing, ordering pre-hung units, and ensuring code compliance. Interior doors are typically 80 inches (6'-8") tall, while exterior doors may be 80 or 96 inches (8'-0"). Widths range from narrow 24-inch closet doors to wide 36-inch entry doors. Rough openings add 2 to 2.5 inches to both width and height to accommodate the door jamb, shims, and leveling tolerance. American residential construction follows these conventions closely. The International Residential Code (IRC) requires at least one egress door with a 32-inch clear opening. Selecting the correct rough opening before framing saves costly rework and ensures doors swing and latch properly. Pocket doors, barn doors, and French double doors each have their own sizing rules that deviate slightly from standard single-door dimensions. Always refer to the manufacturer's spec sheet for pre-hung units, as jamb thickness and threshold height can alter the rough opening requirements. Proper door sizing also affects trim selection and wall finish planning.

Egress and ADA Requirements

Building codes mandate at least one egress door per dwelling with a minimum 32-inch clear opening width and 78-inch clear height when the door is open at 90 degrees. For ADA-compliant doors, the clear opening must be at least 32 inches at 90 degrees, threshold height limited to 0.5 inch, and operable hardware must not require tight grasping or twisting. Lever handles are preferred over knobs. Closer pressure must not exceed 5 pounds of interior force and 8.5 pounds of exterior force.

  • Minimum clear width: 32 inches (egress and ADA)
  • Minimum clear height: 78 inches
  • Threshold height max: 0.5 inch (ADA), 1.5 inch (sliding doors)
  • Hardware: operatable with one hand, no tight grasping
  • Maneuvering clearance: 18 inches min on pull side
  • Door swing must not reduce required hallway width
