Water Conservation Tips

Reducing Household Water Use

The average American household uses approximately 300 gallons of water per day, with about 70 percent occurring indoors. Water conservation reduces utility bills, decreases strain on municipal water treatment systems, and preserves freshwater resources for ecosystems and future generations. Simple fixture upgrades and behavioral changes can reduce water consumption by 30 to 50 percent without sacrificing comfort or convenience.

Low-flow fixtures provide the greatest indoor water savings. Toilets account for nearly 30 percent of indoor residential water use. Pre-1994 toilets use 3.5 to 7 gallons per flush, while modern WaterSense-labeled models use 1.28 gallons or less. Replacing an old toilet saves approximately 13,000 gallons per year for a family of four. Showerheads manufactured before 1992 use 5.5 gallons per minute, while WaterSense models use 2.0 GPM or less, cutting shower water use by more than half. Faucet aerators rated at 1.5 GPM or less replace standard 2.2 GPM aerators and reduce sink water use by 30 percent. ENERGY STAR certified washing machines use 33 percent less water than standard models and significantly less energy for water heating.

Outdoor water conservation focuses on irrigation efficiency and landscape design. A typical irrigation system applying one inch of water to a quarter-acre lawn uses over 6,000 gallons per watering cycle. Smart irrigation controllers with rain sensors and soil moisture monitoring prevent watering during rain events and adjust schedules based on evapotranspiration rates. Rain barrels capture roof runoff for garden irrigation, with a 1,000-square-foot roof collecting approximately 600 gallons from one inch of rainfall. Greywater systems redirect water from bathroom sinks, showers, and laundry machines to subsurface landscape irrigation, though codes restrict use and require permitting in most jurisdictions. Drought-tolerant landscaping, or xeriscaping, replaces water-intensive turf grass with native plants adapted to local rainfall patterns. Grouping plants by water needs, applying 2 to 4 inches of mulch, and using drip irrigation instead of spray heads further reduces outdoor water consumption by 50 to 75 percent.
