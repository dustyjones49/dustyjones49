OSB vs Plywood Comparison

Oriented Strand Board vs Plywood Head-to-Head

Oriented Strand Board (OSB) and plywood are the two dominant wood structural panels used in residential construction for wall sheathing, roof sheathing, and subfloor. Both are rated under APA PS2 performance standards, meaning they carry equivalent span ratings and structural capacity when marked with the same rating. However, their material composition and in-field performance characteristics differ in meaningful ways. OSB is manufactured from fast-growing softwood species (aspen, poplar, southern yellow pine) that are machine-strand-cut into wafers up to 6 inches long, then oriented in alternating cross-directional layers, bonded with waterproof phenol-formaldehyde or PMDI (polymeric methylene diphenyl diisocyanate) resin under heat and pressure. Strands in the face layers are oriented parallel to the panel's long axis for stiffness, while core strands are oriented perpendicular. This cross-lamination provides uniform engineering properties with no knots or voids. Plywood, in contrast, is made from rotary-peeled veneer sheets from log sections, cross-laminated and glued. Plywood has a more uniform stiffness in both directions because each veneer layer is continuous, whereas OSB's stiffness is slightly more biased toward the face orientation direction. Cost is OSB's primary advantage: it is typically 15-25% cheaper than plywood for the same span-rated panel, which translates to significant savings across an entire house. Moisture performance is where the materials diverge most significantly. Both use waterproof adhesives (Exposure 1 rated), but OSB edges are more susceptible to swelling and thickness expansion when exposed to prolonged moisture. Once swelled, OSB does not return to its original thickness, which can create a raised edge at panel seams (telegraphing) under vinyl or resilient flooring. Plywood edges are more moisture-tolerant and recover better from wetting. For subfloor, plywood is preferred under tile or hardwood because it holds fasteners better and does not telegraph edge swell. For wall and roof sheathing, OSB's performance is equivalent to plywood when kept reasonably dry during and after construction. AdvanTech and similar high-performance OSB subfloor products use enhanced resin systems and edge seal coatings that dramatically improve moisture performance over standard OSB.

Application-Specific Recommendations

For wall sheathing, OSB is the dominant product in new residential construction due to its cost advantage and excellent racking resistance. For roof sheathing, both OSB and plywood perform equally, though OSB requires H-clips at unsupported edges to prevent differential deflection between panels. For subfloor, premium plywood (AC or BC sanded face) or AdvanTech OSB is recommended. For tile underlayment, plywood is strongly preferred because it does not edge-swell and telegraph under thin-set mortar and tile. Engineered wood siding (LP SmartSide type) uses OSB-based technology with zinc borate treatment and overlay for durability. Both OSB and plywood must be protected from weather exposure for no more than the limited time specified by the manufacturer and the APA.

  • Wall sheathing: OSB (cost-effective, code-approved equivalent)
  • Roof sheathing: OSB with H-clips at panel edges between rafters/trusses
  • Subfloor: Plywood or premium OSB (AdvanTech) for smooth surface
  • Tile underlayment: Plywood preferred (minimal edge telegraphing)
  • Both must be protected from weather (covered within 30-60 days)
  • Both carry APA span rating (e.g., 32/16) indicating structural equivalency
