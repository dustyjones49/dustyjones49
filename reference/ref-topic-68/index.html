Countertop Edge Profiles

Edge Profile Selection

The edge profile of a countertop is both a functional safety feature and a significant design element. The profile determines how the countertop interacts with the user on a daily basis: whether leaning against the edge is comfortable, whether spills drip over or are contained, and how the profile catches light and defines the visual weight of the surface. Fabricators can produce standard edge profiles on natural stone using CNC routers and progressively finer diamond polishing pads. More complex profiles require additional machine time, skilled handwork, and material removal, directly increasing cost.

The eased edge is the most common and economical profile. It is a square edge with the sharp corners slightly rounded, typically with about a 1/8-inch radius. Eased edges suit modern and contemporary designs with their clean, minimal appearance. They cost little beyond the basic slab fabrication price and work on any stone thickness. The bevel edge adds a flat angled cut, typically 45 degrees, along the top or bottom edge. This creates a crisp, geometric detail that enhances thicker slabs and adds a transitional design element without ornate curves. Single bevel edges are common on 3cm granite and quartz.

The bullnose edge is a full half-round profile that completely rounds the edge of the countertop. It is the safest profile for households with small children, as it has no sharp corners at arm height. A demi-bullnose (half bullnose) rounds only the top corner while leaving the bottom edge square. Ogee edges feature an S-shaped curve with both convex and concave elements, creating the most ornate and traditional appearance. An ogee requires a thicker slab, typically 3cm, and adds substantial fabrication cost due to multi-pass routing. The waterfall edge describes not the profile shape but the countertop extending vertically down the sides of an island or peninsula to the floor, creating a dramatic full-height stone panel effect that defines luxury kitchen design.
