Tile PEI Ratings

Porcelain Enamel Institute (PEI) Wear Rating Scale

The PEI rating is a standardized test developed by the Porcelain Enamel Institute that measures a glazed tile's resistance to surface abrasion. The test uses a rotating abrasive wheel under load to simulate foot traffic wear, and the rating indicates how many revolutions the tile's surface can withstand before visible wear appears. This is distinct from the tile's breaking strength or water absorption rating (which determines whether a tile is ceramic or porcelain). Selecting the correct PEI rating for the intended application prevents premature wear that results in dull, scratched tiles that cannot be restored. PEI 0 tiles are unrated glazed tiles and are suitable for wall applications only — they have zero foot traffic resistance and should never be installed on a floor. PEI 1 tiles have very light traffic resistance and are also restricted to walls, such as bathroom wainscoting and kitchen backsplashes, or light-use residential bathrooms where occupants are barefoot. PEI 2 tiles are rated for light residential foot traffic in areas with soft-soled footwear or bare feet; this includes residential bathroom floors and bedrooms with low traffic. PEI 3 tiles are the all-around workhorse for residential flooring, rated for all residential foot traffic including kitchens, hallways, and entryways where outdoor shoes and normal levels of grit are tracked in. Most residential floor tile sold is PEI 3 or 4. PEI 4 tiles are rated for medium commercial traffic such as restaurant dining areas, small offices, and hotel lobbies where foot traffic is moderate but does not involve heavy abrasives. PEI 5 tiles are the highest wear rating, suitable for heavy commercial and industrial traffic including retail stores, airports, hospital corridors, public buildings, and areas exposed to tracked-in dirt, sand, and grit. PEI 5 tiles maintain their appearance under the most demanding foot traffic conditions. Note that PEI applies only to glazed tiles — unglazed tiles (quarry tile, porcelain pavers, natural stone) do not carry a PEI rating but use other durability metrics like the Mohs hardness scale for stone.

Other Critical Tile Ratings: COF and Water Absorption

Beyond PEI, two other ratings are critical for floor tile selection. The Coefficient of Friction (COF) measures slip resistance. The Dynamic Coefficient of Friction (DCOF) measurement per ANSI A137.1 requires floor tiles intended for level interior spaces to have a DCOF of 0.42 or greater when tested with the BOT-3000E tribometer. Tiles with a DCOF below 0.42 are considered too slippery for floor use without additional treatments. Water absorption (WA) classifies tiles: porcelain tiles have a WA of 0.5% or less (impervious), making them frost-proof and suitable for exterior and freeze-thaw applications. Ceramic tiles have a WA above 0.5% (vitreous or semi-vitreous) and are suitable for interior floors and walls. Glass tiles and natural stone have their own rating systems.

  • PEI measures surface wear resistance (glazed tiles only)
  • DCOF: 0.42 minimum for level interior floors (ANSI A137.1)
  • Porcelain: water absorption < 0.5% (frost-proof)
  • Ceramic: water absorption > 0.5% (interior only)
  • Unglazed tile: no PEI rating (rated by water absorption and breaking strength)
  • Shade/V shade variation (V1-V4): indicates color consistency from tile to tile
