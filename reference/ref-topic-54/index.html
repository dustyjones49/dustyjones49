Concrete Finish Types

Choosing the Right Concrete Finish

Concrete finishes dramatically influence both the appearance and performance of a slab. The finishing process occurs during the narrow window between initial set and final set, requiring skilled timing and technique. Each finish type balances cost, skid resistance, maintenance requirements, and aesthetic appeal. Understanding the trade-offs helps homeowners and contractors select the optimal finish for each application.

Broom finish is the most common exterior concrete finish, created by dragging a stiff-bristled broom across partially set concrete. The resulting texture provides excellent slip resistance, making it ideal for driveways, sidewalks, and pool decks. It is also the most economical finish, adding little to no cost beyond basic finishing. Trowel finish uses steel trowels to produce a smooth, dense surface with minimal porosity. A standard trowel finish is typical for interior garage and basement slabs, while a burnished or hard-trowel finish creates a nearly polished surface suitable for warehouses where forklifts operate.

Stamped concrete replicates the appearance of stone, brick, tile, or wood by pressing patterned mats into the surface. Color hardeners and release agents add depth and realism. Stamped finishes are popular for patios, walkways, and pool surrounds, though they add significant cost per square foot. Exposed aggregate finish removes the top layer of cement paste to reveal the decorative stones within the concrete mix, creating a naturally textured, durable surface. Salt finish uses rock salt pressed into the surface and later washed away, leaving small pits that provide slip resistance with a more subtle texture than broom or exposed aggregate finishes.
