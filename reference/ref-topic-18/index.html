Wood Screw Dimensions and Specifications

Wood screws are sized by a gauge number (#) that indicates the major (outside) thread diameter and a length measured in inches. The gauge number system runs from #0 (smallest) through #24 (largest), with #4 through #14 covering virtually all residential and light commercial applications. A #8 screw has a major diameter of 0.164 inch and is the most common size for general woodworking, cabinet assembly, and light-duty fastening. #10 screws (0.190 inch diameter) are the standard for deck screws, structural connectors, and medium-duty fastening. Structural wood screws like GRK-RSS, SPAX, and Simpson SDWS are a modern alternative to lag bolts with no pre-drilling required in many cases. These screws have patented thread designs, hardened steel construction, and high shear and withdrawal values published in ICC-ES evaluation reports. Threads per inch (TPI) decreases as the screw diameter increases: #4 screws have about 36 TPI, #8 screws around 32 TPI, and #14 coarse-thread screws have about 10 TPI for maximum grip in softwood. The pilot hole size for the shank (non-threaded portion) in hardwood is typically the screw's major diameter; in softwood it is 70-90% of the major diameter.

Screw Type and Head Selection

Beyond gauge and length, the drive type and head style significantly impact ease of use. Phillips drive (#2 for #6 to #10 screws) is the most common but prone to cam-out at high torque. Torx (star) and Robertson (square) drives resist cam-out and are preferred for decking and structural applications. Flat-head screws are countersunk into the wood surface for a flush finish. Bugle-head drywall screws are designed to dimple without tearing the paper face. Pan-head screws sit above the surface and are used with metal brackets. Trim-head screws have a small diameter head (similar to a #6 or #8 screw head on a #10 body) for a nearly invisible appearance on composite decking. Lag screws (lag bolts) use a hex head and are sized by diameter in 1/4 inch increments (1/4, 5/16, 3/8, 1/2, 5/8, 3/4, 1 inch) rather than the gauge system.

  • Phillips #2: standard for #6-#10 screws
  • Torx T25: preferred for deck and structural screws
  • Robertson #2: popular in Canada (red handle)
  • Flat head: flush countersink in wood
  • Bugle head: drywall screws, self-countersinking
  • Pan head: mounts above surface, used with metal
