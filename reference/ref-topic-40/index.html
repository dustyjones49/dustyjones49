Hardwood Floor Janka Ratings

Janka Hardness Scale for Common Flooring Species

The Janka hardness test measures the force required to embed a 0.444-inch steel ball to half its diameter into a sample of wood. The result, expressed in pounds-force (lbf), provides a standardized measure of hardness and dent resistance. For residential flooring, hardness is one of the most important material properties because it directly correlates to how well the floor resists denting from foot traffic, furniture, pet claws, and dropped objects. Red oak is the industry benchmark at 1,290 lbf and serves as the reference point for comparing all other wood species. White oak is harder at 1,360 lbf and has naturally closed grain (tyloses in the pores) making it more water-resistant, preferred for kitchens and entryways. Sugar maple (hard maple) at 1,450 lbf is used for sports floors (NBA courts) because of its uniform density and light color that hides minor scratches. Hickory at 1,820 lbf is the hardest commonly available domestic hardwood and is an excellent choice for high-traffic areas, but its extreme hardness makes it difficult to sand and nail during installation. Brazilian walnut (ipe) at 3,684 lbf is nearly three times harder than red oak and so dense it barely floats, earning it a Class A fire rating without chemical treatment. Brazilian cherry (jatoba) at 2,350 lbf offers deep reddish-brown tones and immense durability. Bamboo flooring, while technically a grass and not wood, can achieve Janka ratings from 1,180 (natural vertical grain) to over 3,000 (strand-woven carbonized) depending on the manufacturing process. Strand-woven bamboo, compressed under extreme pressure with resin binders, is one of the hardest flooring materials available at a fraction of the cost of exotic hardwoods. For rooms with large dogs, heavy furniture, or high-heel traffic, consider woods rated 1,800 lbf or above. For formal living rooms with moderate traffic, oak (red or white) and walnut provide an excellent balance of appearance and practical durability.

Applying Janka Ratings to Flooring Decisions

Hardness is one factor among many in flooring selection. Stability (resistance to expansion and contraction with humidity changes) matters as much as hardness, particularly in climates with wide seasonal humidity swings. Engineered wood flooring with a cross-ply construction provides greater dimensional stability than solid hardwood. The finish applied to the floor provides the first line of defense against wear — an aluminum oxide-infused UV-cured factory finish (typical of pre-finished flooring) is significantly harder and more wear-resistant than site-applied polyurethane. The Janka test only measures raw wood hardness, not finished floor performance.

  • Red oak (1,290): hardwood flooring benchmark
  • Pet-friendly: select wood rated 1,500+ Janka
  • Heavy traffic (hallways, kitchens): 1,500+ Janka
  • Formal/low-traffic rooms: 950-1,290 Janka acceptable
  • Engineered vs solid: engineered more stable, same wear layer
  • Finish = first defense; aluminum oxide factory finish superior
