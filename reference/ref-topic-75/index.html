Exterior House Color Combinations

Exterior Color Selection

The exterior color scheme is the most visible design decision for any home, affecting curb appeal, resale value, and neighborhood character. A successful exterior color palette balances three elements: the body color covering the largest surface area, the trim color defining architectural details, and the accent color drawing attention to special features like the front door and shutters. Historical context, architectural style, and local climate all influence appropriate color choices.

For Colonial and Federal style homes, the classic combination is white body with black or dark green shutters and a bold front door in red, navy, or black. This high-contrast palette emphasizes symmetry and formal geometry. Craftsman homes traditionally use earthy, natural tones that integrate the house with its landscape. Popular Craftsman combinations include olive or sage green body with cream trim, deep brown body with tan trim, or warm gray body with white trim and a wood-stained front door. The trim color should highlight the distinctive brackets, rafter tails, and window casings that define Craftsman architecture.

Mid-century modern homes embrace the colors of their era: warm terracotta, mustard yellow, teal, and coral paired with crisp white trim. These homes often feature accent panels in a contrasting color to break up large wall planes. Victorian homes, by contrast, celebrate color complexity with three to five distinct colors highlighting different architectural elements. A typical Victorian palette might use sage body, burgundy trim, gold accents, and dark teal for the window sashes. Contemporary homes favor dark, dramatic body colors like charcoal gray, deep navy, or black with bright white trim for maximum contrast, or alternatively, warm neutrals with minimal trim differentiation for a monolithic modern look.
