Brick Bond Patterns

Understanding Brick Bond Patterns

Brick bond patterns refer to the arrangement of bricks in a wall to create both structural integrity and visual character. The bond pattern determines how stretchers (long face visible) and headers (short end visible) are oriented, and how courses are staggered. The choice of bond affects wall strength, water resistance, and the overall aesthetic of the building. Historically, solid masonry walls used bonds that integrated headers to tie multiple wythes together. Modern brick veneer construction relies on metal ties for this purpose, freeing designers to choose bonds based purely on appearance.

The running bond is the most common pattern in modern construction. Each brick is offset by half its length from the course below, creating a simple, classic look that is efficient to lay and minimizes cutting. It is the default pattern for residential brick veneer. Stack bond aligns bricks directly on top of one another, creating clean vertical and horizontal lines. It is strictly a veneer pattern requiring metal ties and is popular in mid-century modern and contemporary architecture. However, stack bond lacks the interlocking strength of staggered patterns and requires rigid reinforcement behind it.

Flemish bond alternates headers and stretchers within each course, with headers centered over stretchers below. This intricate pattern was the hallmark of Georgian and Colonial architecture and conveys a sense of craftsmanship and permanence. English bond alternates entire courses of headers with courses of stretchers, producing one of the strongest possible bonds. It is common in older load-bearing brick buildings and civil engineering structures. Common bond uses a running bond pattern with a header course inserted every fifth, sixth, or seventh course to tie multiple wythes together in solid wall construction.
