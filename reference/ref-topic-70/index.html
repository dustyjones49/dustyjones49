Floor Transition Types

Managing Flooring Transitions

Where two different flooring materials meet, a transition strip bridges the gap both physically and visually. These moldings accommodate height differences, allow for independent expansion and contraction of each flooring type, and create a finished edge where flooring terminates. Selecting the correct transition type depends on the height differential between the two flooring surfaces and the specific edge condition at the transition point.

T-molding gets its name from its T-shaped cross section. The vertical stem of the T fits into a gap left between two flooring surfaces of equal height, while the horizontal cap spans across both surfaces. T-molding is used primarily in doorways between rooms with the same flooring type or between two different flooring materials that are the same thickness. It allows each floor to expand and contract independently, which is critical for floating floors like laminate and luxury vinyl plank. Reducer strips transition from a higher flooring surface to a lower one, such as hardwood to vinyl or tile to carpet. The strip has a sloped profile that gradually ramps down from the thicker floor to the thinner one, eliminating a trip hazard at the threshold.

End cap molding is used where flooring terminates against a vertical surface such as a sliding glass door track, a fireplace hearth, or a step. It has a square edge on one side and a rounded profile that drops down over the flooring edge, protecting the exposed raw edge of the flooring material. Stair nose molding bridges the edge of a stair tread, providing a rounded or squared transition from the horizontal tread surface to the vertical riser below. Stair noses must be durable and slip-resistant, and they are often manufactured with a grooved surface for traction. Threshold strips are the simplest transition, typically a flat or slightly beveled piece of metal or wood that covers the joint between two flooring surfaces where height difference is minimal.
