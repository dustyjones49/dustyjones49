How to Hire a Contractor

Hiring the right contractor is the single most important decision you'll make on any construction or renovation project. A skilled, reliable contractor delivers quality work on time and on budget. A bad one can cost you months of delays, shoddy workmanship, and thousands in repairs.

  1. Define your project scope — Write down exactly what you want done, including materials, finishes, and timeline. The more specific you are, the more accurate your bids will be.
  2. Get at least 3 written bids — Never go with the first estimate. Compare scope, materials, timeline, and price across multiple contractors.
  3. Verify credentials — Check licenses, insurance (liability and workers' comp), and bonding. Call your state licensing board to confirm the license is active and free of complaints.
  4. Check references and past work — Ask for 3-5 recent references. Call them. Ask to see photos of completed projects similar to yours. Check online reviews on multiple platforms.
  5. Interview candidates — Meet each contractor in person at the project site. Gauge communication style, punctuality, and professionalism. A contractor who returns calls promptly before the job will likely do so during it.
  6. Get a detailed written contract — The contract should include scope of work, materials specifications, start and completion dates, payment schedule, change order process, and warranty terms. Never rely on verbal agreements.
  7. Never pay in full upfront — A reasonable deposit is 10-30%. Payments should be tied to milestones (e.g., foundation poured, framing complete, rough-in inspection passed). Hold at least 10% until final walk-through and punch list completion.

Questions to Ask Any Contractor

  • How long have you been in business under your current name?
  • Do you carry general liability and workers' compensation insurance? Can I see the certificates?
  • Will you pull all required permits? (The correct answer is always "yes.")
  • Who will be the day-to-day project manager or foreman on my job?
  • Do you use subcontractors? If so, how do you vet them and manage their work?
  • What is your typical daily start and end time? How many weeks is this project expected to take?
  • How do you handle change orders? What is the markup?
  • Can you provide a lien waiver from all subcontractors and suppliers at each payment milestone?
  • What warranties do you offer on labor? What are the manufacturer warranties on materials?
  • How do you prefer to communicate — phone, text, email? How often will I get updates?

Red Flags to Watch For

  • • No license or insurance — Walk away. No exceptions.
  • • Pressure to sign immediately — "This price is only good for 24 hours." Legitimate contractors don't use high-pressure tactics.
  • • Requires full payment upfront — A deposit is normal; full payment is not. Be especially wary if they ask for cash only.
  • • No written contract — If it's not in writing, it doesn't exist. Handshake deals lead to disputes.
  • • Bid is significantly lower than others — If one bid is 40%+ below the rest, they're either cutting corners, missing scope, or desperate.
  • • Refuses to provide references — Or gives only references from years ago. Ask for recent projects.
  • • Poor communication from the start — Late to the estimate, slow to return calls, vague answers. It won't improve later.
  • • Offers a discount for using leftover materials — This often means subpar or mismatched materials from another job.
  • • Suggests you skip permits — Pulling permits protects you. Unpermitted work can stall a future home sale and may not meet code.
  • • No physical business address — A P.O. box or only a cell phone number is a warning sign. Established contractors have a real office or shop.
