Construction Glossary

A

Aggregate

Crushed stone, gravel, or sand used as a base material in concrete and asphalt mixes, providing bulk and strength.

Air Change

The replacement of the full volume of air in a room or building, usually expressed as air changes per hour (ACH) for ventilation design.

Anchor Bolt

A steel bolt cast into concrete or grouting to fasten structural members like columns, plates, or sill plates to foundations.

Apron

The interior trim board placed beneath a window stool, covering the gap between the wall finish and window frame.

Attic

The unfinished space between the ceiling of the top floor and the underside of the roof, often used for ventilation and insulation.

Awning

A rooflike structure projecting over a window or door, typically canvas or metal, providing shade and weather protection.

Axial Load

A structural load applied along the longitudinal axis of a member, producing either compression or tension through the centerline.

ABS Pipe

Acrylonitrile Butadiene Styrene piping, a rigid black plastic pipe commonly used for drain, waste, and vent systems.

AC (Alternating Current)

Electric current that reverses direction periodically; standard household and commercial electrical supply in the United States.

Access Panel

A removable panel in a wall or ceiling providing entry to plumbing, electrical, or mechanical components for maintenance.

Acoustical Ceiling

A ceiling system using sound-absorbing tiles, typically suspended in a metal grid, to reduce noise and improve acoustic quality.

Adhesive

A bonding agent used to attach two surfaces together by surface attachment and internal strength; includes construction glues and epoxies.

Admixture

A chemical additive introduced to concrete, mortar, or grout to modify properties such as set time, workability, or durability.

Aerator

A threaded fitting on a faucet spout that mixes air with water to reduce splashing and conserve water while maintaining pressure.

AFCI

Arc Fault Circuit Interrupter; a circuit breaker that detects dangerous electrical arcs and shuts off power to prevent fires.

Aggregate Base

A layer of compacted crushed stone or gravel placed beneath concrete slabs, pavements, or foundations to provide a stable subgrade.

Air Barrier

A continuous material or system that restricts airflow through the building envelope, improving energy efficiency and moisture control.

Air Gap

A physical separation between a water supply outlet and a potential source of contamination, preventing backflow into the potable system.

Air Handler

The indoor part of an HVAC system that circulates conditioned air through ductwork using a blower fan and heat exchange coils.

Alcove

A recessed area or nook in a room, often used for built-in shelving, seating, or fixtures like bathtubs and cabinets.

Allowable Load

The maximum load a structural member can safely support as determined by building codes and engineering calculations.

Anchor

A mechanical device embedded in concrete or masonry or driven into framing to secure attachments and resist pull-out forces.

Angle Iron

A structural steel member with an L-shaped cross-section, used for bracing, framing, and supporting masonry or equipment.

Annealed Glass

Standard glass that has been slowly cooled during manufacturing to relieve internal stress; breaks into sharp shards if fractured.

Anode Rod

A sacrificial metal rod inserted into a water heater tank to attract corrosive elements and protect the tank from rusting.

Apron (Window)

The flat trim board attached to the wall below the window stool, running parallel to the floor and finishing the window opening.

Arc Fault

An unintended electrical discharge that can generate extreme heat and ignite nearby combustibles; detected by AFCI breakers.

Architectural Shingle

A premium asphalt roofing shingle with a thickened profile and layered construction, providing enhanced durability and a dimensional appearance.

Area Drain

A floor drain located in a depressed area or exterior paved surface to collect and remove surface water runoff.

As-Built

Drawings or documentation showing the final constructed condition of a project, including all changes made during construction.

Asbestos

A naturally occurring fibrous mineral once widely used for fireproofing and insulation, now heavily regulated due to health hazards from airborne fibers.

Ashlar

Stone masonry made of dressed, squared, and fitted stone blocks laid in regular courses with thin mortar joints.

Asphalt

A dark, viscous petroleum-based binder used in paving, roofing materials, and waterproofing compounds for its adhesive and waterproof qualities.

ASTM

American Society for Testing and Materials; an international standards organization establishing technical standards for materials and testing methods.

Atrium

A large open space, often with a skylight or glass roof, located at the center of a building and extending through multiple floors.

Attic Vent

An opening or device in an attic space that allows moisture and hot air to escape, reducing heat buildup and preventing condensation damage.

Auger

A spiral-shaped drill bit used for boring deep holes in wood or soil; also refers to a device for unclogging drain pipes.

Awl

A pointed hand tool used for marking surfaces, starting screw holes, or piercing small holes in wood, leather, and other materials.

Axe

A chopping tool with a sharp steel head mounted on a handle, used for shaping and cutting wood in rough carpentry and timber framing.

Axial

Pertaining to or aligned with the central axis of a structural member; describes forces applied along the member's centerline.

B

Backfill

Soil or fill material placed against a foundation wall or in a trench around buried structures after construction is complete.

Backflow

The reverse flow of contaminated water into a potable water supply, prevented by air gaps, vacuum breakers, and check valves.

Backsplash

A vertical surface, typically tile or stone, installed behind a countertop or sink to protect the wall from water and food splatter.

Baluster

An individual vertical post or spindle supporting a handrail on a staircase, balcony, or railing system spaced for safety.

Band Joist

The outer floor joist running perpendicular to the main joists around the perimeter of a floor frame, closing off the joist cavities.

Baseboard

A molding strip covering the joint between the interior wall finish and the floor, protecting the wall from scuffs and hiding gaps.

Batten

A narrow strip of wood or material used to cover joints between boards in board-and-batten siding or to secure materials.

Beam

A horizontal structural member designed to support loads across a span, transferring weight to columns, posts, or bearing walls.

Bearing Wall

A wall that supports vertical loads from floors or roofs in addition to its own weight; critical to structural integrity.

Bedding

A layer of sand, mortar, or gravel on which masonry units, pipes, or pavers are laid to provide a level and stable base.

Berm

A raised earthen mound or linear bank of soil used for landscaping, drainage control, or as a visual or noise barrier.

Bevel

An angled cut or edge on a material, not at a right angle, used for fitting, aesthetics, or to eliminate sharp corners.

Bid

A formal price proposal submitted by a contractor or subcontractor for performing specified construction work on a project.

Birdsmouth

A notch cut into the underside of a rafter where it sits atop the top plate of a wall, providing positive bearing and alignment.

Bitumen

A sticky, viscous hydrocarbon material derived from petroleum, used as a binder in asphalt paving and built-up roofing systems.

Blind Nail

A nail driven so its head is hidden, such as in a tongue of tongue-and-groove flooring or behind a shingle tab.

Blocking

Short pieces of framing lumber installed between joists or studs to provide nailing surface, stiffening, or fire-stopping.

Blower Door

A diagnostic tool using a calibrated fan in a doorway to measure the airtightness of a building by pressurization or depressurization.

Blueprint

A traditional term for construction drawings; historically blue-line paper prints, now typically large format digital prints of plans.

Board Foot

A unit of lumber volume equal to a board 12 inches by 12 inches by 1 inch thick; used for pricing and ordering hardwood lumber.

Bond

The pattern in which bricks, blocks, or stones are laid to create structural stability; also the adhesion between materials.

Bottom Plate

The lowest horizontal member of a framed wall, resting on the floor or foundation, to which vertical studs are attached.

Bracing

Temporary or permanent structural members installed diagonally or laterally to resist wind, seismic, or buckling forces.

Brick

A rectangular fired clay or concrete masonry unit used for walls, pavements, and facades in residential and commercial construction.

Bridging

Cross bracing or solid blocking installed between floor joists at mid-span to distribute loads and prevent joist twisting.

BTU

British Thermal Unit; the amount of heat needed to raise one pound of water by one degree Fahrenheit, used to rate heating and cooling capacity.

Building Code

A set of regulations adopted by a jurisdiction governing the design, construction, and occupancy of buildings to ensure safety.

Building Envelope

The physical barrier separating the interior conditioned space from the exterior environment, including walls, roof, foundation, windows, and doors.

Built-Up Roof

A multi-layer roofing system of alternating bitumen and felts, topped with aggregate, providing a durable waterproof membrane for flat roofs.

Bullnose

A rounded edge on trim, tile, stair treads, or drywall corners, eliminating sharp angles for a softer appearance and safety.

Burl

An irregular growth on a tree producing highly figured wood grain, prized for veneer and decorative woodworking applications.

Butt Joint

A simple joint where two pieces of material meet end-to-end or edge-to-edge without overlapping, fastened with nails, screws, or adhesive.

Butterfly Roof

An inverted roof form with two surfaces sloping downward toward a central valley, resembling the wings of a butterfly.

BX Cable

A type of armored electrical cable with a flexible metal sheathing enclosing insulated conductors, used before Romex became widespread.

Bypass Door

A sliding door system where panels slide past each other on parallel tracks, commonly used for closets and patio doors.

C

Cantilever

A structural element extending horizontally beyond its support, anchored at one end while carrying loads on the projecting portion.

Cap

The top protective layer of a wall, chimney, or post, often sloped to shed water and prevent moisture intrusion into the structure.

Capillary Action

The movement of moisture through small pores or cracks against gravity, drawing water upward through materials by surface tension.

Casing

Trim molding installed around door and window openings, covering the gap between the frame and the surrounding wall finish.

Cast Iron

A dense, durable iron alloy with high carbon content, poured into molds and used for drain pipes, radiators, and heavy-duty fittings.

Caulk

A flexible sealant applied along joints and gaps to seal out air, moisture, and insects, allowing for minor material movement.

Cavity Wall

A masonry wall constructed with two separated layers (wythes) tied together, leaving a continuous air space for drainage and insulation.

CCA

Chromated Copper Arsenate; a wood preservative historically used for pressure treating lumber, phased out for most residential uses due to toxicity.

Ceiling Joist

A horizontal framing member supporting a ceiling surface and transferring roof loads to the bearing walls; may also serve as rafter ties.

Cement

A fine gray powder, typically Portland cement, that chemically reacts with water to bind aggregates together and form concrete or mortar.

CFM

Cubic Feet per Minute; a unit measuring airflow volume, used to rate exhaust fans, ventilation systems, and HVAC equipment capacity.

Chair Rail

A horizontal molding installed on interior walls at approximately chair-back height, primarily decorative but also protecting walls from furniture.

Chase

A vertical or horizontal framed enclosure through a building, used to conceal ductwork, plumbing, wiring, or chimneys between floors.

Chimney

A vertical masonry or metal structure containing one or more flues for exhausting combustion gases from fireplaces, furnaces, or water heaters.

Circuit Breaker

An automatic electrical switch in a panel that interrupts current flow when overcurrent or short circuit conditions are detected.

Cladding

The exterior surface material of a building envelope, such as siding, brick veneer, or metal panels, providing weather protection and aesthetics.

Cleanout

A plugged access fitting in a drainage pipe providing an entry point for a snake or auger to clear blockages in the piping.

Clear Span

The unobstructed distance between two structural supports without intermediate columns or bearing walls, allowing open floor plans.

Cleat

A small strip of wood or metal attached to a surface as a support or fastener to secure shelving, cabinets, or roof panels.

CMU

Concrete Masonry Unit; a standardized hollow concrete block used for load-bearing and non-load-bearing walls, typically 8"x8"x16" nominal.

CO Detector

Carbon Monoxide Detector; a safety device that sounds an alarm when dangerous levels of odorless, colorless carbon monoxide gas accumulate.

Code

The legally adopted set of regulations dictating minimum construction standards for structural safety, fire protection, energy, and other aspects.

Coffered Ceiling

A ceiling with recessed panels formed by intersecting beams, creating a grid pattern; adds architectural depth and visual interest.

Cold Joint

The interface between consecutive concrete pours where the previous pour has already hardened, creating a potential weakness if not properly treated.

Collar Tie

A horizontal framing member connecting opposing rafters in the upper third of a roof, resisting outward thrust at the ridge.

Column

A vertical structural member primarily subjected to compressive loads, transferring weight from beams or slabs to the foundation below.

Combustion Air

Air supplied to a fuel-burning appliance to support complete combustion and safe venting of exhaust gases, required by code.

Compaction

The mechanical process of densifying soil or fill material using rollers, tampers, or vibratory compactors to improve bearing capacity.

Composite

A material made from two or more constituent materials with different properties, combined to produce enhanced structural characteristics.

Compression

A force that pushes or presses a material together, shortening it; the opposite of tension. A fundamental structural stress type.

Concrete

A composite material of cement, aggregate, and water that hardens through hydration into a stone-like mass for structural applications.

Condensate

Water formed when warm, moisture-laden air contacts a cold surface and humidity condenses into liquid; must be drained from HVAC equipment.

Conduction

The transfer of heat through a solid material by molecular vibration without bulk movement of the material; one of three heat transfer modes.

Conduit

A metal or plastic tube or channel that protects and routes electrical wiring through a building, from panel to outlets and fixtures.

Construction Joint

A planned interface between two adjacent concrete placements, designed to allow movement or to separate a pour into manageable sections.

Control Joint

A deliberately weakened linear groove in a concrete slab or wall that controls the location of cracking from drying shrinkage.

Convection

Heat transfer through the movement of fluids or gases, such as warm air rising or water circulating, used in heating and cooling systems.

Coping

A protective cap at the top of a wall, parapet, or chimney, typically sloped or projecting to direct water away from the masonry below.

Corbel

A stepped projection of masonry courses extending outward from a wall surface, supporting an overhanging load or providing decorative effect.

Corner Bead

A metal or vinyl strip applied to external drywall corners before finishing, providing a straight, durable, impact-resistant edge.

Cornice

A decorative horizontal projection at the top of a wall or along the roofline, consisting of moldings and trim that crown the building.

Counterflashing

A vertical metal flashing secured into a wall slot and folded down over the top of base flashing to create a waterproof roofing junction.

Course

A continuous horizontal layer of masonry units such as bricks, blocks, or stones laid in a single level of a wall.

Cove

A concave molding or curved joining surface at the junction between ceiling and wall, or a recessed area in a ceiling for indirect lighting.

Crawl Space

An accessible, shallow, unfinished space between the ground and the first floor of a building, used for access to utilities beneath.

D

Dado

A rectangular groove cut across the grain of a board, used in joinery to accept the edge of another board for cabinets and shelving.

Dampproofing

A coating applied to foundation walls to resist the passage of moisture in vapor form, less robust than full waterproofing.

Dead Load

The permanent static weight of a building including all structural elements, fixed finishes, and permanently attached equipment.

Decking

Structural sheathing material attached to roof or floor framing; also refers to the finished surface boards of an outdoor deck.

Deflection

The amount a structural member bends or displaces under an applied load; limited by building codes to prevent damage and discomfort.

Demising Wall

A fire-rated wall dividing adjacent units in a multi-unit building, extending from slab to underside of floor above, separating tenants.

Dew Point

The temperature at which air becomes saturated with moisture and water vapor begins to condense into liquid; critical for condensation control.

Dimension Lumber

Sawn lumber cut to standardized widths and depths, such as 2x4, 2x6, or 2x10, primarily used for structural framing.

Direct Vent

A sealed combustion system drawing outside air directly into a fuel-burning appliance and exhausting gases through a coaxial vent.

Door Jamb

The vertical side members of a door frame forming the opening and providing the mounting surface for hinges and strike plates.

Dormer

A vertical window projection built through the sloping plane of a roof, adding headroom, daylight, and architectural character to upper floors.

Double-Hung

A window with two operable sashes, one upper and one lower, sliding vertically within the frame for ventilation from top or bottom.

Downspout

A vertical pipe attached to a gutter system that carries collected roof water down to ground level or into a drainage system.

Drain Tile

Perforated pipe installed in a gravel bed below grade around a foundation to collect and redirect ground water, preventing basement flooding.

Drip Edge

A metal flashing installed along roof edges, designed to direct water runoff into the gutter and away from the fascia and building.

Dry-In

The stage of construction when the roof covering, weather barrier, windows, and doors are installed, making the interior weathertight.

Drywall

A rigid interior wall and ceiling finish material made of a gypsum plaster core sandwiched between paper facings, also called gypsum board or sheetrock.

Duct

A sheet metal, fiberglass, or flexible tube used to distribute conditioned air from HVAC equipment to rooms and return air for recirculation.

DWV

Drain, Waste, and Vent; the plumbing system carrying wastewater and sewage from fixtures to the sewer while venting sewer gases.

Dye Lot

A batch of material, such as carpet or tile, produced in the same dye run to ensure color consistency; different lots may vary slightly.

Deadbolt

A door lock with a solid rectangular bolt that slides into a strike plate, operated by key or thumb turn for enhanced security.

Diagonal Bracing

Structural members installed at an angle to the vertical plane to resist lateral forces such as wind and seismic loads on framed walls.

Diaphragm

A horizontal structural element, such as a floor or roof deck, that transfers lateral forces to vertical shear-resisting elements.

Dimension

The exact or nominal measurements of a building component, material, or space, specifying length, width, height, and thickness.

Distributor

A device or component that divides and channels air, water, or other materials to multiple outlets in a building system.

Diverter

A plumbing valve that redirects water flow from one outlet to another, such as from a tub spout to a shower head.

Dovetail

A woodworking joint featuring interlocking fan-shaped pins and tails, providing exceptional strength in drawer and box construction.

Dowel

A cylindrical wooden or metal pin inserted into corresponding holes in two pieces of material to align or join them together.

Downlight

A recessed light fixture mounted flush in a ceiling, directing illumination downward; also called a can light or pot light.

Draft Stop

A material or assembly installed in concealed spaces such as soffits and dropped ceilings to limit the passage of air and fire spread.

Drain

A pipe or channel that carries liquid waste or storm water by gravity from fixtures or surfaces to the sewer or disposal system.

Drawer

A sliding storage compartment built into cabinetry, composed of a front, sides, back, and bottom, sliding on guides or runners.

Dressed Lumber

Lumber that has been planed smooth on one or more sides to a uniform thickness and width after initial rough sawing.

Drip

A molded projection at the bottom of exterior trim that directs water away from the wall surface, preventing moisture accumulation.

Dry Rot

Timber decay caused by certain fungi that consume cellulose in wood, leaving it brittle, shriveled, and structurally compromised.

E

Eave

The lower edge of a roof projecting beyond the exterior wall, providing shade and directing rain water away from the building.

EER

Energy Efficiency Ratio; a measure of cooling output divided by electrical input for air conditioners at a specific outdoor temperature.

Efflorescence

A white powdery deposit of soluble salts on the surface of masonry or concrete, caused by water migrating through and evaporating.

Egress

A path of exit for occupants from a building to safety; building codes specify minimum egress window sizes for sleeping rooms.

Elastomeric

A coating material with rubber-like elastic properties that stretches and returns to its original shape, used for waterproofing and crack bridging.

Elbow

A pipe fitting allowing a change in direction, typically at 45 or 90 degrees, used in plumbing, conduit, and ductwork installations.

Electrical Panel

The main distribution point for a building's electrical circuits, containing circuit breakers or fuses that protect each branch circuit.

Elevation

An orthographic drawing showing the exterior face of a building; also refers to the height of a point above a reference datum.

End Grain

The exposed face of a board cut across the growth rings; absorbs moisture more readily than face or edge grain and requires sealing.

Energy Star

A government-backed program certifying appliances, windows, and building practices that meet superior energy efficiency standards.

Engineered Wood

Structural wood products manufactured by binding wood strands, veneers, or fibers with adhesives, including LVL, OSB, and plywood.

Epoxy

A two-part thermosetting resin adhesive known for extremely high bonding strength, chemical resistance, and gap-filling properties.

Escutcheon

A decorative protective plate or flange surrounding a plumbing pipe, valve, or keyhole on a finished surface, covering the wall opening.

Estimate

A calculated approximation of the cost of materials, labor, and equipment required to complete a construction project.

Evaporator

The indoor coil in an air conditioning system where liquid refrigerant absorbs heat from interior air as it evaporates into a gas.

Excavation

The process of removing earth for foundations, basements, trenches, or grading; the first major sitework activity in construction.

Expansion Joint

A gap or flexible material installed between building elements to accommodate thermal expansion and contraction without causing damage.

Expansion Tank

A small pressurized tank connected to a water heating system, absorbing the increase in water volume as it heats to prevent pressure buildup.

Exposure

The portion of a roofing shingle or tile that is visible to the weather after installation; the exposed tab or surface area.

Eave Vent

An intake vent located in the soffit or eave area that allows fresh outside air to enter the attic space as part of balanced ventilation.

Edge Banding

A thin strip of veneer, PVC, or wood applied to the exposed edges of plywood or composite panels for appearance and protection.

Edge Grain

Lumber sawn with growth rings running roughly perpendicular to the wide face, resulting is stable, vertical grain with minimal cupping.

Effluent

Liquid waste discharged from a septic tank into a leach field for further treatment and absorption into the surrounding soil.

EIFS

Exterior Insulation and Finish System; a synthetic stucco cladding system with foam insulation board and a textured finish coat applied over it.

Elbow Catch

A cabinet door latch that secures when the door is pushed closed and releases with a push or pull, often used on cabinet pairs.

Electric Strike

An electronically controlled door strike plate allowing a door to be locked or unlocked remotely, used in access control and intercom systems.

EMT

Electrical Metallic Tubing; thin-wall, unthreaded steel conduit used to protect and route electrical wiring in exposed and concealed locations.

Enamel

A hard, glossy paint finish that dries to a smooth, durable coating, often used on trim, cabinets, and high-touch surfaces.

End Cap

A fitting that seals the open end of a pipe or duct, preventing leaks and protecting the interior from debris and pests.

Exhaust Fan

A mechanical ventilation fan that removes stale air, moisture, and odors from a room and exhausts it to the outdoors through ductwork.

F

Face Brick

High-quality brick with a finished surface intended for exposed wall applications where appearance is important.

Fall

The slope or gradient of a drainage pipe, surface, or gutter designed to allow water to flow by gravity; typically expressed as inches per foot.

Fascia

The vertical board nailed to the ends of rafters or trusses at the eave line, supporting the gutter and providing a finished roofline edge.

Fastener

A mechanical device such as a nail, screw, bolt, or staple used to join two or more building components securely together.

Felt Paper

Asphalt-saturated heavy paper used as an underlayment beneath roofing and siding to provide secondary water protection.

Fender Washer

An oversized thin washer with a small center hole, used to distribute the clamping force of a fastener over a larger surface area.

Fenestration

The arrangement, design, and placement of windows, doors, skylights, and other openings in the exterior envelope of a building.

Fiber Cement

A composite siding material made of cement, sand, and cellulose fibers; durable, fire-resistant, and dimensionally stable exterior cladding.

Field Stone

Naturally rounded or irregular stones gathered from fields or the ground surface, used for rustic walls, veneers, and landscaping.

Filler

A material used to level imperfections, fill nail holes, gouges, or cracks in wood, drywall, or other surfaces before painting or finishing.

Finish Carpentry

The final phase of woodwork including installation of trim, moldings, doors, cabinets, and built-ins requiring precise fit and appearance.

Fire Block

A material or assembly installed in concealed spaces to block the vertical passage of fire and smoke within walls, floors, and chases.

Fire Rated

A classification indicating a building assembly has been tested to resist fire spread for a specified period of time, such as 1 hour or 2 hours.

Fish Tape

A flexible, flat steel or fiberglass tape used by electricians to pull wire through conduit, wall cavities, and other enclosed spaces.

Fixture

A permanently attached plumbing, electrical, or lighting device such as a sink, toilet, ceiling light, or sconce in a building.

Flagstone

Flat, thin, irregularly shaped natural stone pieces used for paving patios, walkways, and stepping paths with a rustic appearance.

Flange

A projecting rim or collar on a pipe, beam, or component used for attachment, strengthening, or connecting to another element.

Flashing

Thin metal or membrane material installed at roof intersections, valleys, chimneys, and window tops to direct water and prevent leaks.

Flat Grain

Lumber sawn with growth rings roughly parallel to the wide face; more prone to cupping but more economical than edge grain.

Flex Duct

Flexible insulated ductwork with a wire coil core wrapped in plastic and insulation, used to connect HVAC supply air to registers.

Float Glass

Sheet glass manufactured by floating molten glass on a bed of molten tin, producing a uniform thickness with smooth parallel surfaces.

Floor Joist

A horizontal framing member supporting floor loads between bearing walls, beams, or girders, typically spaced 16 or 24 inches on center.

Flue

A passage inside a chimney, vent, or pipe through which combustion gases, smoke, and heat travel upward and out of the building.

Flush

Aligning two surfaces so they are perfectly level and even with each other, without any projection or recess between the adjoining elements.

Flux

A chemical compound applied to metal before soldering that cleans the surface, prevents oxidation, and promotes uniform solder flow.

Footing

The widened base of a foundation wall or column that distributes building loads over a greater soil area to prevent settling.

Form

A temporary wooden or metal mold into which concrete is placed and held until it hardens, gaining the desired shape and dimensions.

Foundation

The lowest load-bearing part of a building, typically below grade, that transfers structural loads into the ground; includes footings and walls.

Framing

The skeletal structure of a building composed of studs, joists, rafters, and beams that forms the shape and supports all finishes.

French Drain

A gravel-filled trench containing a perforated pipe that redirects surface water and groundwater away from foundations or landscape areas.

Frost Line

The maximum depth below grade at which soil freezes in winter; footings must extend below this line to prevent frost heave damage.

Furring

Thin strips of wood or metal attached to a wall or ceiling surface to create a level nailing surface or provide an airspace behind finishes.

Fuse

A safety device containing a metal strip that melts when excessive current flows, breaking the circuit; an older form of overcurrent protection.

F-Clamp

A clamp shaped like the letter F with a sliding lower jaw, used to hold workpieces together during drilling, gluing, or assembly.

G

Gable

The triangular upper portion of an exterior wall between the edges of a sloping roof, enclosing the end of the roof ridge.

Gable Vent

A louvered vent installed in a gable end to allow passive air circulation through the attic space, reducing heat and moisture buildup.

Galvanized

Steel coated with a protective layer of zinc through hot-dipping or electroplating to resist rust and corrosion in exposed applications.

Gambrel Roof

A double-sloped roof with a steeper lower pitch and shallower upper pitch on each side, providing extra upper floor headroom in barn-style designs.

Gauge

A measurement of sheet metal thickness, wire diameter, or the distance between nails in a nail gun; a lower gauge number indicates thicker material.

GFCI

Ground Fault Circuit Interrupter; a fast-acting receptacle or breaker that shuts off power when current leakage to ground is detected, preventing shock.

Girder

A large horizontal structural beam, often steel or built-up wood, supporting floor joists, walls, or concentrated loads and transferring them to columns.

Glazing

Glass installed in windows, doors, or curtain wall systems; also the process of installing glass panes into frames with putty or gaskets.

Glulam

Glued Laminated Timber; an engineered wood beam made by bonding together layers of dimension lumber with durable moisture-resistant adhesives.

Grade

The elevation of the ground surface around a building; also the classification of lumber quality, or the slope of a drainage surface.

Grade Beam

A reinforced concrete beam at ground level spanning between piers or piles, supporting wall loads above and transferring them to deep foundations.

Grain

The orientation, size, and pattern of wood fibers determined by growth rings; affects strength, workability, and appearance of lumber.

Green Lumber

Freshly sawn lumber with a moisture content above 19%, not yet dried or seasoned; prone to shrinkage, twisting, and checking as it dries.

Ground

A conducting connection between an electrical circuit and the earth, providing a safe path for fault current to prevent shocks and fires.

Ground Fault

An unintentional electrical connection between an energized conductor and a grounded surface, creating a shock hazard detected by GFCI devices.

Grout

A fluid mixture of cement, sand, and water used to fill joints between tiles, bond reinforcing steel in masonry, or fill cavities in concrete.

Gusset

A flat plate, usually metal or plywood, fastened across a joint to strengthen and stiffen the connection between framing members at a truss node.

Gutter

A horizontal channel attached to the eave of a roof that collects rain runoff and directs it to downspouts, protecting the foundation from water.

Gypsum

A soft sulfate mineral used as the primary core material in drywall panels, valued for its fire-resistant and sound-dampening properties.

Gable End

The vertical wall at the end of a gable roof from the eave to the peak; requires proper bracing due to large wind load exposure.

Galvanic Corrosion

Electrochemical deterioration occurring when two dissimilar metals in contact with an electrolyte, such as water, create a low-energy corrosion cell.

Gang Nail

A metal connector plate with many small teeth punched from the plate, pressed into adjacent wood members to fasten truss joints quickly.

Gasket

A compressible sealing material placed between two mating surfaces to prevent the passage of fluid, air, or gas at the joint.

GFI

Ground Fault Interrupter; an alternate term for GFCI, referring to the same safety device that trips when detecting current leakage to ground.

Glue Block

A small block of wood adhesively attached at an interior corner joint to reinforce the connection, commonly used in cabinet and furniture construction.

H

Habitat

The natural environment where an organism lives; in construction, sensitivity to habitat affects site development and environmental permitting.

Hairline Crack

A very fine, narrow crack in concrete, plaster, or drywall, typically less than 1/16 inch wide, often caused by minor shrinkage or settlement.

Half Wall

A partial-height wall, typically extending 36-42 inches from the floor, used to define spaces visually without fully enclosing them; also called a pony wall.

Hammer

A hand tool with a weighted head on a handle, used to drive nails, break materials, or shape metal; available in many configurations for specific tasks.

Handrail

A continuous rail mounted on brackets alongside stairs or ramps, graspable for balance and support; required by code within specific height ranges.

Hanger
