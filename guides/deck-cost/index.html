Deck Cost Guide

2026 Pricing — Per Sq Ft by Material & Full Build

Overview

A new wood deck costs $30–60 per sq ft installed. A standard 16×12 ft (192 sq ft) deck runs $5,760–11,520. Composite decks cost $45–75 per sq ft. Prices include framing, decking, railings, and stairs — permits and site prep may add extra.

Factors Affecting Cost

Material Choice
Pressure-treated pine is the budget option; composite costs more upfront but eliminates annual staining.
Height
Ground-level decks are cheapest; elevated decks need footings, longer posts, and more structural engineering.
Railings & Stairs
Code-compliant railings add $15–30/linear ft. Stairs with landings add $500–1,500 depending on height.
Permits
Building permits cost $200–600. Most jurisdictions require permits for any attached deck or elevated structure.
Joist Spacing
Standard 16" OC spacing; composite typically needs 12" OC for diagonal patterns to prevent bounce.
Fasteners
Hidden fasteners add $2–4/sq ft but eliminate visible screw heads for a cleaner finish.

Tips to Save Money

  • Use 16" OC joist spacing for composite — 12" OC is only needed for diagonal decking; standard spacing saves 15% on framing lumber.
  • Double-check local codes and permits — building without a permit can result in fines and forced removal. Call your building department first.
  • Build in spring or fall — contractors have lower demand and may offer 10–15% off peak-season rates.
  • Use joist tape — a $50 roll of butyl tape over joist tops extends joist life by years by preventing water from sitting on the top edge.

Regional Price Variation

Construction costs vary significantly by region due to labor rates, material availability, and local demand. Here's how deck pricing compares across U.S. regions:

  • Northeast: 10-20% above national average — higher labor and stricter building codes
  • Southeast: 5-15% below national average — lower labor, pressure-treated pine is abundant
  • Midwest: Near national average — competitive market with moderate labor rates
  • West Coast: 15-30% above national average — highest lumber costs and labor rates
  • Southwest: 5-10% below national average — competitive market and year-round building

Always get 3+ local quotes. National averages are a starting point — your specific zip code, site conditions, and current material prices determine the final cost.

When to Schedule Your Project

Best time: SPRING or FALL — moderate temperatures make outdoor work comfortable. Contractors have lower demand and may offer 10-15% off peak-season rates.

Avoid: Winter (frozen ground for footings, snow/ice hazards), peak summer (contractors are busiest and charge premium). Wet spring delays footings and framing.

Many deck builders offer 10-15% discounts for projects booked 2-3 months in advance during their slow season. Ask about off-peak rates.

FAQ

What is the cheapest deck material?

Pressure-treated pine is cheapest at $25–45/sq ft. For a 16×12 deck that's about $5,500–7,700 finished. Cedar adds $10–15/sq ft for natural rot resistance and looks. DIY saves 40–50% on labor if you have framing experience.

How much does a 20×20 deck cost?

A 400 sq ft deck costs $10,000–24,000 depending on material. That's $25–60/sq ft with 400 sq ft being large enough for dining and lounge areas. Stairs, rails and built-in benches add to the total.
