Bump-Out Addition Cost Guide 2025

Cost Overview

A bump-out addition extends an existing room by 2 to 15 feet and costs $5,000 to $30,000, making it one of the most affordable ways to add square footage. A small cantilevered bump-out that doesn't require a foundation can be built for as little as $5,000, while a larger ground-level extension with a full foundation, roofing, and finished interior costs $20,000 to $30,000. Bump-outs are commonly used to enlarge kitchens for an island, expand bathrooms for a larger shower, or add a breakfast nook.

Factors That Affect Cost

  • Foundation requirements: A cantilevered bump-out supported by extending floor joists avoids foundation work entirely, saving $4,000-$10,000 over a ground-level bump-out. Cantilevers are typically limited to 2-3 feet without engineering.
  • Roof tie-in complexity: Matching the existing roofline requires tearing back shingles, extending roof sheathing, and tying in new flashing. A simple shed roof over the bump-out costs less than integrating into the main roof pitch.
  • Plumbing and electrical: If the bump-out extends a kitchen or bathroom, moving plumbing fixtures or adding outlets may require opening walls in the existing room, adding $2,000-$5,000.
  • Siding and exterior matching: Blending new siding, brick, or stone with the existing exterior can be straightforward or nearly impossible depending on material availability and weathering.

Money-Saving Tips

  • Keep the bump-out under 3 feet to use cantilevered construction; avoiding a foundation saves $5,000-$10,000 and eliminates the need for excavation and concrete work.
  • Extend the room along an exterior wall where the roofline is simple — gable ends are ideal because you can extend the peak without complex valley tie-ins.
  • Match siding as closely as possible rather than trying for a perfect match; painting the entire wall after construction creates a seamless look for less money than custom-matching materials.
  • Plan the bump-out during a siding or roofing replacement project so subcontractors are already on site and material orders can cover both the existing house and the new addition.
  • Use the existing HVAC system if the bump-out is small (under 50 sq ft); a room that size typically doesn't need its own supply and return if the door to the main room stays open.
