Garage Conversion Cost Guide 2025

Cost Overview

Converting a garage into a living space — whether for a rental unit, home office, or family room — typically costs $10,000 to $30,000 for a standard 2-car garage. This includes insulating walls and ceiling, raising the floor to match the main house, installing drywall, running electrical and HVAC, and adding egress windows. The final cost depends heavily on whether you need to pour new concrete to level the slab and whether plumbing rough-in is required for a bathroom or kitchenette.

Factors That Affect Cost

  • Slab condition and height: Garage slabs often sit 4-18 inches below the main house floor and may lack a vapor barrier. Raising the floor with sleepers and plywood costs more than pouring self-leveling compound.
  • Garage door removal: Removing the overhead door and framing in a wall with windows costs $1,500 to $4,000; keeping the door (for potential future reversion) saves this cost but limits design options.
  • Bathroom addition: Adding even a half bath requires cutting into the slab for drain lines, which can add $5,000-$10,000 to the total project.
  • Permit requirements: Converting a garage to living space almost always requires a building permit; some jurisdictions also require zoning variances or HOA approval.

Money-Saving Tips

  • Consider a partial conversion — keep one parking bay as storage and convert the other half; this cuts costs roughly in half while still providing usable living square footage.
  • Use a ductless mini-split for heating and cooling instead of extending existing ductwork; mini-splits are 30-50% cheaper to install and far more efficient for converted spaces.
  • Frame walls with 2x4 studs 24 inches on center rather than 16 inches where code allows; it reduces lumber costs by about 25% and increases cavity insulation value.
  • Tackle demolition, insulation, and painting yourself — these are the most DIY-friendly phases and can save $3,000-$5,000 in labor.
  • Install luxury vinyl plank flooring directly over a properly leveled slab instead of building up a plywood subfloor; it saves $1,500-$3,000 on a 400 sq ft space.
