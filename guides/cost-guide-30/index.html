Attic Conversion Cost Guide 2025

Cost Overview

Finishing an attic into a bedroom and bathroom costs $40,000 to $80,000 for a typical 300-500 square foot conversion. The major expenses are structural reinforcement of floor joists, installation of a code-compliant staircase, adding dormers for headroom and natural light, and running plumbing to the top floor. An attic conversion adds significant livable square footage and typically returns 60-75% of the investment at resale, making it one of the better ROI home additions.

Factors That Affect Cost

  • Existing roof structure: Truss-framed roofs are far more expensive to convert than stick-framed attics because trusses cannot simply be cut — the entire roof structure may need reframing, adding $10,000-$30,000.
  • Ceiling height: Building codes typically require at least 7 feet of headroom over 50% of the floor area. If your attic is too low, you may need to raise the roof or add dormers.
  • Staircase placement: A straight run of stairs takes roughly 3x10 feet of floor space on the level below. If no obvious location exists, a spiral staircase can save space but costs $3,000-$8,000 on its own.
  • HVAC extension: Extending existing ductwork to the attic may require a larger air handler; a ductless mini-split is often cheaper and more efficient for attic spaces.

Money-Saving Tips

  • Get a structural engineer's assessment before hiring a contractor — some attics are simply not convertible without prohibitive costs, and a $500 report saves you from a $5,000 design mistake.
  • Use skylights instead of dormers for natural light when code allows; a skylight costs $1,500-$3,000 installed versus $10,000-$20,000 for a dormer.
  • Place the bathroom directly above an existing bathroom below to share the plumbing stack; this can save $3,000-$7,000 in plumbing rough-in costs.
  • Install spray foam insulation at the roofline rather than insulating the attic floor; it creates a conditioned attic space and eliminates the need for separate ventilation baffles.
  • Use knee-wall storage areas for built-in drawers and shelving instead of trying to finish every inch of sloped ceiling space — you save on drywall and trim while gaining functional storage.
