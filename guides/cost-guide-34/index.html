Second Story Addition Cost Guide 2025

Cost Overview

Adding a second story to a single-story home is a major undertaking costing $100,000 to $300,000 for 500 to 1,500 square feet. The project involves structural reinforcement of the existing first-floor walls and foundation, framing new walls and roof, installing a staircase, and extending all mechanical systems. While expensive, a second-story addition typically doubles the home's square footage at $100-$200 per square foot — often cheaper per square foot than a ground-level addition because no new foundation is needed.

Factors That Affect Cost

  • Existing foundation capacity: The first-story walls and footings must support the added load. Older homes may require foundation underpinning or steel beam reinforcement, adding $10,000-$30,000.
  • Occupancy during construction: Living in the home during a second-story addition is extremely disruptive. Many homeowners budget $15,000-$30,000 for temporary housing during the 4-8 month construction period.
  • Roof removal and weather protection: Once the old roof comes off, the house must be protected from rain. Temporary weatherproofing and sequencing add cost and risk.
  • Staircase design: A straight staircase takes about 40-50 sq ft from each floor. A switchback or L-shaped stair costs more to build but uses space more efficiently.
  • MEP system sizing: The existing electrical panel, HVAC unit, and water heater may all need upsizing to serve the additional square footage, adding $5,000-$15,000.

Money-Saving Tips

  • Build a partial second story over the garage or a single wing rather than the entire footprint; a 500 sq ft addition over the garage costs $100,000-$150,000 versus $250,000+ for full coverage.
  • Choose simple rooflines — a gable roof over the new story costs less than complex hip roofs with multiple valleys and dormers.
  • Place bathrooms directly above existing bathrooms below to share plumbing stacks and minimize the distance water supply and drain lines must travel.
  • Install a high-efficiency mini-split system for the second story instead of upsizing the central HVAC; second stories naturally stay warmer, and a dedicated unit provides better comfort at lower cost.
  • Act as your own general contractor if you have construction experience; general contractor fees typically run 15-20% of the total project cost, which on a $200,000 project means $30,000-$40,000 in savings.
