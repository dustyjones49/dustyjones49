Overview

A wood privacy fence costs $15–30 per linear foot installed. A typical 150 ft backyard fence runs $2,250–4,500. Vinyl costs $20–40 per foot, chain link $10–20. Fencing is one of the most common home improvement projects with clear per-foot pricing across materials.

Factors Affecting Cost

Material
Wood is the most popular; vinyl costs more upfront but requires zero maintenance; chain link is cheapest.
Height
4 ft fences cost 20–30% less than 6 ft. Most privacy fences are 6 ft; pool code typically requires 4 ft minimum.
Terrain & Grading
Sloped lots require stepped or racked fencing, adding 15–25% to labor. Rocky soil slows post-hole digging.
Gate Count
A standard walk gate adds $200–500; a double-drive gate adds $500–1,200. More gates = more hardware and labor.
Post Setting Method
Concrete-set posts cost more but last longer. Gravel-set posts drain better in wet climates but may shift over time.
Region & Labor
Labor rates vary from $5–12/linear ft depending on your market. Dense urban areas pay the highest rates.

Tips to Save Money

  • Get at least 3 quotes — fence bids on the same job can differ by 50%. Always compare line-item breakdowns.
  • Measure accurately — use a measuring wheel or laser to get exact linear feet before calling for quotes.
  • Consider DIY if flat terrain — setting posts and attaching panels on level ground is achievable. Post-hole digging is the hardest part.
  • Call 811 before digging — utility line marking is free and legally required. Hitting a buried line costs thousands in repairs.
  • Buy materials off-season (winter) — lumber yards and fence suppliers often discount 10–20% in December–February.

Regional Price Variation

Construction costs vary significantly by region due to labor rates, material availability, and local demand. Here's how fence pricing compares across U.S. regions:

  • Northeast: 10-20% above national average — higher labor rates and rocky soil slow digging
  • Southeast: 5-15% below national average — lower labor costs, many fence companies
  • Midwest: Near national average — competitive market, abundant lumber
  • West Coast: 15-30% above national average — highest labor and land costs
  • Southwest: 5-10% below national average — competitive, year-round installation

Always get 3+ local quotes. National averages are a starting point — your specific zip code, site conditions, and current material prices determine the final cost.

When to Schedule Your Project

Best time: SPRING or FALL — ground is soft enough to dig but not frozen. Mild weather makes outdoor work comfortable.

Avoid: Winter in cold climates (frozen ground requires expensive equipment to dig), peak summer (dried-out soil is harder to dig, contractors charge premium).

Many fence companies offer 10-15% discounts for winter bookings in moderate climates. Buy materials December-February for 10-20% off at lumber yards.

FAQ

How much does 200 ft of fence cost?

Wood: $3,000–6,000. Vinyl: $4,000–8,000. Chain link: $2,000–4,000. Each gate adds $200–500. Demolition of an old fence adds $3–5/linear ft for removal and disposal.

What is the cheapest fence?

Chain link is cheapest at $10–20/linear ft. For privacy on a budget, use pressure-treated dog-eared pickets at $15–20/linear ft. Wire farm fencing with t-posts is even cheaper at $3–5/ft but offers no privacy or security.
