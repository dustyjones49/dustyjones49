Gravel Driveway Cost Guide

2026 Pricing — Materials, Delivery & Installation

Overview

A gravel driveway costs $1–3 per sq ft installed or $25–50 per ton for materials. A typical 12×50 ft driveway runs $600–1,800 total including base and delivery. Gravel remains the most affordable driveway option with straightforward DIY potential.

Factors Affecting Cost

Material Type
Crushed stone stays put better than round pea gravel; crusher run is cheapest and compacts well as a base layer.
Delivery Distance
Quarry proximity is key — delivery fees jump $2–5/mile beyond 15 miles. Local quarries save hundreds.
Depth
New driveways need 4–6 inches; resurfacing an existing drive needs 2–3 inches. Deeper = more tons.
Site Prep & Grading
Clearing vegetation, grading for drainage, and compacting the sub-base may add $200–800.
Compaction Costs
Renting a plate compactor costs ~$60/day; hiring a roller operator adds $100–200.
Landscape Fabric
Geotextile fabric ($0.50–1.00/sq ft) separates soil from stone, reducing future gravel loss — highly recommended.

Tips to Save Money

  • Buy in bulk by the ton, not by the bag — bagged gravel at home centers costs 3–5× more than bulk delivery.
  • Use crusher run as a base with clean stone on top — crusher run compacts tightly; top with angular stone that locks together.
  • Compact in 2–3 inch lifts — compacting thin layers prevents settling and rutting. Don't dump all at once.
  • Add geotextile fabric — a $200 roll of fabric prevents gravel from sinking into mud and saves a full resurface every 3 years.

Regional Price Variation

Construction costs vary significantly by region due to labor rates, material availability, and local demand. Here's how gravel driveway pricing compares across U.S. regions:

  • Northeast: 10-20% above national average — higher delivery costs and older homes with tight access
  • Southeast: 5-15% below national average — abundant quarries and lower labor
  • Midwest: Near national average — competitive market with many local quarries
  • West Coast: 15-30% above national average — highest delivery fees and labor rates
  • Southwest: 5-10% below national average — competitive and steady demand

Always get 3+ local quotes. National averages are a starting point — your specific zip code, site conditions, and current material prices determine the final cost.

When to Schedule Your Project

Best time: SPRING or FALL — dry ground prevents ruts and permits proper compaction. Cooler temps make spreading and raking easier.

Avoid: Wet seasons and winter (muddy conditions make grading impossible, trucks get stuck). Summer's fine but the heat makes the work harder.

Many quarries offer 5-10% discounts on bulk orders during late fall when demand drops. Ask about off-peak delivery rates.

FAQ

How much does a ton of gravel cover?

One ton covers about 80–100 sq ft at 3 inches deep, or 60–80 sq ft at 4 inches. That's roughly 10–12 linear feet of a 10 ft wide driveway at 4 inches. Use our gravel calculator for precise estimates.

Is gravel cheaper than asphalt?

Yes, gravel is 40–60% cheaper upfront. At $1–3/sq ft vs $4–7/sq ft for asphalt. But gravel needs annual regrading and a new layer every 3–5 years, while asphalt lasts 15–20 years with sealcoating. Long-term, asphalt may cost less per year.
