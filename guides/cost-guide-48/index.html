Generator Install Cost Guide 2025

Cost Overview

Installing a whole-house standby generator costs $5,000 to $15,000 including the generator unit, automatic transfer switch, concrete pad, gas line connection, and electrical work. A 20-22 kW air-cooled generator, which covers most 2,000-3,000 sq ft homes including central AC, costs $5,000-$7,000 for the unit plus $3,000-$5,000 for installation. Larger liquid-cooled units (30-48 kW) for larger homes or commercial applications run $10,000-$15,000 installed.

Factors That Affect Cost

  • Fuel type: Natural gas is the most common and convenient since it taps into the existing utility line. Propane requires a tank lease or purchase ($500-$2,000 for an underground 500-gallon tank). Diesel generators cost more but run longer between maintenance intervals.
  • Location and setbacks: Generators must be placed 5 feet from windows, doors, and combustible walls, and at least 18 inches from the house. Tight lot lines or HOA rules may require a corner of the property far from the gas meter and panel, increasing trenching costs.
  • Gas meter capacity: A 20 kW generator consumes 250,000-300,000 BTU/hour. Many residential gas meters are rated for 250,000-400,000 BTU total. If the generator plus furnace, water heater, and range exceed the meter rating, the utility must upgrade the meter ($500-$1,500).
  • Load management: To avoid oversizing the generator, load-shedding modules can temporarily shed large loads like electric dryers or EV chargers, allowing a smaller generator to serve a larger home.

Money-Saving Tips

  • Choose a generator that covers essential circuits rather than the entire house; a 14-16 kW unit with a load-shed transfer switch costs $3,000-$5,000 less installed than a 22 kW whole-house unit.
  • Place the generator as close to the gas meter and electrical panel as code allows; every 10 feet of trenching for gas and electrical lines costs $200-$400 in combined labor and materials.
  • Install a pre-cast concrete generator pad ($100-$200) instead of pouring a custom pad; pre-cast pads meet code, are perfectly level, and eliminate concrete curing delays.
  • Schedule installation in spring or early summer when HVAC contractors are less busy with emergency furnace and AC calls; off-peak labor rates can save 10-15%.
  • Join a generator maintenance plan through the installer rather than hiring ad-hoc service; annual service contracts cost $200-$400 per year but include priority scheduling during outages and prevent costly engine damage from neglected oil changes.
