Sunroom Addition Cost Guide 2025

Cost Overview

A sunroom addition costs $20,000 to $70,000, with prefabricated aluminum-framed kits on the lower end and custom stick-built rooms with high-performance glazing on the upper end. A typical 12x16 foot sunroom on a concrete slab runs $25,000 to $40,000 including electrical, flooring, and basic finishes. The cost per square foot ranges from $100 for basic three-season rooms to $300+ for four-season rooms with insulated glass, HVAC integration, and premium finishes.

Factors That Affect Cost

  • Glazing package: Single-pane glass is the cheapest but only suitable for 3-season use. Double-pane low-E argon-filled glass costs $40-$70 per square foot but enables year-round comfort.
  • Foundation type: A concrete slab is standard, but decks or pier foundations vary in cost; frost-depth footings are required in northern climates, adding $2,000-$5,000.
  • Roof design: A solid shingled roof matching the house costs more than an all-glass ceiling but provides better insulation, noise reduction, and protection from falling debris.
  • Electrical and HVAC: Ceiling fans, recessed lighting, and baseboard heaters are standard; extending central HVAC or adding a mini-split costs $3,000-$7,000 extra.

Money-Saving Tips

  • Build a 3-season room instead of a 4-season room if you live in a moderate climate; you'll save 30-50% on glazing costs and may avoid HVAC requirements altogether.
  • Choose a kit manufacturer that includes assembly instructions and video support; DIY assembly can save $5,000-$12,000 in labor for a standard prefab sunroom.
  • Orient the sunroom to face south or southeast to maximize passive solar gain during winter and reduce heating costs; this free design decision pays dividends every month.
  • Use operable windows and a ceiling fan instead of adding ducted HVAC; natural ventilation keeps a sunroom comfortable for three seasons at near-zero operating cost.
  • Pour the slab at the same time as any other concrete work on the property to reduce mobilization fees and get a better per-yard price from the concrete supplier.
