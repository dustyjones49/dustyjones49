Paver Patio Cost Guide

2026 Pricing — Concrete, Brick & Natural Stone Pavers

Overview

A paver patio costs $15–30 per sq ft installed including a 4–6 inch base, sand, and labor. A 12×12 ft (144 sq ft) patio runs $2,160–4,320. Pavers offer unmatched durability and repairability compared to poured concrete slabs.

Factors Affecting Cost

Paver Material
Concrete pavers are most affordable; brick offers classic warmth; natural stone (bluestone, travertine) is premium.
Base Depth
4–8 inches of compacted gravel sub-base is needed. Deeper bases in freeze-thaw climates push costs higher.
Pattern
Herringbone and circular patterns cost 20% more than running bond due to extra cuts and layout complexity.
Site Access
Difficult access (fences, narrow gates) requires wheelbarrowing materials, increasing labor by 10–20%.
Excavation & Grading
Removing sod and grading for drainage adds $2–4/sq ft. Existing concrete removal costs extra.
Edging Type
Plastic edging is standard; concrete or metal edging adds $3–6/linear ft but prevents shifting longer.

Tips to Save Money

  • Buy pavers in spring — suppliers stock the widest selection and may offer early-season discounts of 5–10%.
  • Rent a plate compactor ($60/day) — proper compaction of base and pavers is essential; don't skip this step.
  • DIY saves $5–8/sq ft — paver patios are labor-intensive but technically straightforward. Rent a sod cutter and compactor.
  • Order 10% extra for cuts — running short mid-job means color-lot mismatches. Return unopened pallets if unused.
  • Use polymeric sand in joints — it hardens when wet, blocking weeds and ants far better than regular sand.

Regional Price Variation

Construction costs vary significantly by region due to labor rates, material availability, and local demand. Here's how paver patio pricing compares across U.S. regions:

  • Northeast: 10-20% above national average — higher labor rates and deeper frost-depth base requirements
  • Southeast: 5-15% below national average — lower labor costs, simple base needs
  • Midwest: Near national average — competitive market, many paver suppliers
  • West Coast: 15-30% above national average — highest labor rates and material costs
  • Southwest: 5-10% below national average — year-round installation keeps demand steady

Always get 3+ local quotes. National averages are a starting point — your specific zip code, site conditions, and current material prices determine the final cost.

When to Schedule Your Project

Best time: SPRING or FALL — moderate temperatures keep you comfortable. Ground is workable, and material suppliers have full stock with early-season discounts.

Avoid: Winter (frozen ground can't be excavated), peak summer (extreme heat makes heavy labor a health risk, and compaction is harder). Rainy seasons delay base work by days.

Many hardscape contractors offer 10-15% discounts for projects booked in late fall or early spring before peak season. Ask about off-peak rates.

FAQ

Is a paver patio cheaper than concrete?

No, pavers cost more than poured concrete. Concrete is $4–8/sq ft while pavers are $15–30/sq ft installed. But pavers won't crack, are easy to repair (swap one out), and add more resale value. They're a long-term investment.

How long do paver patios last?

A properly installed paver patio on 6+ inches of compacted base lasts 25–50 years. Individual pavers may need re-leveling after 10–15 years. The base remains intact and pavers can be lifted and reset — unlike concrete which requires full replacement when cracked.
