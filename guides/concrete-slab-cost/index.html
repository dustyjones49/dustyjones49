Concrete Slab Cost Guide

2026 Pricing — Material, Labor & Installation Costs

Overview

The average concrete slab costs $4–8 per sq ft installed. A basic 4-inch patio runs $2,000–3,600 for a 20×20 ft area. Driveways poured at 6 inches cost $6–12 per sq ft. Final pricing depends on thickness, reinforcement, site conditions, and finish type.

Factors Affecting Cost

Thickness
A 4" slab is standard for patios; driveways need 6" to support vehicle weight.
Rebar vs. Mesh
Welded wire mesh is standard; rebar adds $1–2/sq ft but increases strength for heavy loads.
Site Prep & Grading
Clearing, excavation, and gravel base add $1–3/sq ft depending on soil conditions.
Accessibility
Tight spaces requiring a pump truck instead of a chute add $200–500 to the job.
Finish Type
Broom finish is standard; stamped concrete costs $3–5/sq ft extra.
Region & Labor Rates
Urban areas and cold climates cost 15–30% more due to higher wages and frost-depth requirements.

Tips to Save Money

  • Get 3+ quotes — bids on the same job can vary by 40% between contractors.
  • Pour in mild weather — spring and fall offer ideal curing temps; avoid extreme heat or freezing.
  • DIY form setting saves $1–2/sq ft — building and leveling wood forms is labor-intensive but beginner-friendly.
  • Order exact yardage — short-load charges apply if you order less than a full truck (typically under 5 yards).

Regional Price Variation

Construction costs vary significantly by region due to labor rates, material availability, and local demand. Here's how concrete slab pricing compares across U.S. regions:

  • Northeast: 10-20% above national average — higher labor rates and frost-depth requirements add cost
  • Southeast: 5-15% below national average — lower labor costs and minimal frost requirements
  • Midwest: Near national average — competitive market with moderate labor
  • West Coast: 15-30% above national average — highest labor rates in the country
  • Southwest: 5-10% below national average — competitive and steady demand

Always get 3+ local quotes. National averages are a starting point — your specific zip code, site conditions, and current material prices determine the final cost.

When to Schedule Your Project

Best time: SPRING or FALL — concrete cures best at 50-70°F. Moderate temperatures and lower contractor demand before peak summer season.

Avoid: Extreme heat (causes rapid drying and cracking), freezing temperatures (prevents proper curing). Summer pours require early morning starts and curing blankets.

Many contractors offer 5-15% discounts for projects booked 2-3 months in advance during their slow season. Ask about off-peak rates.

FAQ

How much is a 20×20 concrete slab?

$1,600–3,200 for a 4-inch patio, $2,400–4,800 for a 6-inch driveway. Stamped concrete adds $3–5/sq ft more. Always get local bids — prices vary significantly by region.

Can I pour concrete myself?

DIY saves labor ($2–5/sq ft) but you need a crew of 3–4 for a slab pour, plus a rental mixer or pump truck. Small pads under 100 sq ft are DIY-friendly. Order ready-mix delivery — don't hand-mix for anything over a few bags.

How long does concrete last?

A properly poured slab lasts 30–50 years with minimal maintenance. Avoid de-icing salts the first winter, seal every 3–5 years, and fill cracks immediately to prevent water damage.
