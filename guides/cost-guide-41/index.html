Pergola Cost Guide 2025

Cost Overview

A built pergola costs $1,500 to $8,000, with DIY wood pergola kits on the low end and custom aluminum louvered-roof systems on the high end. The national average for a professionally installed 10x10 wood pergola is $3,500 to $5,500. Pergolas provide partial shade and architectural definition to patios and decks without the full enclosure of a gazebo, making them one of the most popular backyard structures.

Factors That Affect Cost

  • Material: Pressure-treated pine costs the least but requires annual staining. Cedar is naturally rot-resistant and costs 25-40% more. Aluminum with a powder-coated finish is maintenance-free and the most expensive option.
  • Size: Most pergolas are 10x10 or 12x12 feet. Each additional foot in width or depth adds roughly $200-$400 in materials due to longer beams, rafters, and purlins.
  • Footing type: Post anchors bolted to an existing concrete patio are the simplest; digging and pouring concrete footings below frost depth adds $500-$1,500 depending on soil conditions.
  • Roof style: Traditional open-rafter with spaced purlins is standard. A louvered roof that opens and closes costs 3-4x more but provides adjustable shade and rain protection.
  • Permits: Some municipalities require permits for pergolas over 120 sq ft or attached to the house. Permit fees range from $100 to $500.

Money-Saving Tips

  • Build a pergola kit yourself — most kits include pre-cut and pre-drilled lumber with detailed instructions that two people can assemble in a weekend, saving $1,500-$2,500 in labor.
  • Anchor posts to an existing concrete patio using galvanized post bases instead of pouring new footings; the hardware costs $15-$30 per post and eliminates excavation and concrete work.
  • Use pressure-treated pine and stain it to match cedar — a $50 gallon of quality exterior stain makes $1,000 worth of pine look like $2,500 worth of cedar.
  • Attach one side of the pergola to the house as a lean-to design; using the house wall as a ledger board eliminates two posts and footings, reducing costs by $500-$1,000.
  • Add shade cloth or a retractable canopy instead of a louvered roof system; shade cloth costs $50-$150 and provides 80-90% UV blockage for a fraction of the cost of mechanical louvers.
