Dormer Addition Cost Guide 2025

Cost Overview

Adding a dormer to an existing roof costs $20,000 to $50,000 when fully finished with drywall, flooring, electrical, and a window. A dormer cuts into the existing roofline to create additional headroom and floor space, making it a popular solution for attic conversions and second-story expansions. Simple shed dormers cost less than gable or hip-roof dormers due to their straightforward framing and roofing geometry.

Factors That Affect Cost

  • Roof pitch and complexity: Steeper roofs require taller dormer walls and more complex flashing; intricate roof tie-ins with valleys and crickets add framing and waterproofing labor.
  • Dormer style: A shed dormer provides the most usable interior space per dollar spent. Gable and hip dormers look more traditional but cost 20-30% more for the same square footage.
  • Interior finish level: A shell-only dormer costs about half as much as a fully finished one, but you'll need to complete the interior later. Finishing includes insulation, drywall, flooring, trim, paint, and electrical.
  • Existing roof condition: If the roof needs replacement anyway, bundling the dormer with a re-roof saves on mobilization and tear-off costs. If the roof is new, cutting into it adds complexity.

Money-Saving Tips

  • Choose a shed dormer over a gable dormer — the simple roof plane requires less framing, fewer materials, and less complex flashing, saving 15-25% on total cost.
  • Frame the dormer as a shell and finish the interior yourself over several weekends; interior finishing (drywall, flooring, paint) accounts for 40% of the total dormer cost.
  • Time the dormer addition with a roof replacement so the roofing crew can shingle the dormer and main roof together, eliminating a separate mobilization charge.
  • Use standard-size windows that fit the dormer opening without custom ordering; a stock window costs $200-$500 versus $800-$2,000 for a custom-sized unit.
  • Keep the dormer width within standard lumber spans to avoid needing engineered beams or steel I-joists, which add $1,000-$3,000 in material costs.
