French Drain Cost Guide 2025

Cost Overview

French drain installation costs $10 to $25 per linear foot for a professionally installed system, or $4 to $8 per linear foot for materials alone if you DIY. A typical 50-foot French drain along a foundation or through a soggy yard runs $500 to $1,250 installed. French drains solve groundwater problems by collecting water in a perforated pipe surrounded by gravel and a filter fabric sleeve, then channeling it to a safe discharge point like a dry well, storm drain, or daylight outlet on a slope.

Factors That Affect Cost

  • Trench depth and width: A standard exterior French drain is 18-24 inches deep and 6-12 inches wide. Deeper trenches cost more in labor and require more gravel fill. Interior basement drains that sit below the slab are far more expensive due to concrete cutting.
  • Soil type: Sandy soil is easy to trench. Rocky soil or heavy clay that requires pickaxe work or a trencher rental adds $2-$5 per linear foot. Soil with large tree roots may require root sawing or re-routing.
  • Discharge point: A gravity-fed daylight outlet (pipe exits on a downslope) is cheapest. If the yard is flat, a dry well or sump pump basin is needed to collect and disperse water, adding $800-$2,000.
  • Access and landscaping: Drains installed in finished landscapes require sod removal, plant relocation, and restoration. Drains under existing hardscape (patios, walkways) require demolition and replacement.

Money-Saving Tips

  • Dig the trench yourself with a rented trencher ($100-$200/day) — the trench is the most labor-intensive part, and DIY trenching on a 50-foot drain saves $400-$800 in labor.
  • Buy gravel in bulk from a landscape supply yard rather than bagged gravel from a home center; bulk gravel costs $20-$40 per cubic yard versus $4-$6 per 0.5 cubic foot bag.
  • Use 4-inch corrugated HDPE pipe with a pre-installed filter sock instead of rigid PVC; it costs $0.50-$0.80 per linear foot, is flexible enough to curve around obstacles, and the sock eliminates wrapping fabric by hand.
  • Discharge to a downhill daylight point whenever possible — a gravity outlet costs nothing beyond the pipe run, while a sump pump and dry well system costs $1,000-$2,500 extra.
  • Combine the French drain installation with other landscaping projects that require equipment rental; a mini-excavator or trencher can dig the drain trench, planting holes, and a patio base in one rental period.
