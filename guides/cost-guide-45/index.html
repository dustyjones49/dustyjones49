Driveway Gate Cost Guide 2025

Cost Overview

An automated driveway gate costs $1,000 to $8,000 installed, covering the gate itself, support posts, the motor/operator, and electrical connections. A basic single swing gate made of steel or aluminum with a chain-drive opener starts around $1,500-$2,500, while an ornate wrought-iron double gate with brick pillars, underground gate operator, and intercom system can exceed $10,000. The automation hardware and electrical work alone typically account for $800-$3,000 of the total.

Factors That Affect Cost

  • Gate style: Swing gates are simpler and cheaper but need level ground and clearance to open. Sliding gates work on sloped driveways and in tight spaces but cost 30-50% more due to the track and roller hardware.
  • Material: Galvanized steel with powder coating is the most affordable and durable. Aluminum costs 20-30% more but won't rust. Wrought iron is the most expensive and requires periodic rust treatment.
  • Pillar construction: Mounting the gate to existing wood posts costs the least. Brick or stone pillars with concrete cores cost $1,500-$4,000 per pair but provide a grand entrance aesthetic and hold up to heavy gates.
  • Operator type: Above-ground arm operators cost $500-$1,200. Underground operators concealed in the ground cost $1,500-$3,500 but preserve the clean look of the gate.
  • Accessories: Keypad entry adds $200-$500, intercom/video systems $500-$2,000, and vehicle detection loops embedded in the driveway add $800-$1,500 for automatic opening upon exit.

Money-Saving Tips

  • Choose a single swing gate instead of a double gate; a 12-foot single gate costs $1,000-$2,000 less than a dual-gate system because you only need one operator, half the hinges, and simpler control wiring.
  • Install a solar-powered gate opener if running electrical conduit to the gate location is expensive; solar kits cost $300-$800 and eliminate the need to trench 120V power across the driveway.
  • Use galvanized steel tubing with an aluminum gate frame instead of solid wrought iron; it looks nearly identical once powder-coated but costs 40-60% less and weighs less, reducing operator strain.
  • Mount the gate to heavy-duty steel posts set in concrete rather than building brick pillars; steel posts cost $200-$400 per pair versus $2,000-$4,000 for masonry pillars.
  • Install a simple wireless keypad entry instead of a full intercom/video system; a $100 keypad provides secure access and most guests carry cell phones for communication anyway.
