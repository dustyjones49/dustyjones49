Barndominium Cost Guide 2025

Cost Overview

Barndominiums — metal building shells converted into living spaces — cost $50 to $150 per square foot depending on finish level. A basic 2,000 sq ft barndominium shell (steel frame, metal siding, roof, and concrete slab) runs $40,000-$60,000, while a fully finished interior with custom layout, kitchen, bathrooms, and HVAC brings the total to $150,000-$300,000. Barndominiums are popular in rural areas where land is affordable and zoning allows mixed-use residential and shop space under one roof.

Factors That Affect Cost

  • Shell vs. interior split: Many owners finish only part of the building as living space and leave the rest as a garage or workshop, which significantly reduces the cost per square foot of the finished area.
  • Insulation method: Spray foam provides the best air seal and R-value for metal buildings but costs $2-$5 per sq ft. Fiberglass batts are cheaper ($0.50-$1.50/sq ft) but perform poorly against metal condensation.
  • Roof style: A standard gable roof with metal panels is the most affordable option; monitor-style roofs with a raised center aisle add natural light but increase steel and framing costs by 20-30%.
  • Foundation: A monolithic slab with thickened edges is standard; if the building includes a workshop with heavy equipment, the slab must be thicker (5-6 inches) with reinforced rebar, adding $3,000-$8,000.

Money-Saving Tips

  • Buy the metal building shell directly from a manufacturer and hire a local crew for erection — you'll save 15-25% over going through a general contractor who marks up the shell cost.
  • Finish the interior in phases: complete one bedroom and bathroom first to move in, then finish additional rooms as cash flow allows. This avoids construction loan interest on the full project.
  • Use polished concrete flooring in the living area instead of hardwood or tile; it's durable, costs $3-$5 per sq ft, and complements the industrial aesthetic of a barndominium.
  • Design an open floor plan with minimal interior load-bearing walls — steel clear-span frames can eliminate interior columns and walls, reducing framing and drywall costs while maximizing flexibility.
  • Install a wood-burning stove or pellet stove for primary heat if you have access to firewood; heating a large open space with a stove costs a fraction of running electric or propane heat.
