Asphalt Driveway Cost Guide

2026 Pricing — Hot Mix Asphalt Paving & Sealcoating

Overview

Asphalt paving costs $4–7 per sq ft for a new driveway including 2–3 inches of hot mix on a crushed stone base. A typical 24×50 ft driveway (1,200 sq ft) runs $4,800–8,400. Asphalt is the most popular driveway material in the U.S. for its balance of cost, durability, and cold-weather performance.

Factors Affecting Cost

Thickness
2–3 inches for residential driveways. Commercial and heavy-use driveways may need 4–6 inches.
Site Prep & Base Work
A proper crushed stone base (4–8 inches) is critical. Poor prep leads to cracking and settling within 2 years.
Accessibility for Trucks
Narrow streets or low overhead obstacles may require smaller trucks or hand-laying, increasing costs.
Regional Asphalt Prices
Asphalt is oil-based; prices fluctuate with petroleum markets. Urban areas pay 10–20% more.
Old Driveway Removal
Removing existing concrete or asphalt costs $1–2/sq ft additional. Factor this in for replacements.
Season
Paving in fall often yields lower bids since pavers fill gaps between big summer commercial jobs.

Tips to Save Money

  • Wait 6–12 months before first sealcoat — new asphalt needs time to cure; sealing too early traps oils and softens the surface.
  • Reseal every 3–5 years — a $200 DIY sealcoat prevents $2,000+ in crack repairs and extends driveway life by 5–10 years.
  • Fix cracks immediately — water entering cracks freezes and expands, turning a $10 crack repair into a $500 pothole.
  • Get multiple quotes in fall — pavers are less busy after peak summer season and may offer 10–15% discounts.

Regional Price Variation

Construction costs vary significantly by region due to labor rates, material availability, and local demand. Here's how asphalt driveway pricing compares across U.S. regions:

  • Northeast: 10-20% above national average — oil-based asphalt prices higher, strict frost-depth codes
  • Southeast: 5-15% below national average — lower labor costs and longer paving season
  • Midwest: Near national average — competitive market, asphalt plants plentiful
  • West Coast: 15-30% above national average — highest labor and environmental compliance costs
  • Southwest: 5-10% below national average — year-round paving keeps demand steady

Always get 3+ local quotes. National averages are a starting point — your specific zip code, site conditions, and current material prices determine the final cost.

When to Schedule Your Project

Best time: SUMMER or EARLY FALL — hot mix asphalt needs 275-300°F at laydown. Warm weather keeps the mix workable long enough for proper compaction.

Avoid: Winter and cold months (asphalt cools too fast to compact properly). Early spring paving is possible but requires thinner lifts and may not bond as well to the base.

Many paving contractors offer 10-15% discounts for fall projects booked to fill gaps between big summer commercial jobs. Ask about off-peak rates.

FAQ

How long does an asphalt driveway last?

With proper maintenance, 15–20 years. Sealcoat every 3–5 years and fill cracks annually to prevent water penetration which causes potholes and heaving in freeze-thaw cycles. Neglected asphalt can fail in under 10 years.

Asphalt vs. concrete for driveway?

Asphalt is 30–40% cheaper upfront ($4–7 vs $6–12/sq ft) and easier to repair. Concrete lasts longer (30–50 years) and looks better but costs more and can't be resurfaced. In cold climates, asphalt handles freeze-thaw cycles better and snow melts faster on the dark surface.
