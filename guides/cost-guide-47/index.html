Solar Panel Cost Guide 2025

Cost Overview

A residential solar panel system costs $15,000 to $30,000 before incentives for a typical 6-12 kW system, averaging $2.50-$3.50 per watt installed. After the 30% federal solar investment tax credit (ITC), net cost drops to $10,500-$21,000. Most homeowners break even on their solar investment in 6-10 years through electricity bill savings, after which the system produces free power for the remaining 15-20 years of its warrantied life. State and utility incentives can further reduce the payback period.

Factors That Affect Cost

  • Panel type: Monocrystalline panels offer the highest efficiency (20-23%) and cost $0.80-$1.20 per watt. Polycrystalline panels cost 10-20% less but are slightly less efficient. Premium panels like SunPower or REC cost more but come with better degradation warranties.
  • Inverter technology: String inverters are cheapest ($1,000-$2,000) but the whole string is limited by the weakest panel. Microinverters ($150-$250 per panel) optimize each panel independently and are better for shaded or multi-orientation roofs.
  • Roof condition and type: Asphalt shingle roofs are easiest to mount on. Tile, slate, or metal roofs require specialized mounting hardware that adds $500-$2,000. If the roof needs replacement within 5 years, do it before installing solar.
  • Battery backup: Adding battery storage for outage protection or time-of-use arbitrage adds $8,000-$15,000 but qualifies for the same 30% federal tax credit.

Money-Saving Tips

  • Get at least 3 quotes from local installers — pricing for the same size system can vary by $5,000 or more between companies due to different panel brands, overhead structures, and sales commissions.
  • Use EnergySage or a similar solar marketplace to compare quotes anonymously; installers bidding against each other on a platform typically offer prices 10-20% lower than direct-sales quotes.
  • Size the system based on your actual 12-month electricity usage in kilowatt-hours, not the installer's rule of thumb. Most utilities cap net metering credits at 100-120% of usage, so oversizing wastes money.
  • Finance with a solar loan rather than a lease or PPA; you capture the 30% tax credit, own the system, and build home equity instead of sharing savings with a third-party owner.
  • Install during late winter or early spring when installer demand is lower; many companies offer 5-10% discounts to fill their installation calendar during the slow season.
