EV Charger Install Cost Guide 2025

Cost Overview

Installing a Level 2 EV charger at home costs $500 to $2,500, with the charger unit itself accounting for $300-$700 and the electrical installation making up the remainder. A straightforward installation where the electrical panel is in or near the garage and has spare capacity can be done for $500-$800. Costs climb when the panel needs upgrading, the charger location is far from the panel, or trenching is required to reach a detached garage or driveway.

Factors That Affect Cost

  • Distance from panel: 6/3 or 4/3 copper wire for a 50-amp circuit costs $3-$6 per foot. A charger 75 feet from the panel adds $225-$450 in wire alone before labor.
  • Panel capacity: A 200-amp panel with available slots handles a 50-amp EV circuit easily. Older 100-amp panels may need a load calculation; if the panel is maxed out, a costly upgrade or load management device is needed.
  • Installation type: Hardwired chargers require a disconnect and permit inspection. NEMA 14-50 plug-in installations are simpler and let you take the charger when you move, but code now requires GFCI breakers for outlets that can cause nuisance tripping.
  • Conduit and wall penetration: Running conduit along an exposed garage wall is cheapest. Running wire through finished walls, attics, or underground conduit adds $500-$1,500 in labor and materials.

Money-Saving Tips

  • Install a NEMA 14-50 outlet instead of hardwiring the charger; the installation cost is similar but you can plug in the charger yourself, avoid a hardwiring inspection, and take the unit with you when moving.
  • Choose a 40-amp charger on a 50-amp circuit rather than a 48-amp charger on a 60-amp circuit; the 40-amp unit uses less expensive wire and breakers and still charges most EVs from empty to full in 6-8 hours overnight.
  • Install the charger on a wall directly adjacent to the electrical panel if possible; a 3-foot wire run costs under $50 in materials versus $500+ for a cross-garage run, and charging cable length (typically 18-25 feet) provides plenty of reach.
  • Check for utility rebates before buying a charger; many power companies offer $250-$500 rebates on qualifying charger purchases, and some cover part of the installation cost too.
  • Use a load management device or smart EVSE that monitors total household draw instead of upgrading a full panel; these devices cost $300-$600 versus $2,000-$3,500 for a panel replacement.
